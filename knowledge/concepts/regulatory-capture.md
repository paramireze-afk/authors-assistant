# Regulatory Capture

**Regulatory capture** is the master organizing concept of [[Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]]'s entire body of work, first developed in his environmental-litigation career and later exported to public health, pharmaceuticals, and food policy.

## Definition (as Kennedy uses the term)

A condition in which a government agency, created to police an industry in the public interest, comes instead to serve that industry's interests — through financial dependence, staffing overlap, or political pressure — while retaining the appearance of independent oversight.

## Mechanisms Kennedy cites

- **Industry-funded review budgets:** e.g. [[PDUFA]] (Prescription Drug User Fee Act) fees, which fund a large share of FDA's drug-review operations directly from the companies whose products are under review.
- **The [[Revolving Door]]:** agency officials moving into industry roles (and vice versa), creating incentive alignment across a career rather than within a single administration.
- **Media dependence:** pharmaceutical direct-to-consumer advertising revenue as a structural disincentive for broadcast media to scrutinize drug safety.
- **Philanthropic funding structures:** large foundation grants (e.g., the [[Gates Foundation]]) to public agencies and research institutions, which Kennedy argues shape research and policy priorities toward patentable interventions.
- **Political appointment pressure:** industry-aligned political appointees shaping agency leadership and rulemaking priorities.

## Where the concept appears across his work

- ***[[Crimes Against Nature]]*** (2004) — EPA capture by energy and industrial interests under the Bush administration.
- **Thimerosal advocacy** (2005–2015) — CDC and FDA vaccine-safety oversight, alleged to be shaped by pharmaceutical-industry influence.
- ***[[The Real Anthony Fauci]]*** (2021) — NIH/NIAID prioritization of patentable interventions over cheaper public-health measures.
- **ACIP restructuring** (2025, as HHS Secretary) — Kennedy's own stated rationale for removing all 17 sitting ACIP members was to "eliminate conflicts of interest" and end what he characterized as agency capture by pharmaceutical and public-health orthodoxy.
- **MAHA Report** (2025) — extends the framework to USDA dietary guidance and food-industry influence.

## Critical perspective

Mainstream public-health and regulatory scholars generally acknowledge regulatory capture as a real phenomenon in some contexts (it is a well-established concept in economics and political science, associated with theorists like George Stigler), but dispute Kennedy's specific applications — particularly regarding vaccine safety, where they argue the weight of independent, non-industry-funded epidemiological evidence (much of it international and government-independent) contradicts his conclusions despite any capture dynamics that may exist elsewhere in the system.
