# Grand Strategy

*Scope note: This is a source-derived concept note, built only from transcript-derived reports already documented in this knowledge base — specifically the "Who and Why" interview reference (Mario Nefal) and the "Suez, 'Pinocchio,' and Grand Strategy Reference" (April 19, 2026 interview). No new transcripts, examples, or outside material were introduced. Where the two reports emphasize different aspects, those differences are organized separately below rather than merged into a single reconciled account.*

---

## What Grand Strategy Means According to Yon

Across both documented sources, Yon presents "grand strategy" as the highest of three explicit analytical levels he uses to interpret geopolitical events. He does not offer a single formal definition in either source; instead, the concept is defined by contrast — by what it is not (tactics, strategy) and by analogy (see below).

## The Three-Level Distinction (Mario Nefal Interview)

This source contains Yon's most explicit statement of the hierarchy:

1. **Tactics** — immediate, individual actions and events (his example: a specific strike or missile).
2. **Strategy** — operational-level reasoning connecting immediate actions to intermediate objectives.
3. **Grand strategy** — the top level, which Yon states most analysts do not engage with or "don't even seem to realize... exists."

He illustrates the distinction using a meteorological analogy: a meteorologist cannot predict every individual lightning strike, but can predict "here comes a typhoon" and describe its general "cone of probability." Tactics and strategy, in this framing, are analogous to individual lightning strikes; grand strategy is analogous to the typhoon itself.

**Applied example (direct statement):** when the interview's host offered a tactical explanation for a Saudi strike on Karbala (Shia militias striking Saudi energy infrastructure in coordination with Houthi efforts to lift a Yemen blockade, provoking Saudi retaliation), Yon responded that this explanation was not wrong but was operating "at the barroom fight" level — analogous to explaining a fight by tracing who threw the first punch, rather than asking why the people were in the bar at midnight in the first place.

## The Riot Analogy (Suez/Pinocchio Interview)

This source adds a second, distinct analogy not present in the Mario Nefal interview: Yon states that focusing only on a single flashpoint (in this case, the Strait of Hormuz or Iran specifically) is "like focusing on your toes when you're in the middle of a huge riot" — missing the cause of the broader event by fixating on a narrow, immediate detail.

*Organizational note: the two sources use two different illustrative analogies (typhoon/lightning in the Mario Nefal interview; riot/toes in the Suez/Pinocchio interview) to make a similar underlying point — that grand strategy explains a broader pattern that tactical-level focus misses. Neither source explicitly connects these two analogies to each other; they are presented here side by side rather than merged into one composite analogy, since Yon himself does not merge them.*

---

## Why Yon Believes Most Analysts Focus at the Wrong Level

Both sources support this point, with different emphasis:

- **Mario Nefal interview:** Yon states plainly that most analysts operate at the tactics/strategy level and "don't even seem to realize there's another scale above strategy." He states most governments also do not pay attention to grand strategy.
- **Suez/Pinocchio interview:** Yon frames grand-strategic understanding as available only to a very small group — describing it as clear to "that 1% of 1% of 1%" — implying most analysts and most of the public are structurally unable to access this level of understanding, not merely choosing not to.

*Organizational note: the Mario Nefal interview frames this as a matter of most analysts not recognizing the level exists; the Suez/Pinocchio interview frames it in more exclusionary terms (a very small group who can see it, implicitly excluding most others). Both sources agree analysts generally miss this level; they differ in whether the barrier is framed as an oversight or a more fundamental limitation. This difference is noted rather than resolved.*

## How Grand Strategy Influences Prediction

- **Mario Nefal interview:** the meteorologist analogy directly ties grand strategy to predictive claims — Yon states this level of analysis is what allows him to predict broad patterns (e.g., "the daring gap will be opened up," famines) even without predicting every specific tactical detail. He states explicitly that operating at this level makes some of his correct predictions "feel like cheating."
- **Suez/Pinocchio interview:** grand strategy is connected to a specific predictive claim — that the "real" targets of the current conflict are Israel and the United States, not Iran — which Yon presents as a conclusion only visible from the grand-strategic level, acknowledging it will sound implausible ("kooky") from a tactical or strategic vantage point.

## Relationship to Geography, Logistics, Routes and Resources

**Mario Nefal interview:** grand strategy is not explicitly tied to a specific geography lesson in this source; the connection is more methodological (the analytical level itself) than geographic.

**Suez/Pinocchio interview:** this source ties grand strategy more directly to geography and resources — the claim that Israel and the US are the "real" targets is presented alongside Yon's broader "routes and resources" framework (documented separately in this knowledge base), and the named grand-strategy collaborator, **George McMillan**, is described as someone Yon works with specifically using physical maps ("we just lay out the big maps and talk for hours and days") during extended sessions, including trips to Panama. This suggests, within this source, that grand-strategic analysis is conducted partly through direct geographic/cartographic study, though this connection is not stated as a formal methodological principle by Yon — it is described through the anecdote about how he and McMillan work together.

## Relationship to Migration, Famine, Food Production

**Mario Nefal interview only:** this source connects grand strategy directly to Yon's famine and migration predictions. He states that grand strategy is "where you start predicting that the dairy [sic, Darién] gap will be opened up. You'll end up with famines," directly citing his human-osmotic-pressure/migration framework (documented separately in this knowledge base) as an output of grand-strategic analysis specifically, as distinct from tactical or strategic-level analysis.

**Suez/Pinocchio interview:** does not explicitly connect grand strategy to migration or famine in the documented material; this source's grand-strategy discussion is focused instead on the Israel/US targeting claim and the McMillan collaboration.

## Relationship to Infrastructure, Chokepoints, Energy

Neither documented source explicitly walks through a direct statement connecting "grand strategy" as a term to infrastructure or chokepoints specifically, though both sources discuss chokepoints and infrastructure extensively in adjacent, non-grand-strategy-labeled portions of the same interviews (documented in the Routes and Resources / Strategic Chokepoints material elsewhere in this knowledge base). **This is a gap**: the documented material does not contain a direct statement from Yon explicitly defining chokepoint or infrastructure analysis as an example or output of "grand strategy" specifically, as he does explicitly for famine/migration in the Mario Nefal interview.

# Core Concept

As documented across these two sources, "Grand Strategy" is the top tier of a three-level analytical hierarchy Yon uses to interpret geopolitical events, sitting above tactics (individual actions/events) and strategy (operational reasoning connecting actions to intermediate goals). He argues most analysts, and most governments, operate only at the lower two levels, and illustrates the distinction with two separate analogies across the two sources: a meteorologist predicting a typhoon's general path rather than individual lightning strikes (Mario Nefal interview), and focusing on "your toes" rather than the cause of a riot you're standing in the middle of (Suez/Pinocchio interview). Grand strategy is presented as the level at which Yon's broader predictive claims — famine, migration, and (in the second source) the claim that Israel and the United States are the true targets of the current conflict rather than Iran — become visible, in contrast to tactical-level explanations he considers accurate but insufficient. The Mario Nefal interview more directly ties grand strategy to his famine/migration predictions specifically; the Suez/Pinocchio interview more directly ties it to geographic/cartographic method (via his collaboration with George McMillan) and to a specific claim about the conflict's true targets. Neither source fully explains how one distinguishes valid grand-strategic insight from an unfalsifiable pattern imposed on events after the fact — this is not addressed directly in either source and is flagged as an open question below.

# Hierarchy

Only the tiers explicitly supported by the documented material are included. The Mario Nefal interview is the source for this specific structure:

```
Grand Strategy
      ↓
   Strategy
      ↓
   Tactics
```

*Note: the documented sources do not support a five-tier model (e.g., including a separate "Operations" tier or an explicit "Individual Events" tier below tactics). Only the three tiers above are directly stated by Yon in the Mario Nefal interview. "Individual events" (e.g., a specific strike) are described as examples of the tactics tier, not as a separate tier beneath it.*

# Characteristics

Based solely on the two documented sources, Grand Strategy as Yon uses the term is characterized by:

- Sitting above both "tactics" and "strategy" in an explicit three-tier hierarchy (Mario Nefal interview).
- Being explained through analogy rather than formal definition in both sources (typhoon/lightning; riot/toes).
- Being described as largely unrecognized or inaccessible to most analysts and governments, though the two sources differ on whether this is framed as an oversight (Mario Nefal interview) or a more exclusionary limitation restricted to a very small group (Suez/Pinocchio interview).
- Producing broader, pattern-level predictions (famine, migration, or — in the second source — the claim about Israel/US as true targets) rather than tactical-level predictions.
- Associated, in the Suez/Pinocchio interview specifically, with a named collaborator (George McMillan) and a specific method involving extended, in-person map-based discussion.
- Not explicitly and directly connected, in either source, to chokepoints, infrastructure, demographics, energy, or information warfare as the term "grand strategy" specifically — even though these topics appear extensively elsewhere in the same interviews.

# Open Questions

1. How does Yon distinguish valid grand-strategic insight from a pattern imposed on events after the fact? Neither documented source addresses this directly, and it is arguably the most important open question for evaluating the concept's usefulness as an analytical tool.
2. Is grand strategy formally connected, in Yon's own words, to chokepoints, infrastructure, energy, demographics, or information warfare — topics that appear extensively in both sources but are never directly and explicitly labeled by Yon as "grand strategy"-level concerns?
3. Does Yon reconcile the two different framings of why most people miss the grand-strategic level (general oversight, per the Mario Nefal interview, vs. a more exclusionary "1% of 1% of 1%" framing, per the Suez/Pinocchio interview) anywhere in his broader output?
4. Does Yon apply a consistent method for grand-strategic analysis beyond the map-based collaboration with George McMillan described in one source, or is the method itself only partially documented?
5. Do the two distinct analogies (typhoon/lightning; riot/toes) ever appear together or get explicitly connected by Yon, or are they independent illustrations he uses in different contexts?
6. What, specifically, does Yon mean by describing grand-strategic understanding as available to "1% of 1% of 1%" — is this a claim about innate ability, specialized training, access to information, or something else? The documented material does not clarify this.
