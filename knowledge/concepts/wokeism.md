# Wokeism

## Summary

"Wokeism" is a broad and politically contested term commonly used to describe a collection of modern political, cultural, and institutional ideas centered around identity, social justice, equity, and systemic oppression.

The meaning of the term varies depending on who is using it. Supporters often view these ideas as efforts to address historical discrimination and improve inclusion. Critics argue that wokeism has evolved into an ideological framework that prioritizes group identity over individual merit, encourages ideological conformity, and reshapes institutions around political incentives.

Because the term has no universally accepted definition, discussions should focus on specific policies, organizations, or beliefs rather than relying solely on the label itself.

## Historical Context

Although many ideas associated with wokeism have earlier intellectual roots, the term became widely used during the late 2010s and early 2020s as corporations, universities, governments, and media organizations increasingly adopted language and policies emphasizing diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI).

## Common Characteristics

### Identity Politics

Greater emphasis is placed on group identity such as race, ethnicity, gender, or sexual orientation when analyzing social and political issues.

Common terminology includes:

- privilege
- oppression
- intersectionality
- lived experience
- allyship

### Equity

A distinction is often made between:

**Equality**

- Equal rules
- Equal opportunity

**Equity**

- Adjusting outcomes or opportunities to reduce disparities between groups.

Critics argue that equity often requires unequal treatment in pursuit of proportional outcomes.

### Institutional Adoption

Ideas commonly associated with wokeism have increasingly appeared within:

- corporations
- universities
- media organizations
- government agencies
- nonprofit organizations

Examples include:

- DEI offices
- diversity training
- ESG investing
- preferred pronoun policies
- demographic hiring goals
- inclusive language guidelines

### Language

Common phrases associated with woke discourse include:

- systemic racism
- unconscious bias
- cultural appropriation
- anti-racism
- inclusion
- privilege
- intersectionality
- marginalized communities

## Vivek Ramaswamy's Perspective

### Core Thesis

In *Woke, Inc.*, Vivek Ramaswamy argues that many corporations have adopted progressive social activism not primarily from moral conviction, but because it serves their institutional interests.

He argues that corporations increasingly use social activism to improve public legitimacy while avoiding scrutiny of their economic power.

### Occupy Wall Street Thesis

One of Vivek's central historical arguments is that the rise of corporate activism followed the legitimacy crisis created by the 2008 financial collapse and the Occupy Wall Street movement.

His argument can be summarized as:

```text
2008 Financial Crisis
        ↓
Loss of public trust in corporations
        ↓
Occupy Wall Street
(class-based criticism)
        ↓
Corporations adopt stakeholder capitalism
        ↓
Corporate embrace of progressive social causes
        ↓
Public attention shifts toward identity politics
rather than corporate power
```

According to Vivek, this shift allowed corporations to present themselves as moral leaders while leaving many underlying business practices unchanged.

This represents Vivek Ramaswamy's interpretation of these historical events rather than an established historical consensus.

### Stakeholder Capitalism

Vivek argues that modern corporations increasingly see themselves as political and cultural institutions rather than organizations focused primarily on serving customers and shareholders.

He contends that this expansion of corporate influence creates incentives for companies to participate in political and cultural debates.

## Arguments Commonly Made in Support

Supporters argue that policies associated with wokeism:

- address historical discrimination
- improve workplace inclusion
- reduce unconscious bias
- encourage empathy toward marginalized groups
- make institutions more representative

## Common Criticisms

Critics argue that wokeism:

- prioritizes identity over individual merit
- discourages open debate
- encourages self-censorship
- weakens merit-based institutions
- promotes ideological conformity
- shifts attention away from economic inequality
- expands political influence into non-political institutions
