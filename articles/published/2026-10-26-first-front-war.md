# The First Front of War

*Netanyahu calls information the eighth front. I think it’s the first.*

A while back, Netanyahu said Israel was fighting a seven-front war, and that there was an eighth front: “the battle for truth.” Other times he’s called it the battle for hearts and minds in the West, especially in the United States, and he’s called social media “the most important weapon” in that fight. I don’t think that’s right at all. I think social media, the internet, and information itself are the first front of any war.

I find the Charlie Kirk assassination story absolutely fascinating. It reminds me of a kid who has chocolate covered all over his face, and his parents are asking who ate the chocolate, and the kid just shrugs his shoulders in feigned perplexity. To me, this is the dumbest assassination and cover-up in the history of cover-ups. You’ve got magic necks, magic bullets, and a teleporting Tyler Robinson.

Anyways, I’ve been thinking about what billions of dollars would buy you when it comes to informational warfare. I think it would buy bots and operatives of sorts to help promote the people you like and attack the ones you don’t. I think it can also buy you the ability to enshroud, or capture, genuine populist movements. I think that’s what happened to Charlie Kirk, and I think it’s happening right now to Candace Owens. The reasons are very hard to explain, but essentially, I think Baron Coleman, a shark of a lawyer, is pretending to be a truth teller and a friend. I strongly suspect his attacks on other researchers who stumbled onto the Fort Huachuca story are him tipping his hand.

Anyways, I see YouTube influencers very differently now. I think people like Bret Weinstein and Heather Heying can’t be permitted a large audience, because they could very easily equip others to see through the various methods used for controlling narratives. Same with Jordan Peterson. I remember how huge the demand was back in the 2010s when he was teaching about cautionary tales and evolutionary thinking when it came to biblical stories and Disney movies. I think there’s also a huge demand for debates like the ones Bret had with Jonathan Pageau. Those kinds of dialogues would absolutely shatter a lot of misconceptions. They would make non-religious people curious about biblical studies, and they would make religious people more accepting of non-religious people as a result of that curiosity. They might also get religious people more interested in evolution itself, which I find is being used as a boogeyman among my religious family.
