# Powers of Three and Two — A Reader's Guide

## 1. The big idea in one paragraph

The author's starting point is simple. If you multiply threes together and
multiply twos together, the two families of numbers almost meet, but never quite.
**243** (five threes multiplied) falls just short of **256** (eight twos
multiplied). The gap is **13**.

The author does not treat that gap as an accident. To him, the shortfall is the
most important thing in the picture. It is a *residual*, a leftover that keeps the
system open, and he reads it as the same structure he has been building across his
other writing: a **Master Table** of twelve **stations**. His central claim is that
**arithmetic (numbers) and geometry (shapes) are not two separate languages but one
continuous order**. The figure of nested circles is his way of making that visible.

## 3. The arithmetic, checked

Every number fact in the post is correct:

| Statement | Correct? |
|---|---|
| 9 × 27 = 243 | ✓ |
| 243 = 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 3⁵ | ✓ |
| 243 + 13 = 256 | ✓ |
| 256 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 2⁸ | ✓ |
| 108 × 8 = 864 | ✓ |
| 13 = 1 + 12 | ✓ |

**Technical note.** The author is pointing at a real property: a power of 3 can
never equal a power of 2. Every power of 3 is odd and every power of 2 above 1 is
even. So whenever the two families come close, there will *always* be a leftover.
That guaranteed leftover is what he builds his philosophy on.

## 5. The twelve stations

This is the core of the post. The author takes his twelve-station row and reads
the figure through each station. Every station has a **name**, a **physical unit or
symbol**, and a **role in the figure**.

The units are not used as ordinary physics measurements. The author attaches each
one to a stage of the system, a bit like giving each stage a signature.

| # | Name | Tag | Unit / symbol | Role in the figure | Plain reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | Home | Light lock | σ m/s² (acceleration) | The residual that keeps the 2/3 ratio open | The starting point and source of the whole packet |
| II | Void | Volume skin | m³ (volume) | The left-hand circle that receives the 13 | Empty space waiting to be filled: "unactualised volume" |
| III | Limit | Celerity | m/s (speed) | The contraction toward the unit | The speed left over once the ratio is used up |
| IV | Strength | Wall | N/m² = ∞ (pressure, set to infinity) | The shortfall that cannot buckle | An unbreakable floor; area itself as infinitely strong |
| V | 1st Moment | First stroke | N·m·s² (force × length × time²) | The moment that lifts 243 to 256 | The first push: force, length and duration together |
| VI | 2nd Moment | Mass seat | +m · N | The nested shells | Brackets that hold and receive the exponent |
| VII | 3rd Moment | Give | –m · s² | The overlaps that keep the proportion intact | The flex in the system: the "inverse magic of the give" |
| VIII | 4th Moment | Arena clamp | m⁴ | The centroid that holds the nested sets | The clamp that holds everything after the pulse |
| IX | Division | Angle | rad · 22/7 · α | The 2/3 ratio written as a fraction | Tilt and turning; division expressed as an angle |
| X | Fragment | Constructs | [kg][m][s][h] | The successive diameters | Human measuring units as constructs, not nature itself |
| XI | Bar | Transcendental joint | r^(m) · π_E | The meeting of the powers | What's left once everything else is seated; "no centroid", no centre of balance |
| XII | Face | Light emit | π_N · ContSec | The unit that contracts to 1 | The observable surface: "Face of XI" |

### The shape of the sequence

Read top to bottom, the stations tell a story:

1. **I–III: Origin and motion.** A source (Home) opens a space (Void), and motion
   runs until it hits a speed limit (Limit).
2. **IV: The floor.** Something that cannot fail (Strength).
3. **V–VIII: The four moments.** A push, a holding, a give, a clamp. This is the
   mechanical heart, where 243 becomes 256.
4. **IX–X: Measurement.** Angles, fractions and units appear (Division, Fragment).
5. **XI–XII: The join and the surface.** The two families of numbers meet (Bar),
   and the result is what we see (Face).

Note the progression in the units. Stations V to VIII use what the author calls
**moments**, borrowing the engineering idea of first, second, third and fourth
moments, each one a step more complex.
