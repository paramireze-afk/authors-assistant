# Michael Yon: US-China Trade War Interview Reference (Financial Rebellion)

*Primary source documentation, from an episode of "Financial Rebellion" (Children's Health Defense TV), hosted by "Lou" and a co-host referred to in the transcript as "Carolyn" (likely Catherine Austin Fitts, based on Yon's own reference to staying with her in the Netherlands in prior documented material — the name may be a transcription artifact). This entry documents Yon's portion of the interview only. The pre-interview financial news segment (pesticide liability litigation, AI data center buildout, the GENIUS Act, DTCC tokenization, and the US-China trade war/sanctions overview) is independent host commentary, not Yon's material, and is summarized only briefly as context below. A separate, unrelated segment on COVID-19 treatment (Dr. Tom Rogers) appears appended to the same video/transcript and is excluded entirely as it has no connection to Yon or this interview.*

## Context: Pre-Interview Segment (Not Yon's Material)

Briefly, for context only: the hosts discuss a pesticide manufacturer liability shield narrowly defeated in Congress (with a pending related Supreme Court case), aggressive AI data center buildout (citing a Utah project projected to eventually use 3-4x the electricity of the entire state), the GENIUS Act and pending Clarity Act (US programmable-money/stablecoin regulation), the DTCC's move toward tokenizing traditional securities (noting the Tel Aviv Stock Exchange's inclusion among 50 initial participating firms), and a detailed overview of the US-China trade war and the mechanics of sanctions (economic/trade, financial, "smart"/targeted, arms, diplomatic, sports, and secondary sanctions), including the observation that tariffs are increasingly functioning as a sanctions mechanism. This section frames the trade-war discussion that follows with Yon but is independently reported, not part of Yon's worldview documentation.

## Kjiro House and Ninomiya Sontoku

Yon describes searching for land near Mount Fuji to build what he calls "Kjiro House" (a planned personal project/retreat). He explains the name references **Ninomiya Sontoku** (Kinjirō), a historical 1800s Japanese figure commemorated in statues nationwide (typically depicted carrying sticks while reading a book), who is credited with foreseeing an oncoming famine and preparing his followers, who survived while others starved. Yon frames his own famine warnings (which he states he has been issuing "for six years") as directly analogous to this figure's foresight, and Kjiro House as a practical response to his own predicted famine.

## Strait of Hormuz and Strait of Malacca

Yon restates his prior Hormuz/Malacca claims with additional specificity:

- He states that in 2025 he was "writing in very clear words" that he believed the **US and Israel** (stated together, not solely "the Zionist" as in some prior documented material) would close the Strait of Hormuz and blame Iran, and states this "sounded crazy in 2025" but reflects what he now believes has occurred.
- He ranks the Strait of Malacca as potentially **"maybe seven times more important"** than the Panama Canal, which he separately describes as very important based on extensive personal time spent there (including, he states, being permitted by canal staff to help operate a lock and visit the control tower).
- He describes recent (as of this transcript) US-Indonesia military cooperation (overflight rights) as relevant context, framing Indonesia (the world's largest Muslim-majority country, per Yon) as maintaining good relations with the US despite poor relations with Israel, and states Indonesia's closest international relationship is specifically with Japan, which he attributes to Japan's role in ending Dutch colonial rule there after WWII (citing the renaming of Batavia to Jakarta as evidence).

**Yon's stated reasoning method, explicitly addressed:** when the host asks directly "who is 'they'" regarding chokepoint closures, Yon responds using an explicit hunting analogy: "I understand what this crocodile is doing... it's not always deductive. It's inductive based on what their goals are." This is Yon's own characterization of his reasoning process — inference from perceived goals to predicted action, rather than deduction from direct evidence.

## Named Actors: "The Zionist Structure" and "The Epstein Class"

Yon states one of "their" goals is global depopulation, attributing this to what he calls **"the Zionist structure, the Epstein class"** — explicitly naming this as an "oligarchal constellation" of actors. This is the first instance in this reference set where Yon directly links "the Epstein class" as a named component alongside "the Zionist" actor label used throughout prior documented material (a previous entry recorded a passing "Epstein Island" reference without this more formal "Epstein class" phrasing).

He connects this to previously documented mechanisms (glyphosate/agricultural chemicals, energy/fertilizer disruption) and adds a new element: **digital infrastructure and "digital prison."** He credits Catherine Austin Fitts (the co-host) with originating this specific concept, and frames AI data centers as serving a dual purpose within his framework — population reduction plus the construction of digital control infrastructure for the reduced/surviving population, which he describes as working in tandem with physical imprisonment/control ("digital prison... along with physical prison").

He explicitly distinguishes other named actors from this goal: he states China and Russia do not want to be depopulated themselves, consistent with the "frenemies" framing documented in prior entries.

## China's Countermeasures: Panama Canal and Shipping Sanctions

The host (not Yon) reports that China recently announced it would not comply with new US sanctions related to Iran, and separately banned companies that use the Panama Canal (naming MSC and Maersk specifically) from Chinese ports. Yon responds to and builds on this reporting (rather than originating the underlying facts, which are presented by the host as current news):

- He states Maersk has, per information he says he received roughly six months prior, been attempting unsuccessfully to acquire the two key ports flanking the Panama Canal (Balboa on the Pacific side, Cristóbal on the Caribbean side).
- He states he and his wife visited Denmark specifically because of the Danish Straits and Maersk's significance there, and predicts **someone will eventually target Denmark and the Danish Straits**, and possibly Maersk directly, framing this in terms of historically targeting an adversary's merchant fleet and ports directly ("old school... you take their whole fleet").
- **Observation on Walmart:** the host notes Maersk's stock fell significantly over the prior month while Walmart's stock rose over the same period despite Walmart's heavy reliance on Chinese imports, and both speakers interpret this divergence as evidence of "a meta control higher than the US-China war" — i.e., that some companies are being deliberately shielded from the trade conflict's effects while others are not. This is presented as inference from stock performance data, not from a named source confirming intentional protection.

## Global Famine: Geographic Scope

Yon extends the famine-mechanism framework (documented in prior entries) to a broader named list of vulnerable countries: **Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Myanmar, and Thailand**, in addition to China. He provides a specific, sourced-to-his-own-biography case study for Thailand:

- He states he met personally with former Thai PM Abhisit Vejjajiva approximately four to five years prior to this recording (consistent with his account in the previously documented Suez/Pinocchio interview) and warned him that Thailand produces only about 8% of its nitrogenous fertilizer domestically (importing roughly 92%, per Yon), making its major rice export industry highly vulnerable to chokepoint disruption (Hormuz, Turkish Straits) affecting fertilizer imports.
- He argues famine "spreads like fire" as displaced populations move toward food sources, predicting famine-driven migration from South Asia into northern Thailand (naming Mae Sai, Chiang Rai).
- He distinguishes this predicted famine from historical "quick" famines (citing the 1707 Mount Fuji/Hōei eruption and resulting Edo-area famine as a historical example of a short, severe, naturally-caused famine) — arguing the famine he predicts is different in kind: a deliberate, slow-onset, structural reduction in food-production *capacity* itself, which he argues is more severe and durable than a short-term supply shock.

- He raises independent suspicion about the **Fukushima Daiichi disaster**, stating he flew directly from a remote deployment location on the Afghanistan-Iran border to Japan in response to the event, citing this personal action as evidence of how significant he judged the event to be at the time, though the transcript does not specify what alternative explanation (if any) he holds for Fukushima's cause beyond general suspicion ("there's something strange about the whole Fukushima thing").
- He frames all three (Nord Stream, the Bavarian nuclear plant, and by implication Fukushima) as part of a broader pattern of deliberately removing energy-generation capacity — nuclear, coal, and gas — to reduce population.

## The Iran Conflict as a Proxy War Against China's Cost of Capital

This section is significantly co-developed with the host, who proposes a specific economic mechanism which Yon partially endorses:

- **Host's proposed mechanism:** the primary US objective regarding Iran is to raise China's energy costs, connecting this to a claim that Chinese 10-year sovereign bond yields fell below US yields in 2023 (indicating a lower Chinese cost of capital), which — combined with China's investment in nuclear and other lower-cost energy sources — gives China a structural cost advantage in a broader economic competition with the US.
- **Yon's response:** he states he "partially" agrees, adding his own historical framing — citing the long-standing British strategic goal (which he traces back at least a couple of centuries, referencing geopolitical theorist Halford Mackinder) of keeping Russia and Germany diplomatically/economically separated, and framing Nord Stream's destruction as consistent with this much older strategic pattern.
- **Yon's own stated conclusion, going further than the host's economic framing:** he states plainly that he believes the actual, ultimate goal is to **"reduce the number of Chinese on planet Earth"** — extending the depopulation framework (previously documented primarily in terms of general global population reduction) into an explicit claim about a targeted reduction specifically of the Chinese population, attributed to the same actor structure discussed throughout this reference set.

## Migration, Ireland, and the "Plunder Model"

- Yon restates the "human osmotic pressure" concept (documented in prior entries) and predicts continued migration via the Darién Gap, direct ship routes up the Mississippi River, and states he does not expect the US government to prevent this, predicting active facilitation instead.
- He extends this to Ireland specifically, framing organized depopulation/displacement efforts there as driven by wealthy individuals ("trillionaires") interested in Irish land/real estate independent of the current Irish population, without citing a specific named individual or source for this claim.
- **The "Plunder Model," introduced primarily by the host (Catherine Austin Fitts) and endorsed by Yon:** a proposed economic pattern in which public/taxpayer funds cover costs (e.g., land clearance in conflict zones) while private actors capture the resulting resource value (oil, gas, water, land), then taxpayer funds are used again to rebuild infrastructure under continued private ownership — described as a public-private partnership structured so the public bears costs while private actors retain the upside. Yon agrees and extends this with the "company town" historical analogy, describing it as a long-standing, common model ("tens of thousands" of historical company towns) rather than a novel mechanism.

## The "Dog King" Analogy

Yon recounts a historical practice (which he states occurred in various forms including in Scandinavia and parts of Africa) in which a conquering power installs a literal animal (a dog, fitted with a crown and an "interpreter") as the nominal ruler of a defeated population, arguing this served two purposes: demoralizing the conquered population (by demonstrating their ruler's insignificance) and emboldening external rivals. He connects this to the (historically disputed, which Yon himself notes — "every time I really dig down... it kind of fades to gray") story of Caligula appointing his horse as a Roman senator, and applies both as an analogy for what he views as the installation of compromised or illegitimate figures into contemporary elected office. He states he first published this analogy in 2022.

*Classification: Yon explicitly flags the Caligula's-horse story as historically uncertain in his own telling — a notable instance of him applying source skepticism to a claim used to support his own broader argument.*
