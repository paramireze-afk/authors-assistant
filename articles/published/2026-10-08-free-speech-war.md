# Did You Know There Was a Major Battle Taking Place at Cornell Leading Up to the Cornell 7 Headlines?

*College campuses are the front lines of the free speech battle*

You may not have heard, but there was recently an anti-war event at Cornell, one critics called anti-Israel, that took place days before the Cornell 7 story hit all the major news headlines. I didn't know about it either. I only found out because I was watching the DarkHorse Podcast, Bret and Heather were discussing the Cornell 7, and I kept asking myself the same question: why is this national news?

The answer I found had less to do with what happened in a fraternity house two years ago and more to do with what was happening at Cornell right now.

## The Event You Didn't Hear About

In mid-September, a student coalition called Students Not Bombs invited Hasan Piker, one of the most popular and controversial political streamers in the country, to speak on campus. The coalition is pushing Cornell to divest from Israel and its wars. Meanwhile, a former student known as Jane Doe filed a lawsuit over an alleged gang rape at a fraternity house back in 2024, the case now known as the Cornell 7. Look at the timing:

- **September 15:** Students Not Bombs announces Piker's visit. Tickets sell out within hours.
- **September 16:** Jane Doe files a civil lawsuit.
- **September 24:** Piker speaks at Sage Chapel.
- **September 28:** The DA reopens the case, citing the "community outrage stemming from the incomplete news and social media coverage."
- **October 2:** Governor Kathy Hochul appoints Attorney General Letitia James as special prosecutor.

## What Happened in 2024

Here's the short version. In October 2024, Jane Doe had about ten drinks and walked to the Chi Phi fraternity house to see a friend. She says two brothers pressured her into taking ketamine, and she agreed to a threesome with her friend and another member. Then one of them posted about her in the fraternity group chat, more men came into the room, and by her account it went on for hours. She says two of the newcomers also had sex with her without her consent, and others kissed, groped or watched.

She reported it to campus police. The DA declined to file charges, saying her signed statement described everything as voluntary, even though a transcript later surfaced where she told police she was raped. His office never actually investigated. Cornell ran its own disciplinary process and expelled two of the men.

Obviously, I have no clue what actually happened that night, and horrible decisions were likely made by a lot of people. Violence sucks, and never do anything against someone's consent. So let's assume every word of her account is true. That still doesn't explain why a case that sat quietly for almost two years suddenly had page after page of national coverage, all with the same catchy name.

To be fair, a 101-page lawsuit doesn't get written overnight, so I'm not claiming it was filed because of Piker. What I'm asking is why it exploded when it did.

## What They Were Fighting Over

Students Not Bombs isn't protesting war in the abstract. It has a specific target: the Technion, Israel's top engineering school and a major pipeline into the Israeli defense industry.

This isn't some stock Cornell could quietly sell off. Cornell Tech, the university's flagship campus on Roosevelt Island in New York City, was built as a joint project with the Technion, and the two schools run an institute together right in the middle of it. "Sever ties with the Technion" was printed right on the Piker flyer.

And the movement had momentum. In March, Cornell's Student Assembly voted 17 to 5 to end the partnership. President Michael Kotlikoff shot it down, calling the resolution's political bias "deeply disturbing." Even Zohran Mamdani said during his mayoral campaign that he'd "reassess" the partnership before walking it back. Then Piker filled Sage Chapel. Within days, nobody was talking about the Technion. Everybody was talking about the Cornell 7.

## What I Think the Actual Story Is

It seems to me that the Cornell 7 story has been weaponized to turn the public's attention into emotional outrage over a two-year-old case, one the DA didn't think was worth investigating until it went viral. So to me, the actual story is: who is the Goliath here? On one side, you have students at a college campus wanting to express their opposition to the foreign wars taking place, among other things. On the other, you have large swaths of the media apparatus seemingly moving in lockstep, all running with the same punchy tagline: "Cornell 7."

## Are We Falling for It Again?

A lot of people are missing the underlying battle taking place at Cornell. By narrowly focusing public outrage on a contested two-year-old case, the media is giving powerful interests leverage to pressure the university into silencing dissenting voices on Israel's actions in the Middle East.

And we've seen this before. In December 2023, the presidents of Harvard, Penn and MIT were hauled in front of Congress and dressed down over how they handled campus protests about Gaza. The presidents of Penn and Harvard resigned shortly after, and Columbia's president followed in 2024. This September alone, Macklemore was kicked off Ed Sheeran's tour for saying "Free Palestine," and Ms. Rachel, a children's YouTuber already named a finalist for "Antisemite of the Year" for speaking up about kids in Gaza, got piled on again for matching his donation to Palestinian children.
