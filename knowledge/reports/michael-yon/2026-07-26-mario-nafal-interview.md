## Track Record Claims: Nord Stream and Related Predictions

Yon restates and adds detail to his claimed prior predictions:

- **Nord Stream:** Yon states he predicted Nord Stream's destruction "so many times in advance," and that he and his wife Msako traveled to Germany specifically anticipating this, visiting the BASF plant in Ludwigshafen twice (he describes BASF as a likely target of "destroying Nord Stream," without fully explaining the connection between the plant and the pipeline in this transcript). He states he purchased an iPad solely to keep open a website tracking cubic meters per second flowing through Nord Stream, monitored for months, and that the readings dropped to zero at the time of the pipeline's destruction. He states they were at the BASF plant 14 days before the pipeline was destroyed.
- **Groningen gas field:** Yon states he also predicted the closure of the Groningen gas field (which he describes as the largest gas field in Europe) in advance.
- **Screwworm return via Panama:** referenced again as a prior successful prediction, consistent with earlier documented material.
- **Darién Gap flood:** Yon states he predicted a "massive flood" of migration opening up through the Darién Gap.

**Yon's stated method for these predictions:** he attributes his success to use of a **CARVER matrix** — a target-selection framework he identifies as used in military sabotage/targeting analysis, standing for Criticality, Accessibility, Recuperability, Vulnerability, Effect, and Recognizability. He states he applied this matrix himself to identify Nord Stream and Groningen as top targets, describing this as similar to a process he associates with CIA and Special Forces targeting doctrine.

**Yon's stated evidentiary basis, in his own words:** "We know what they're doing. They say it. They tell people what they're doing" — i.e., he frames these predictions as derived from stated, public intentions rather than hidden or inferred information.

## Grand Strategy vs. Tactics/Strategy: Yon's Analytical Layers

Yon presents an explicit three-tier framework for levels of analysis:

1. **Tactics** — the immediate, individual actions and events (e.g., a specific strike, a specific missile).
2. **Strategy** — the operational-level reasoning connecting immediate actions to intermediate objectives.
3. **Grand strategy** — the top level, which Yon argues most analysts do not engage with, and which he claims produces clearer, simpler patterns than the lower two levels, using the analogy that a meteorologist cannot predict every individual lightning strike but can predict "here comes a typhoon" and its general cone of probability.

Yon explicitly frames his own analytical contribution as operating primarily at this grand-strategy level, and frames a real-time exchange with the host (who offered a tactical explanation for the Saudi strike on Karbala — Shia militias striking Saudi energy infrastructure in coordination with Houthi efforts to lift a blockade on Sanaa, provoking Saudi retaliation) as correct in its own terms but operating "at the barroom fight" level rather than the level Yon says he works at.

## Wars as Routes, Resources, and Ideology

Yon restates a claimed general rule: "every war that you've ever looked at comes down to three things... routes, resources, and ideology," and further specifies that ideology functions as a tool for managing "human resources," which he identifies as "the number one resource on planet Earth" — the resource from which access to other resources follows.

He extends this into two named categories of ongoing conflict:
- **"Standing wars"** — recurring, geography-driven conflicts over fixed strategic locations (his example: Panama, due to its role as a land bridge and canal), which he argues will recur regardless of which specific groups or "players" are involved, using the analogy that different bears could be swapped in and out of the same territory and would still fight over the same rivers and caves.
- **"Process wars"** — ideologically or organizationally driven, ongoing efforts (religious conversion efforts by different faiths, corporate expansion efforts historically carried out by chartered companies like the British East India Company, Dutch East India Company, and the Virginia Company of London) that are not always waged as conventional war but sometimes are.

**Biographical detail offered:** Yon states his own family arrived in America in 1609 with the Virginia Company of London, was shipwrecked on Bermuda en route (which he connects to Bermuda's continued relationship with the UK), and reached Jamestown in 1610.

## The "Synthetic 1922 States" Framing

Yon refers to the Gulf states as "synthetic 1922 states," attributing their creation to British and French colonial administrators (naming Percy Cox and referencing the Sykes-Picot agreement) as artificial constructions serving what he calls "globalism," and by extension, routes and resources. He references T.E. Lawrence and *Seven Pillars of Wisdom* in this context, without making a specific evidentiary claim beyond citing the book as part of his personal library and research.

He extends this same logic explicitly to Israel, stating "why does Israel even exist... why do any of those countries [Gulf states] exist" as parallel questions, both answered (in his framework) by colonial-era great-power decisions made in service of routes and resources — presented as historical interpretation rather than a claim specific to the present-day depopulation framework.

## Direct Exchange: "Who and Why?"

This is the most significant new material in this transcript. The host directly and repeatedly presses Yon to identify who is responsible for the depopulation framework and why, across several exchanges. This section documents that exchange closely, as it is a rare instance of Yon being pressed on evidentiary grounding in real time.

**Host's question, restated by Yon accurately:** why would anyone plan mass depopulation — "that's a very evil thing for anyone to plan" — and who has the power to do it?

**Yon's response, first pass:** he states he cannot "read their minds," and reframes the question by describing his own background studying cults, including more than three years spent researching cults after 2001 (stated separately from his later war-reporting career), during which he states he infiltrated and lived with a cult.

**The Aghori comparison:** Yon describes Aghori, which he characterizes as an extreme sect associated with Hinduism, as holding a belief that since a god or gods made everything, everything is by nature perfect, and that this belief is used to justify unrestricted behavior including (per Yon's description) cannibalism and necrophilia, in pursuit of a stated goal of "release" — specifically, in Yon's words, freedom from the birth-and-death cycle and "power over life and death."

**Yon's direct claim:** he states "the hardcore Zionist" are "extremely similar" to this cult in structure — operating, per Yon, with "no rules whatsoever" and pursuing the same stated end-goal of "release"/power over life and death — and cites "Epstein Island" as an example of this pattern, without further elaboration of what connection he is drawing in this transcript.

**Yon's explicit epistemic position, when pressed further:** when the host asks directly whether specific named individuals (he raises Trump and Netanyahu) are the responsible decision-makers, Yon states no — he calls them "condoms," meaning fungible, expendable, replaceable tools rather than the actual controlling actors ("he's a leaf on a tree, fungible and expendable... just like all the other actors in this"). When the host asks who is "behind the scenes" — a company, billionaires, policymakers, or something else — **Yon states directly: "actual names, I don't know because they come and go."** He argues the relevant frame is not identifying specific individuals or organizations, but recognizing an "old war" that predates any living person's lifetime, in which specific "players" and organizational vehicles (he cites the historical decline of the Dutch and British East India Companies as an example) change over time while the underlying structure persists.

**Yon's response to the host's alternative framing:** the host explicitly proposes a middle position — that some events may be part of a larger plan while others (his example: Trump's Iran strike decision) may simply be mistakes or misjudgments, drawing a comparison to ordinary human error. Yon's response: he agrees "accidents happen all the time," and states that his framework does not require every specific detail to be planned, using an analogy to building the Panama Canal — an operation with a clear overall "commander's intent" (dig the canal, build the locks) that does not require pre-planning every individual action within it. He maintains that the overall trajectory (which he again asserts is "nakedly obvious") is intentional even if individual tactical events are not each specifically choreographed.

**Yon's suggested method for evaluating the framework:** he recommends the audience "experiment with the idea for a couple of months" as a way of testing the paradigm, and separately describes his own background studying physics for roughly ten years as a young person, which he states shaped his approach to what he calls "paradigms," "thinking hygiene," and "information hygiene" — being careful with information sources and logical process. He states a specific heuristic tied to this: treating one's own feeling of *surprise* at an event (distinct from a startle reaction, e.g., "why would they attack the people in Karbala, that doesn't make any sense") as a signal that one's current paradigm may be incomplete or incorrect, rather than as evidence the event itself was random or irrational.

*Classification note: the "who and why" section above is documented in unusual detail relative to other sections because it is the clearest direct evidence in this transcript of Yon's own stated epistemic position — he explicitly disclaims knowledge of specific responsible individuals or organizations while maintaining confidence in the overall directional framework. This is Yon's own statement, not an outside inference.*

## Core Analytical Framework

This transcript adds two important elements to the framework documented previously: an explicit three-tier analytical model (tactics/strategy/grand strategy), and Yon's own direct answer to "who and why."

1. **Yon explicitly locates his own analysis at the "grand strategy" level**, arguing this level of analysis is simpler and more predictive than tactical or strategic-level analysis, and treats tactical-level explanations (even ones he agrees are internally coherent, as with the host's Karbala explanation) as correct but insufficient for understanding the larger pattern he asserts is occurring.
2. **When directly pressed on responsible parties, Yon explicitly disclaims naming specific individuals or organizations** ("actual names, I don't know because they come and go"), while maintaining that named public figures (Trump, Netanyahu) are not decision-makers but replaceable instruments of a longer-running, structurally continuous process that he states predates any living person. This is a self-stated epistemic position, not an inference on the part of this document.
3. **The CARVER matrix and stated public announcements ("they say what they're up to") are offered as Yon's primary method for generating specific predictions** (Nord Stream, Groningen), distinct from the broader, less evidenced claim about the overarching depopulation objective and its responsible actor.
4. **Yon frames feelings of analytical surprise as diagnostic of an incomplete paradigm**, treating "why would they do that, it doesn't make sense" as evidence the observer's framework needs revision rather than as a possible sign that the event is, in fact, poorly explained or contingent.

One note on this transcript specifically, offered descriptively and consistent with how prior entries in this reference set have handled comparable material: Yon's direct comparison of "hardcore Zionists" to an extreme cult associated with cannibalism and necrophilia, framed around a stated goal of "power over life and death" and supported by a reference to "Epstein Island," is a more explicit and more clearly demonizing characterization of a named religious/ethnic-identified group than in the previously documented transcripts, which more often used "the Zionist" as an unelaborated actor label. It is documented above as Yon's own stated comparison, not expanded upon or evaluated further here.
