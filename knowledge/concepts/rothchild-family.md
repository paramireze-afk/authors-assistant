# The Rothschild Family

## Why This Page Exists

The Rothschild family is the single most recurring named entity in this knowledge base without a page of its own — it shows up as supporting material in *[[The Bank of England]]* (the Napoleon/Waterloo episode), *[[The Rothschild Formula]]* (the family's namesake role), *[[The First and Second Banks of the United States]]* (Griffin's claim of Rothschild control over the First Bank), and *[[The Bolshevik Revolution]]* (via the Rhodes–Milner–Round Table lineage). This page consolidates all of that material into one place and adds substantial content — the George Peabody/J.P. Morgan "concealed agent" narrative, August Belmont's Civil War dealings, and the 1896 gold-rescue episode — that has not appeared in any prior document in this knowledge base.


## Origins: Mayer Amschel Rothschild

Griffin traces the dynasty to **Mayer Amschel Rothschild** (born **Mayer Amschel Bauer**), son of a Frankfurt goldsmith. Per Griffin, the family fortune began when Mayer adopted the practice of **fractional-reserve banking** more aggressively and effectively than his competitors, and he changed the family name from Bauer to Rothschild — after the "red shield" (*rot schild*) that had marked the family's house — around the time he began sending his sons to establish branches across Europe. Griffin quotes biographer Frederic Morton's assessment that the Rothschild dynasty "conquered the world more thoroughly, more cunningly, and much more lastingly than all the Bismarcks and Napoleons."

### The Five Sons

Mayer Amschel's five sons were dispatched to different European financial capitals — the network structure that made the family's later cross-border operations (funding multiple governments and, at times, multiple sides of the same conflict) possible. **[Note:]** Griffin's text names individual sons in specific episodes (Salomon and James recur most prominently) but does not provide a single consolidated table of "which son went where" — this is well-documented general history rather than a specific Griffin claim, and would be worth verifying independently if built into its own page (see Suggested Knowledge Files).

## Character and Method

Griffin devotes significant space to the personality profile he says defines the Rothschilds and, by extension, the financiers who came after them — not out of biographical interest, but because this profile becomes the psychological foundation for his broader **[[The Rothschild Formula]]** theory (see that page for the full five-point doctrine).

- **Secrecy as deliberate strategy.** Griffin, quoting Derek Wilson, writes that "clandestinity was and remained a feature of Rothschild political and business life" — the family rarely sought public office, preferring instead to shape events through private financial leverage, informal access to statesmen, and — as detailed below — the cultivation of agents who could operate without the Rothschild name attached at all.
- **Profit from both sides of a conflict.** "Since the Rothschilds often financed both sides in a conflict... the House of Rothschild profited handsomely" across two centuries of European war — the specific behavior that gives *[[The Rothschild Formula]]* its name.
- **Mastery of private intelligence and smuggling.** The family "operated a highly efficient intelligence gathering" network and had "mastered the art of smuggling on a large scale," using courier networks that regularly outpaced official government and military communication.
- **The Disraeli portrait.** Novelist and future British Prime Minister **Benjamin Disraeli**, who Griffin says "greatly admired" the Rothschilds, modeled the financier character *Sidonia* in his 1844 novel *Coningsby* directly on the family — a fictionalized portrait Griffin uses as evidence of the contemporary reputation the family had earned. He also attributes to "one Rothschild" the chilling line: **"When the streets of Paris are running with blood, I buy."**

## Case Study: The Napoleonic Wars and Waterloo

The fullest single Rothschild narrative in Griffin's book — see *[[The Bank of England]]* for the complete telling — centers on the family's role financing the coalition against Napoleon:

- When the Duke of Wellington needed funds for his Iberian campaign, **Nathan Rothschild** organized a gold-smuggling operation routed *through France itself*, using his sons **James** (posing as a Napoleon sympathizer in Paris) and **Kalmann** (coordinating along the Pyrenees) to convert British gold into Spanish currency and deliver it to Wellington undetected.
- Griffin recounts — and notes was later **vindicated by an actual English court ruling in 1915**, after a rival author was sued for repeating it — the famous story that Nathan Rothschild's private courier network delivered news of the **Battle of Waterloo** to London nearly a full day before Wellington's own official messenger. Rothschild allegedly used this information advantage to first sell British government bonds (letting the market assume Britain had lost), then buy the entire panicked market back at a fraction of its worth once prices collapsed — acquiring, in Griffin's phrase, "the dominant holding of England's entire debt at but a tiny fraction of its worth."

## Case Study: Belgium, 1830

Documented in full in *[[The Rothschild Formula]]*: when Belgium's 1830 revolution against Dutch rule created a financing opportunity, **Salomon Rothschild** declined to meaningfully fund the rebel side ("Our goodwill does not yet extend to the point of putting clubs into the hands that would beat us"), while **James Rothschild**, once the revolution was settled by negotiation rather than victory, moved immediately to capture the new government's finances, writing that the goal was "to make ourselves absolute masters of that country's finances." Griffin calls this "the strategy that has become the model of such operations ever since."

## The Rothschilds and America

This is the area of Griffin's book with the most material not yet consolidated anywhere else in this knowledge base.

### The First Bank of the United States (1791)

Per *[[The First and Second Banks of the United States]]*: Griffin, citing biographer Derek Wilson and historian Gustavus Myers, claims the Rothschild banking dynasty was "the power behind the Bank of the United States" — with **Nathan Rothschild** serving for a time as the official European banker for the U.S. government. Myers is quoted directly: "Under the surface, the Rothschilds long had a powerful influence in dictating American financial laws. The law records show that they were the power in the old Bank of the United States."

### George Peabody, the "Concealed Alliance," and the Rise of J.P. Morgan

This is the richest new material on this page. Griffin lays out a case — built from multiple historians, with appropriate hedging — that the House of Morgan's American banking dynasty was, from its origins, closely and perhaps secretly intertwined with the Rothschilds:

- **George Peabody**, an American who set up shop in London and would later mentor the Morgan banking line, allegedly received an early visit from **Baron Nathan Mayer Rothschild**, who — per a story Griffin attributes to writer Eustace Mullins, while noting Mullins "does not give a reference" and that the story cannot be verified — proposed to secretly bankroll Peabody's lavish social entertaining, since Rothschild himself was personally disliked by much of the London aristocracy. London society, the story goes, drank Peabody's wine and joked about Rothschild's manners "without realizing that every drop they drank had been paid for by Rothschild."
- **Why an intermediary was useful:** Griffin connects this pattern directly to the antisemitism of the period, noting a "strong anti-Semitic and anti-Rothschild sentiment had grown up in Europe and the United States," which gave the family "ample reason" to prefer working through non-Jewish agents rather than dealing under their own name.
- **August Belmont**, the Rothschilds' documented American agent, changed his name from **Schoenberg** upon arriving in New York in 1837 — a change Griffin attributes to the same antisemitic pressure — becoming the family's *known*, publicly acknowledged American representative (succeeding an earlier, deliberately anonymous-sounding agent firm, J.L. and S.I. Joseph & Company).
- **The 1848 scouting trip:** the family sent **Alphonse Rothschild** to evaluate whether to replace Belmont with a direct Rothschild presence. Alphonse's own letter home, quoted by Griffin, is a striking piece of prophecy: *"In a few years from now America will have attracted to itself the greater part of trade with China and the Indies and will be enthroned between the two oceans... I have no hesitation in saying that a Rothschild house, and not just an agency, should be established in America."* The recommendation was never acted on — a decision biographer Derek Wilson calls "the greatest opportunity the Rothschilds ever lost."
- **Griffin's speculative question:** he raises, without asserting as fact, the possibility that this apparent "lost opportunity" was not really lost at all — that the Rothschilds may have deliberately used Belmont as a *known*, publicly visible agent while simultaneously cultivating **George Peabody**, and later the **House of Morgan**, as an *unknown* one, effectively providing their own hidden competition. He supports this with a chain of historian quotations: John Moody ("The Rothschilds were content to remain a close ally of Morgan rather than a competitor as far as the American field was concerned"); Gabriel Kolko (Morgan's 1895–96 European gold-bond sales were "based on his alliance with the House of Rothschild"); and Sereno Pratt (comparing J.P. Morgan & Company's role to how "August Belmont & Company have long represented the Rothschilds").

### The American Civil War: August Belmont's Bond Dealing

Documented for the first time on this page (not previously covered in this knowledge base's Civil War material): with the First and Second Banks long gone and American banks unable to supply adequate war financing, Griffin describes **August Belmont**, as the Rothschild consortium's American agent, playing both sides of the Civil War's bond markets. He bought heavily depreciated Confederate state bonds at pennies on the dollar as the war turned toward the Union, betting the reunified country would eventually be forced to honor pre-war Southern debt at full value — while simultaneously serving as the chief agent selling **Union** bonds in England and France. Griffin relays, while flagging it as "doubtful," a story that Belmont personally offered President Lincoln Rothschild financing at 27.5% interest and was thrown out of the office — noting that whether or not the specific anecdote is true, it "represents a larger truth" about the family's reputation for profiting from both sides of a conflict.

**[Note — a further American connection worth flagging:]** Griffin separately notes, in his account of Edward Mandell House (President Wilson's closest adviser, pivotal both to the passage of the Federal Reserve Act and to Leon Trotsky's 1917 release from detention — see *[[The Federal Reserve System]]* and *[[The Bolshevik Revolution]]*), that House's father, Thomas William House, "had acted as the confidential American agent of unknown banking interests in London" during the Civil War, and that "it was commonly believed he represented the Rothschilds." This is presented by Griffin as contemporary belief/rumor, not documented fact.

### The 1857 and 1896 Financial Crises

- **1857 crisis:** Per material also covered in *[[The Bank of England]]*, the Peabody firm was rescued during the 1857 financial crisis "by the Bank of England, over which the Rothschilds held great influence," after which, Griffin states, "Morgan appears to have served as a Rothschild financial agent."
- **1896 U.S. gold rescue:** When the federal government needed to borrow $62 million in gold to defend its currency, the Treasury reportedly wanted to avoid publicly borrowing from "the Rothschilds" given anti-Jewish sentiment. Griffin quotes historian George Wheeler describing how the deal was deliberately restructured so that **J.P. Morgan** appeared as the primary lender while Rothschild involvement was minimized in public view — even though, per Wheeler, the original suggestion for pulling Morgan into the rescue had come **from the London branch of the House of Rothschild itself**.
- **The estate-size comparison:** Griffin notes that when J.P. Morgan Sr. died in 1913, his estate was valued at a comparatively modest $68 million — "paltry" next to the Vanderbilts, Astors, or Rockefellers — and his son Jack Morgan's 1943 estate was smaller still, at $16 million. He contrasts this with the estate of **Alphonse Rothschild**, who died in 1905 holding $60 million in American securities *alone*. Griffin's implication, stated carefully: "a major portion of the wealth and power of the Morgan firm was, and always had been, merely the wealth and power of the Rothschilds who had raised it up in the beginning."

### On Antisemitism as a Documented Business Factor

Griffin discusses J.P. Morgan Jr.'s reputation as, in his words, "a staunch anti-Semite" as a subject historians have debated — whether genuine personal prejudice or, as several of his cited sources suggest, a useful public posture that let American clients avoid the appearance of dealing directly with "international Jewish financiers." Griffin's own stated conclusion is deliberately measured: *"How much of Morgan's apparent anti-Semitism was real and how much may have been a pragmatic guise is, in the final analysis, of little importance... the fact remains that it was close, it was ongoing, and it was profitable to both."* **[Note:]** This entire thread documents antisemitism as a historical business and social pressure that shaped how the Rothschilds' American agents operated (necessitating name changes, intermediary firms, and public misdirection) — it is presented here as period history Griffin is reporting and analyzing, not as endorsement of the prejudice itself.

## Political Power: Cecil Rhodes and the Round Table

Documented more fully in *[[The Bolshevik Revolution]]*: Griffin, drawing on historian Carroll Quigley's *Tragedy and Hope* and *The Anglo-American Establishment*, describes **Cecil Rhodes** building his South African diamond and gold monopoly "with the cooperation of the Bank of England and financiers like Rothschild." When Rhodes organized his secret society (the eventual template for the Round Table Groups and, in America, the Council on Foreign Relations), Quigley's account lists **Lord Rothschild** among the potential members of its inner "Circle of Initiates," alongside figures like Arthur (Lord) Balfour and Albert (Lord) Grey. This is the thread connecting the family's financial history to the political-power narrative developed at length elsewhere in this knowledge base.

---

## The Founding of the Federal Reserve

Two direct Rothschild threads run through Griffin's account of the 1910 Jekyll Island meeting (see *[[The Federal Reserve System]]* for the full narrative):

- **Paul Warburg**, one of the seven attendees, is described by Griffin as "representative of the Rothschild banking dynasty" in addition to being a partner at Kuhn, Loeb & Co. and brother to Max Warburg, head of the German/Dutch Warburg banking consortium.
- Griffin later groups "the Warburgs in Germany and the Rothschilds in England" together as the two dominant European financial dynasties whose interests the American Federal Reserve System's architects were, in his account, consciously modeling their institution to parallel.

## A Note on Evidentiary Weight

This page draws entirely from one author's (Griffin's) synthesis of multiple secondary sources — historians, biographers, and at least one source (Eustace Mullins) whose claims Griffin himself flags as unreferenced and unverifiable. Some elements here are well-documented mainstream history (the family's origins, the Napoleonic-era financing, the Belgian episode, Belmont's documented role as Rothschild's American agent). Others are explicitly speculative even within Griffin's own text (the Peabody bankrolling story, the "known vs. unknown agent" theory about Morgan). If this material is used in future writing, it is worth distinguishing which category a given claim falls into — the individual entries above generally preserve Griffin's own hedging language for this reason.
