# US Deepens Somalia Footprint as Iran, Gaza, and Lebanon Tensions Simmer: A Recap of Antiwar.com's August 3 News Roundup

*Based on the Antiwar News podcast hosted by Dave DeCamp*

In his August 3 episode of Antiwar News, host Dave DeCamp walked through a dense week of developments across the Horn of Africa, the Persian Gulf, the Levant, and the Black Sea — arguing that a common thread runs through most of it: continued, and in some cases escalating, US military involvement abroad.

## A New US Foothold in Somalia's Bosaso

DeCamp opened with a story he said had gotten almost no attention elsewhere: a US military delegation's visit to Somalia's semi-autonomous Puntland region. According to Puntland officials, the visiting delegation — led by the commander of US Special Operations Command Africa — signed an agreement to expand the American military presence at Bosaso, a port city on the Gulf of Aden. Puntland's government described the deal as aimed at improving counterterrorism operations and maritime security, though DeCamp noted no US government statement on the agreement had surfaced; the story was carried only by Somali outlets.

DeCamp situated Bosaso within a larger regional picture. The base has already been used for US airstrikes against an ISIS affiliate operating in Puntland's mountains, using an airfield originally built by the United Arab Emirates — a facility DeCamp said has also figured in reporting on UAE arms shipments to Sudan's Rapid Support Forces. He also connected the expanded US presence to the broader Gulf of Aden theater and its proximity to Yemen, noting that neighboring Somaliland — recently recognized by Israel, in part for its strategic access to shipping lanes near Yemen — could give Western and Israeli forces additional positioning ahead of a feared future confrontation with Ansar Allah (the Houthis).

DeCamp also flagged a political wrinkle: reporting from the Somali Guardian indicating the Puntland deal bypassed Somalia's federal government entirely. He cautioned listeners against reading this as evidence the US is trying to fracture Somalia — Washington still officially backs the federal government and continues to support and arm its special forces — but said the episode reflects how fluid and fragmented the country's internal politics have become, especially after Puntland's 2024 break from the federal system and recent clashes between federal and regional forces there.

## Somalia Airstrikes Pass a Grim Milestone

DeCamp also reported on what US Africa Command described as its 77th airstrike in Somalia this year, a July 30 strike against al-Shabaab near the village of Jamaame in the country's Lower Juba region — notably, the same area where a November 2025 US strike killed 12 civilians, including eight children, a case recently the subject of a Guardian investigation. AFRICOM offered no further details on the latest strike, and DeCamp said he'd seen no corroborating reports from Somali media, the Somali government, or al-Shabaab.

He put the pace of strikes in historical context: after roughly 10 US airstrikes in Somalia in 2024 (Biden's final full year), that number jumped to 124 in 2025 under President Trump — according to the tracking group New America, more strikes than were carried out under Presidents Bush, Obama, and Biden combined. Despite the intensified air campaign, DeCamp noted, al-Shabaab continues to gain territory.

## Trump-Iran Standoff Continues

Turning to Iran, DeCamp covered Trump's Truth Social outburst after Tehran denied his claim that negotiations with Washington were underway. Iran's foreign ministry said only talks with Oman were taking place, and that the standoff over the Strait of Hormuz wouldn't change unless the US lifted its blockade of Iranian ports. Trump, in turn, insisted the blockade would continue and reiterated that Iran would "never" be allowed a nuclear weapon, while also telling reporters that Tehran faced its "last chance" before what he called "decapitation."

DeCamp noted unconfirmed reports the same day of a drone strike in Kuwait and an attack on a commercial ship in the Strait of Hormuz, as well as Iran's claim to have downed a US MQ-9 Reaper drone — signs, he said, that the conflict remains very much active.

Separately, DeCamp cited CNN reporting that a senior US Central Command intelligence official had asked analysts for "new, creative, and unconventional" ways to pressure Iran — a request CENTCOM's spokesman didn't deny. DeCamp connected this to Wall Street Journal reporting that CENTCOM commander Admiral Brad Cooper has floated a two-week intensified bombing campaign, an option the Joint Chiefs chairman has reportedly warned could produce US casualties given depleted regional air defenses.

## Shipping Disruption, Iraq Withdrawal

DeCamp also flagged a Reuters report that six Saudi-flagged oil tankers have begun rerouting around southern Africa — adding roughly 25 days to their voyages — to avoid a Yemeni blockade imposed by Ansar Allah, underscoring the commercial toll of the ongoing Red Sea conflict.

On Iraq, he discussed reports of a US troop and Patriot air-defense drawdown from the Kurdistan region, which a regional adviser characterized as a "pre-planned" step rather than a retreat. DeCamp said the withdrawal likely traces back to a Biden-era agreement to end the US-led anti-ISIS coalition mission by this September, though he suggested the pressures of the Iran conflict may have accelerated the decision. He said he remains skeptical a full US withdrawal from Iraq will actually materialize.

## Gaza: The "Board of Peace" Walks Back a Key Commitment

DeCamp devoted significant attention to what he described as a reversal by the Trump-created "Board of Peace," the body overseeing the president's Gaza plan. When the disarmament agreement was first announced, both the Board's Gaza envoy and Trump suggested Hamas's disarmament and an Israeli withdrawal would proceed together. After meeting with Israeli Prime Minister Netanyahu, however, the Board stated on social media that any Israeli withdrawal would happen only after disarmament was fully completed — a sequencing DeCamp said contradicts the Board's own earlier "lock step" language.

He cited independent journalist Jeremy Scahill's characterization of the shift as consistent with a recurring pattern: Palestinian negotiators reach an agreement, Israel imposes new conditions afterward, and US-aligned bodies treat the new terms as though they were part of the original deal. DeCamp drew a parallel to the unraveling of the Lebanon ceasefire, and noted that Israeli strikes continued in Gaza even after the Board's statement, killing civilians on both Sunday and Monday. He also cited health ministry figures showing roughly 1,250 Palestinians killed in Gaza since the October 2025 ceasefire was signed.

## West Bank, Lebanon, and Syria

Elsewhere, DeCamp covered a report that Israeli forces destroyed water wells serving Palestinian herding communities in the West Bank's Ras al-Ahmar area, leaving residents dependent on costlier, less reliable water from town. He also previewed a seventh round of US-mediated Israel-Lebanon talks in Rome, where Israel is reportedly expected to demand Hezbollah cede the strategic al-Taher ridge — even as Lebanon seeks a full ceasefire and Israeli withdrawal. And he noted continuing Israeli incursions into southwestern Syria's Daraa and Quneitra provinces, where forces have set up checkpoints and moved armor into villages.

## A Deadly Drone Strike on a Russian Beach

DeCamp closed the news segment with the war in Ukraine, reporting that a Ukrainian drone crashed into a crowded beach in the Russian Black Sea resort town of Gelendzhik, killing at least seven people, including three children. Russian officials blamed Ukraine for deliberately targeting civilians; Ukrainian media suggested the drone had been shot down by Russian air defenses and fell short of its intended target. DeCamp noted the incident followed a similar strike on a Sea of Azov holiday camp roughly a week earlier that killed 12 people, and additional Russian glide-bomb strikes on Zaporizhzhia that killed and wounded Ukrainian civilians — part of what he described as an ongoing exchange of long-range strikes even as front-line positions remain largely static.
