Failure of Imagination
When zero sum thinking is a mindset
Paul Ramirez
Mar 24, 2026
If you haven’t listened to Avraham Burg and Tucker’s conversation, I highly recommend it. As someone who is fascinated with the Israeli mindset, I found the conversation to have a wealth of information. Burg is a former Speaker of the Knesset and interim president of Israel. He grew up inside the system and spent decades at the center of Israeli political life. And in this interview, he genuinely transformed how I think about those in the Israeli political scene.






Here are the quotes that stood out to me the most:

1)
“We Israelis live in a reality where a constitution can be seen as a threat.”

Unfortunately, I am starting to think more Americans feel this way too. A lot of Americans, especially when it comes to vaccines or figures like Charlie Kirk, seem to adopt what I would call “Sam Harrisonian” thinking—where we must trust the institutions because institutions need to be trusted. Therefore, it’s okay to ask questions… but only after the endless emergency 😄.

2)
“When we think about the West in general… how don’t you understand that immigration brings you down—that you compromise your own existence?”

I thought this quote was especially interesting because immigration and open border is something that no one voted on. In fact, very few people were aware of how significant the rise in immigration took place during COVID.

In Sam Harris’s podcast episode #453, “AI and the New Face of Antisemitism” (January 2026), his guest Judea Pearl surprised him by saying that American Jews and Jewish organizations “were the arrowhead pressing for open borders.” There is much more to be said about that, obviously, I highly suggest you check out the podcast

Also, a lot of immigration is downstream of the many wars taking place in the Middle East and South America. I found it interesting that Burg didn’t really connect those dots—how stability in a region could prevent mass migration issues to begin with. That is a reoccurring theme, in my opinion, when it comes to Israeli thinking

3)
“How don’t you understand that a massive wave of Muslim immigration is going to compromise your very identity?”

Okay—he’s talking about identity here. And when it comes to Israel, Zionism, Judaism, and Jewry, identity is a big part of the conversation, and a topic that I think 99.9% of people are ignorant of, including me (until recently).

What does it mean to be Israeli? What does it mean to be American?

There’s no reason someone can’t be Muslim in America. In fact, I have plenty of friends and coworkers (Shariq for example) who are Muslim. In fact, they are some of the most peaceful people I know.

There’s also no reason why you can’t be an American Jew and want nothing to do with the Israeli government. Similarly, someone can be Muslim and want nothing to do with whatever radical movements might be happening elsewhere.

I think this issue of identity is at the very core of everything happening when it comes to Israel. And I also think that these issues are purposely being conflated to confuse the public into believing a larger narrative about why war is justified.

4)
“We don’t care. We hardly care, unfortunately—and it pains me—about American Jewry. When Netanyahu said a couple of years ago, ‘They’re Democrats, they don’t support my position anyway, let’s go with the Christian Zionists—that’s our political backbone,’ we basically gave up on American Jewry.”

Anyone who knows anything about Zionism has seen this tension before—that Jewish identity is sometimes used when it’s convenient. Theodore Herzl, the father of Zionism, despised Judaism and those who followed the Torah. He believed Jewish people should be a nation-state, not religious.

Meanwhile, if you try to critique the Israeli government’s behavior toward Palestinians or its neighbors, you can be immediately labeled as an antisemite, something we’ve seen happen to figures like Tucker Carlson or Megyn Kelly (or just anyone). It’s a tactic ladies and gentlemen, it’s okay to be critical of a government, just like it’s okay to criticize Trans or BLM activists (or any other movement that tries to shove race down your throat). You should not be judged by your DNA, and many want you to believe that White people, or European people, are inherently racist and therefore, guilty of whatever perceived crime of whatever guilt inducing party has an agenda to push.

Conclusion
I think this was an incredibly healthy conversation between Burg and Tucker. I’m trying to learn and don’t pretend to be an expert on anything. However, I do find it interesting because there is a lot of historical context behind what’s being discussed.

I can also see how both Israeli and American societies can get warped into paranoia, often when it comes to Muslims. At the same time, anyone who has picked up a book (or a dozen) on the topic would realize that intelligence agencies have infiltrated many Muslim extremist groups, and much of what we are seeing today is downstream of deeply flawed foreign policy.
