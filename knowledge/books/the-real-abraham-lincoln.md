# The Union Lincoln Forced

Most of us grew up with one version of Abraham Lincoln. He's the honest guy from the log cabin who saved the country and freed the slaves. His face is on the penny and he sits on a giant marble chair in D.C.

After reading Thomas DiLorenzo's *The Real Lincoln*, I don't buy that version anymore. I think Lincoln did something a lot bigger and a lot worse than most people realize. He took a country that was supposed to be a **voluntary** union of states and turned it into a **forced** one. And he did it with guns, prisons, and taxes.

## The country was supposed to be a choice

The whole idea of America started with a breakup. The Declaration of Independence says people have the right to leave a government that no longer works for them. That's secession. That's literally how the country began.

When the states signed on to the Constitution, some of them said so out loud. Virginia, New York, and Rhode Island all wrote that they could take back the powers they handed over if the government turned on them. The union was something states joined, not a cage they got locked into.

And people talked about leaving all the time before 1861. A lot of that talk came from the North. New England states threatened to split off around the War of 1812. Some Northern abolitionists wanted to split away from the slave states too. Nobody treated the idea as crazy or treason back then.

Lincoln came up with a different story. He said the Union came before the states and could never be undone. DiLorenzo argues that was just made up to fit what Lincoln wanted.

## What Lincoln actually wanted

If you look at Lincoln's whole career, the thing he pushed the hardest wasn't ending slavery. It was a big government money plan he got from his hero Henry Clay. It had three parts:

- **High tariffs** (taxes on imported goods) to protect Northern factories
- **A national bank** so Washington could control money and credit
- **Government money for railroads, roads, and canals**, which mostly helped the business guys who were connected

Lincoln pushed this for almost 30 years. He was also a well-paid lawyer for railroads. To DiLorenzo, the Republican Party of 1860 was basically the old Whig Party with a new name, and this plan was the heart of it.

## Follow the money

Here's a part most people never hear. The South paid a big share of the tariffs that funded the federal government. Right as the South was leaving, the North passed the Morrill Tariff, which jacked those taxes way up.

If the South left and became a low-tax trading zone right next door, trade would move to Southern ports and Washington's money would dry up. In his first inaugural speech, Lincoln promised he wouldn't invade anyone, except to "collect the duties and imposts." In other words, the one thing he wouldn't let go of was the tax money.

Then there's Fort Sumter. DiLorenzo, following some earlier historians, argues Lincoln set things up so the South would fire first. He sent a supply mission he knew would set things off. Afterward, he wrote to one of the officers involved that the mission had done its job, even though it "failed." Now Lincoln could start a war and make it look like the other side started it.

## What about slavery?

This is the part people get the most worked up about, so let Lincoln speak for himself.

In 1858 he said he was not, and never had been, in favor of making Black and white people socially and politically equal. He said he didn't want Black people voting, serving on juries, or holding office.

For most of his life he wanted to ship freed slaves out of the country to places like Africa or Central America. He kept working on that idea even as president.

In his first inaugural speech he said he had no right to touch slavery where it already existed. He even backed a constitutional amendment that would have protected slavery in those states forever.

In 1862 he wrote: "If I could save the Union without freeing any slave I would do it."

And the Emancipation Proclamation? It only "freed" slaves in places Lincoln didn't control. It left slavery alone in the slave states that stayed loyal to him. It was a war move, not a moral one.

Meanwhile, almost every other country that had slavery ended it without a war, usually by paying slave owners to free people. DiLorenzo points out this would have cost way less than the war did, in money and in blood. Around 620,000 people died. If ending slavery was really the goal, why was America the one place that needed a war that size to do it?

## How he kept the North in line

Forcing the South back was only half of it. Lincoln also cracked down on his own side.

- He **suspended habeas corpus** on his own, without Congress. That's your right not to be locked up without a reason. When the Chief Justice said that was unconstitutional, Lincoln ignored him.
- **Thousands of Northern citizens** were thrown in jail without charges or trials for speaking out against the war.
- **Hundreds of newspapers** that disagreed with him were shut down.
- An Ohio congressman, Clement Vallandigham, was arrested for antiwar speeches and **kicked out of the country**.
- Maryland lawmakers were **arrested** so they couldn't vote to leave the Union.
- He started a war, raised an army, and blockaded the South **before Congress even met**.

If any president did this stuff today, people would call him a dictator. Lincoln gets a monument.

## War on regular people

The way the war was fought matters too. Sherman burned his way through Georgia and the Carolinas. Sheridan wrecked the Shenandoah Valley so people there would starve. Union troops shelled cities and robbed homes and farms. These weren't battles between armies. They were attacks on regular families to break their will. And some of the same generals went west after the war and did the same thing to the Plains Indians.

## Then came the taxes

Once the Southern lawmakers were gone, nobody was left in Congress to stop the big government plan. So it all passed at once:

- The **first federal income tax**
- **Super high tariffs**
- A **national banking system**
- **Paper money** printed out of thin air (greenbacks)
- **Huge land and cash giveaways** to railroad companies, which led to scandals like Crédit Mobilier

The country didn't just get "saved." It got rebuilt into something new, with a powerful central government on top and the states underneath it.

## What we lost

Before the war, people said "the United States **are**." After it, they said "the United States **is**." That small change says a lot. We stopped being a group of states that chose to be together and became one big country run from the top.

The states lost their real check on Washington. The threat of leaving was the last line of defense if the federal government got too big. Lincoln took that away by force, and it never came back. Pretty much every big expansion of federal power since then sits on top of what he did.

## The myth

So why do most of us only know the marble statue version? DiLorenzo says it's because historians and politicians built Lincoln up into a kind of saint. If Lincoln is a hero, then everything he did to grow the government is heroic too. Questioning him starts to feel like questioning America itself.

But I think real patriotism means looking at the whole record. And when I look at Lincoln's own words and actions, I don't see a man who saved the American experiment. I see the man who ended it. The original deal was a union you could choose. Lincoln made sure it was a union you could never leave.
