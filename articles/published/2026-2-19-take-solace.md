Take Solace

(authors note: I'm slightly drunk and revised my thnking using chatgpt, but the concept is my own)

I think it’s easy to get bummed out about our failed institutions.

It feels like every politician is compromised. Every agency is captured. Every regulator is entangled. Every movement eventually gets absorbed or redirected.

You start reading history — about the American Heart Association and its war against saturated fats, the National Institutes of Health and similar policy positions — that our public health policies are all backwards. Or whatever industry, whether it is the vaccine industries or the dog shit education system.

It can start to feel systemic.

But here’s the part that gives me some comfort:

Corruption has a scalability problem.

If corruption exists at high levels, it doesn’t operate in isolation. It depends on networks. It depends on silence. It depends on mutual compromise. It depends on people not breaking rank — because exposure invites public backlash.

Corruption depends on loyalty tests. It depends on leverage. It depends on fear. It depends on everyone staying quiet.

That means it requires constant maintenance. It requires enormous overhead.

Goodness, by contrast, is decentralized. It can be crowdsourced. It doesn’t require coordination at the same level.

Evil has to worry about infiltration by someone with a conscience — a whistleblower, a defector, a “do-gooder.”

A system built on distrust eventually collapses under its own weight. Every insider becomes a potential liability.

But a person can simply be honest.

One person telling the truth does not require a coordination network. It doesn’t require a loyalty oath. It doesn’t require a hierarchy.

Goodness scales because it is low overhead.
