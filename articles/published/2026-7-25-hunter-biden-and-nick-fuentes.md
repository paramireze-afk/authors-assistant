What Is Wokism?
I don’t use “woke” as a slang term.

As Vivek Ramaswamy argues in Woke Inc., I think the woke revolution was manufactured and injected into our culture through the major media networks and our institutions in response to the Occupy Wall Street movement. Vivek points to a sharp increase in corporate and media language centered around terms like race, systemic racism, equity, diversity, inclusion, privilege, oppression, identity, social justice, unconscious bias, and white supremacy.

But it wasn’t just language. Vivek argues that these ideas became embedded in institutional policy through ESG investing, DEI offices, mandatory workplace training, hiring and promotion practices, university curricula, and corporate activism. Over time, the same worldview was reinforced across the media, higher education, large corporations, and government institutions.

His argument is that, after Occupy Wall Street, corporate America found it much safer to encourage Americans to divide themselves along lines of race, gender, and identity than to unite around class, finance, or corporate power. In other words, they figured out how to get the people with pitchforks to fight the people with torches.

The thing that fascinates me about the woke revolution is just how much power the banks can exert across almost every facet of our civilization. If ever that is a rabbit hole you wish to go down, there is no better book than “The Creature from Jekyll Island”. I plan on rereading that book once a year.





Hunter Is Incredibly Woke
It’s not his fault. Many of us have been there, myself included until I stumbled across Jordan Peterson around 2014. I actually think Hunter is sincere and probably a decent guy. I don’t have any personal qualms with him.

That said, I know Podcastistan, and I don’t think he’d fare well answering difficult questions about his business dealings in Ukraine while serving on the board of Burisma, his father’s role in withholding aid unless Ukraine removed Prosecutor General Viktor Shokin, who was investigating corruption involving Burisma while Hunter was on the board, or the many other controversies surrounding his laptop and personal life. Whether you think those criticisms are fair or not, Nick, and many others could easily press him on those issues and I don’t think he would fare well.

Overall, I enjoyed the conversation. I still think Nick is a tool, but I also think he’s playing a useful role sitting down and having these kind of conversations. Whatever you think of him, he has had a tremendous influence over a generation of young conservatives online. That said, I also think his reputation has been in sharp decline among many of his most loyal followers, especially since taking on the MSM perspective surrounding Tyler Robinson and Charlie Kirk. Other reasons too, but that was what really made me take a second look at Nick.

As for Hunter, I also think he’s playing a valuable role by participating in conversations like this. That’s exactly what I think the world needs more of. It forces each side to stop caricaturing the other’s arguments. It gives people time to reflect instead of simply reacting. It also helps destigmatize difficult conversations around topics like race.

There were a few moments where things became tense, and around the 30:00 mark (watch through about 31:35), you could almost see the other side of Nick starting to come out.
