Why Melisandre Matters
How she captured my imagination
Paul Ramirez
Jan 21, 2026




At first, I hated Melisandre, the Red Woman, an ominous priestess demanding blood sacrifice and using dark rituals in service of Stannis Baratheon, whom she believes to be a prophesied savior. Early on, I thought she was the devil: willing to burn the innocent, convinced that her god’s will justified any atrocity.

But her character became interesting to me once the threat of the White Walkers became known. In that moment, she spared the life of Davos Seaworth, Stannis’s Hand. That moment really struck me when, the pragmatism behind her decisions and how she completely recalculated everything upon learning of the threat of the north.

From then on, Melisandre became one of my favorite characters. As much as I hated her, it became clear that she wasn’t acting out of emotion or hatred, but out of an ideological belief, and at a level that I will never understand, that at some level, she believed she was doing what had to be done.

At the Battle of Winterfell, she used her fires to stave off the White Walkers. She does not defeat the Night King directly, but she helps hold the line long enough for humanity’s last defense to survive. Without her, humanity is doomed and I think that is also symbolic in ways that I don’t quite understand.

Melisandre’s ending was especially meaningful to me. Once the battle was won, she walks out into a field, removes her necklace and allows herself to die. She was a terrifying character, and I can’t help but think she is one of my favorite, if not my favorite, characters of all time.
