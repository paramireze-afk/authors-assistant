# The Real Anthony Fauci: Bill Gates, Big Pharma, and the Global War on Democracy and Public Health (2021)

Kennedy's most commercially successful and most contested book, and the clearest full statement of his [[Regulatory Capture]] thesis applied to public health.

## Central thesis

[[Anthony Fauci]], as longtime director of [[NIAID]], and the broader institutional apparatus he led, systematically prioritized patentable pharmaceutical interventions (drugs, vaccines) over cheaper, non-patentable public-health measures — across both the 1980s–90s HIV/AIDS response and the COVID-19 pandemic — enabled by regulatory capture, media consolidation, philanthropic influence (notably the [[Gates Foundation]]), and the professional marginalization of dissenting scientists.

## Supporting evidence marshaled in the book

- FOIA-obtained NIH internal emails and funding disclosures.
- Patent records connecting NIH-funded research to pharmaceutical products.
- A historical account of the [[AZT]] approval process and early HIV/AIDS drug policy.
- Funding history of [[Gain-of-Function Research]], connecting NIAID grantmaking to the [[EcoHealth Alliance]] and the [[Wuhan Institute of Virology]] (later expanded into *[[The Wuhan Cover-Up]]*, 2023).
- Financial-relationship mapping between the Gates Foundation, WHO, vaccine manufacturers, and global-health research funding.

## Important people introduced or expanded

- **[[Anthony Fauci]]** — primary subject.
- **[[Bill Gates]]** — treated as a central philanthropic-industrial actor shaping global-health priorities.
- **[[Peter Duesberg]]** — HIV/AIDS dissident scientist (his hypothesis that HIV does not cause AIDS is treated sympathetically; this is scientifically rejected by the overwhelming weight of virological evidence — see [[Scientific Controversies]] in the main profile).
- **[[Ralph Baric]]**, **[[Peter Daszak]]** — gain-of-function and coronavirus researchers, later central to *The Wuhan Cover-Up*.

## Reception

The book was a #1 *New York Times* bestseller despite (or, per Kennedy, partly because of) being widely rejected by public-health researchers, medical societies, and fact-checking organizations, who characterized its central claims — particularly regarding HIV/AIDS and vaccine safety — as scientifically unsupported. Kennedy and allies frame this rejection itself as evidence of the media- and institution-capture dynamics the book describes. See the main profile's [[Public Controversies]] section for a fuller treatment of this dispute.
