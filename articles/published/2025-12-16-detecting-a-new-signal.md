Detecting a new Signal
Rantings of a slightly drunk listener of darkhorse
Paul Ramirez
Dec 16, 2025
I often wonder why it is that sometimes I’m able to detect a signal while others seem to get lost in the noise. Do you ever think about that? Why you are able to not fall for obvious bullshit while the masses do?

Lately, though, I think I’m starting to notice a new signal, though I’m not even sure it’s real yet. I’m not entirely sure how to explain it because the stories I pay attention to are not usually the ones that others do, but I’ll give it a shot.

For example, when Trump and his circle flip-flopped on the Epstein files, that stood out to me as something new, a signal we aren’t used to seeing. Whether intentional or not, after being on record for years pushing for transparency, that reversal communicated compromise. And I actually think it’s a mistake to fault them too harshly for it, because this pattern is everywhere. In fact, I’ve even wondered whether he was intentionally signaling that he was compromised and acting accordingly. (Total conspiracy theory on my part 🙂.)

Goliath has an incredible number of levers and sticks to apply pressure to anyone with influence. Let’s not pretend we’re immune to that.

This is where I venture into my hypothesis. I’ve been noticing similar behavior among people who are supposed to be “independent” media types. Again, I won’t list a bunch of examples, because I follow different people and don’t want to derail the point. But recognizing a new signal usually starts with pattern recognition. And the pattern I’m seeing, whether it’s genuinely new or just new to me, is how supposedly independent voices become conspicuously aligned with mainstream narratives at crucial moments.

Once again, I could point to specific examples (cough.. Nick Fluentes) but if you’re not following the same stories, it would probably sound like nonsense. The reason I bring this up is that, in my opinion, anyone with influence eventually runs into something genuinely nasty. And it’s our responsibility, as observers of sorts, to understand that influence itself makes people vulnerable to Goliath’s pressures.

If there’s a way to disarm Goliath at all, it’s by recognizing these patterns, especially when people with a long history of consistent messaging suddenly reverse course. Think about it: if every time someone “flips,” the general population becomes suspicious of external influence instead of in-fighting (which is the goal), that’s a win and, in my opinion, that will disarm Goliath.
