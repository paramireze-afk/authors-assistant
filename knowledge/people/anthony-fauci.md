# Anthony Fauci

> **Method note:** This document reconstructs the case against Anthony Fauci as built by [[Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] and a defined set of physicians, scientists, journalists, attorneys, legislators, and activists who made that case in their own primary work. Because the subject is a specific, living public figure, this document departs from the pure "critics only" structure of the AZT, Thimerosal, and Vioxx documents in one respect: it includes a dedicated section presenting Fauci's own documented responses and defenses, consistent with fairness practice used in the original [[Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] profile's "Public Controversies" section. Every claim below is attributed to the person or document making it.

## Overview

Anthony S. Fauci directed the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases (NIAID), a $6+ billion-budget institute within [[NIH]], from 1984 until his retirement in December 2022 — a tenure spanning seven presidential administrations and the HIV/AIDS epidemic, the 2001 anthrax attacks, SARS, H1N1, Ebola, Zika, and COVID-19. He is, by a wide margin, the most consequential and most litigated-over figure in this knowledge base's pharmaceutical- and public-health-controversy series, appearing already in [[AZT (Zidovudine)]] (HIV/AIDS era), [[Vioxx (Rofecoxib)]] (regulatory-capture framework), and the [[Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] profile (as the central subject of *[[The Real Anthony Fauci]]*). This document consolidates and substantially extends that material, with particular focus on strands not yet covered elsewhere: the COVID-19 origins/gain-of-function controversy, the Great Barrington Declaration dispute, financial conflict-of-interest litigation, and the ongoing (as of this writing) congressional and legal reckoning — including a Senate hearing that took place the same week this document was compiled.

## Criticism From Two Directions

A recurring theme worth stating up front, because it complicates any single-narrative account: Fauci has been attacked by sharply opposed camps for opposite reasons, sometimes by the same individuals at different points in his career.

- **The 1980s AIDS-activist critique (Larry Kramer, ACT UP):** playwright and ACT UP co-founder [[Larry Kramer]] wrote an open letter in the *San Francisco Examiner* (June 26, 1988) calling Fauci a "murderer" and an "incompetent idiot," arguing that NIAID's caution and bureaucratic slowness in approving and expanding access to experimental AIDS drugs was costing lives — the opposite complaint from the one [[Peter Duesberg]] and later Kennedy would make about the same drug (AZT), which held that Fauci pushed the drug to market too fast and too toxically (see [[AZT (Zidovudine)]]). Kramer later wrote that Fauci, appearing before Rep. Ted Weiss's House subcommittee, had been forced to publicly admit incompetence. Notably, Kramer and Fauci later became personal friends, and Kramer was, by Fauci's own account, one of his most vocal defenders during the COVID-19 pandemic — a reconciliation that complicates any flat "Fauci vs. his critics" narrative.
- **The Duesberg/Kennedy critique:** by contrast, holds that Fauci's NIAID pushed a toxic, inadequately tested drug (AZT) through accelerated approval, prioritizing a patentable pharmaceutical response over cheaper alternatives and suppressing dissenting HIV-causation science — see [[AZT (Zidovudine)]] for the full reconstruction, including RFK Jr.'s citation of a physician source (Dr. Robert E. Willner) questioning the elevated transfusion rate in the Vioxx-era placebo-controlled AZT trial arm.

Both critiques target the same institutional structure — an NIAID director wielding outsized influence over which treatments reach patients and how fast — but reach opposite conclusions about whether that influence moved too slowly or too fast.

## Career Overview

- **1968–1984:** Joins NIH as a clinical associate; rises through NIAID's research ranks studying immune-mediated diseases.
- **1984:** Appointed NIAID director by President Reagan, months before the HIV/AIDS epidemic becomes a national crisis.
- **1984–2000s:** Oversees NIAID's HIV/AIDS research portfolio, including AZT's development pathway (see [[AZT (Zidovudine)]]) and the broader antiretroviral era; becomes a fixture of the ACT UP-era protest movement and, later, a central architect of the drug-trial reforms (parallel-track access, community advisory boards) that followed activist pressure.
- **2001:** Plays a lead role in the federal anthrax-attack response.
- **2003–2008:** Central to the design and launch of PEPFAR (President's Emergency Plan for AIDS Relief) under President George W. Bush.
- **2014–2016:** Leads NIAID's Ebola and Zika response efforts, including debates over travel restrictions and experimental-therapy access.
- **2020–2022:** Becomes chief medical advisor to Presidents Trump and Biden during COVID-19; the most publicly visible U.S. government scientist of the pandemic era, and simultaneously its most attacked.
- **December 2022:** Retires from NIAID and federal government after nearly 54 years of federal service.
- **2023–2026:** Subject of continued congressional investigation, financial-disclosure litigation, a preemptive presidential pardon, and — as of this writing — a live Senate hearing dispute over the pardon's scope.

## Financial and Institutional Conflicts of Interest

Kennedy's foundational claim, developed at length in *The Real Anthony Fauci*, is that NIAID's roughly $6.1 billion annual budget (a figure Kennedy cites, expanded to $7.7 billion when combined with Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency biodefense funding he says was routed through NIAID) gave Fauci outsized personal control over the direction of global biomedical research, and that this concentration of grant-making power — combined with individual royalty income — created structural conflicts of interest.

The royalty question specifically concerns the NIH "Inventor Award" program, under which federal scientists named as co-inventors on government-owned patents can receive personal payments when those patents are licensed to private companies. Watchdog groups [[Open the Books]] and [[Judicial Watch]] pursued a multi-year FOIA lawsuit (filed 2021) that forced NIH to disclose more than $2.685 billion in total royalty payments to NIH institutes and scientists between 2010 and 2023, of which over $1 billion was specifically marked for individual inventors; Fauci, as NIAID's director and a co-inventor on multiple patents, was among the recipients, though the litigation — still active as of 2026 — has focused on compelling the NIH to disclose individualized, unredacted payment amounts rather than only aggregate figures. [[Rand Paul]] questioned Fauci directly about these royalty payments in Senate hearings in 2022, and Open the Books founder Adam Andrzejewski has stated publicly that he considers Fauci's congressional testimony on the subject to have been misleading.

## The HIV/AIDS Era

Covered in depth in [[AZT (Zidovudine)]]. In summary, Kennedy's account holds that Fauci enabled Burroughs Wellcome to bring AZT to market at high price and high toxicity, structured NIAID-funded trials to favor patentable interventions, and marginalized dissenting scientists including [[Peter Duesberg]] and [[Kary Mullis]]. Fauci's own institutional legacy from this era is more contested even among sympathetic accounts: he is separately credited — including, per his own later remarks, by Kramer himself — with implementing "parallel track" reforms that expanded experimental-drug access in response to ACT UP pressure, a reform some AIDS-era critics regard as a genuine and responsive policy change rather than only defensive damage control.

## Gain-of-Function Research and COVID-19 Origins

This is the most extensively documented and most currently active front of Fauci criticism, grounded in FOIA-released emails, subpoenaed Slack messages, and House Select Subcommittee investigative reports.

### The February 1, 2020 conference call
According to the House Select Subcommittee on the Coronavirus Pandemic (chaired by Rep. Brad Wenstrup), Fauci convened or participated in a conference call on February 1, 2020, with virologists including [[Kristian Andersen]] (Scripps Research), [[Robert Garry]] (Tulane), [[Andrew Rambaut]] (University of Edinburgh), and [[Edward Holmes]] (University of Sydney), alongside then-NIH director [[Francis Collins]], to discuss the possibility that SARS-CoV-2 had a laboratory origin. The Subcommittee's March 2023 memorandum states new evidence indicates Fauci "prompted" the drafting of a paper to address the lab-origin possibility, contrary to his later public framing that he played no editorial role.

### The Proximal Origin paper
Three days after the call, the same group of scientists began drafting what became "The Proximal Origin of SARS-CoV-2," published in *Nature Medicine* on March 17, 2020, concluding that a laboratory scenario was "not plausible." Fauci cited the paper publicly at an April 17, 2020 White House briefing to dismiss lab-origin questions, describing it as the conclusion of a group of highly qualified evolutionary virologists, without disclosing his own role in prompting the paper's drafting. FOIA-released emails show Fauci wrote to Andersen after publication, "Nice job on the paper" — a message cited by critics as evidence of direct involvement he did not initially disclose to Congress.

### The Slack messages (released 2023, expanded 2026)
Subpoenaed Slack communications among the paper's authors, portions of which the House Select Subcommittee inadvertently released following a 2023 hearing and additional messages released in 2026, show Andersen privately describing a lab-related accidental origin as "highly likely" and "not some fringe theory" in the same period he was publicly and increasingly emphatically dismissing the possibility; other authors used similarly candid private language about the plausibility of a lab-based origin that did not appear in their public statements or the published paper. Andersen has testified to the Subcommittee that his view shifted based on subsequent scientific analysis, not external pressure; critics, including Subcommittee Republican staff, argue the documentary record shows the paper's conclusions were shaped in advance of, rather than derived from, that analysis.

### Uncredited involvement and funding context
The Subcommittee's 2023 memorandum additionally states that [[Jeremy Farrar]] (then director of the Wellcome Trust) played an uncredited role in shaping the paper. Separately, [[Richard Ebright]] (Rutgers molecular biologist) has stated publicly, and was cited in Rand Paul's congressional exchanges with Fauci, that NIH-funded research conducted by the [[EcoHealth Alliance]] at the [[Wuhan Institute of Virology]] met the definition of gain-of-function research of concern for which federal funding had been paused from 2014–2017 — directly contradicting Fauci's repeated congressional testimony that NIH never funded such research at the Wuhan lab. This funding history and the associated DEFUSE grant proposal are covered in greater technical depth in [[AZT (Zidovudine)]]'s discussion of *[[The Wuhan Cover-Up]]* (2023).

### The perjury dispute and Rand Paul's referrals
Fauci testified before the Senate HELP Committee on May 11, 2021, stating NIH had never funded gain-of-function research at the Wuhan Institute of Virology. Senator Rand Paul disputed this in real time and, in July 2021, formally referred Fauci to the Department of Justice for a criminal investigation into whether that testimony constituted a false statement to Congress. Paul renewed the referral in 2023, citing Fauci's internal emails (including a February 2020 message in which Fauci wrote that Wuhan University scientists were known to be working on gain-of-function experiments involving bat viruses) as further evidence the May 2021 testimony was false. The Department of Justice did not pursue charges within the original statute-of-limitations window.

## The Great Barrington Declaration and Lockdown Policy

On October 4, 2020, epidemiologists [[Jay Bhattacharya]] (Stanford), [[Martin Kulldorff]] (then Harvard), and [[Sunetra Gupta]] (Oxford) published the Great Barrington Declaration, arguing that blanket lockdown policies were producing severe collateral public-health harm and advocating a "focused protection" strategy for high-risk populations while allowing broader society to build immunity. FOIA-released emails (obtained by the American Institute for Economic Research and separately by the House Select Subcommittee) show then-NIH director Francis Collins wrote to Fauci four days later, on October 8, 2020, describing the document's authors as "fringe epidemiologists" and stating there needed to be "a quick and devastating published take down" of its premises. The same evening, Fauci replied to Collins forwarding a *Wired* UK article critical of the Declaration. Later correspondence, obtained through a separate Missouri attorneys general lawsuit deposition, shows Fauci directing staff to compile political op-eds opposing the Declaration and, in one email, comparing the Declaration to what he called the "AIDS denialist days" of the 1980s — a comparison critics (including Kennedy, given his own sympathetic treatment of AIDS-dissident science in *The Real Anthony Fauci*) regard as revealing given Fauci's own institutional role during that earlier era.

Kulldorff has publicly responded to the disclosure by stating a public debate would have been preferable to a private takedown campaign, and has said the invitation for such a debate remains open. Fauci, under deposition in the Missouri litigation, stated he did not recall directing the op-ed compilation effort described in the correspondence.

## School Closures

Portions of Fauci's contemporaneous pandemic-era diary, released by Senator Paul's committee in 2026, reportedly include entries in which Fauci reflects on his influence over policies that closed schools during the pandemic. This is in tension with his sworn statement to the House Oversight Committee in January 2024, in which he stated, "I did not close the schools." The full context and content of the diary entries, and whether they represent a direct contradiction or a broader reflection on influence versus formal authority (school closure decisions were made by state and local officials, not NIAID), remains a disputed point between Fauci's defenders and his congressional critics.

## Legal and Accountability Record

### The preemptive pardon (January 2025)
Hours before leaving office on January 20, 2025, President Biden issued Fauci a "full and unconditional" preemptive pardon covering his federal conduct from 2014 through January 19, 2025, including any actions taken as a member of the White House Coronavirus Task Force. Biden's accompanying statement said the pardon should not be read as an acknowledgment of wrongdoing, and was intended to protect against what he characterized as politically motivated prosecution threats from the incoming administration. Fauci stated publicly that he had committed no crime and that there were no legitimate grounds for any criminal investigation, while saying he appreciated the gesture.

### The autopen dispute
Because Biden's pardons in this batch, including Fauci's, were signed using an autopen device rather than Biden's own hand, some Republican officials — including House Oversight Committee Chairman [[James Comer]] — have argued the pardons' validity is legally questionable, a dispute that remains unresolved in court as of this writing.

### July 29, 2026: The Fifth Amendment hearing
On July 29, 2026, Fauci appeared before the Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee, chaired by Rand Paul, and invoked his Fifth Amendment right against self-incrimination in response to more than 100 questions, on the advice of counsel. Ahead of the hearing, Paul's committee released roughly 1,100 pages of Fauci's pandemic-era government diary. In his opening statement, Paul argued that a pardon "doesn't rewrite history" and does not prevent Congress from establishing the underlying facts. Fauci, in a statement read at the hearing, said Paul had an "obsession" with securing his prosecution and that the hearing's purpose was to elicit a statement that could open new legal exposure not covered by the pardon's fixed end date. Committee Republicans, including Comer, argued Fauci's decision to invoke the Fifth undermines the legitimacy of his own pardon, since a valid, unconditional pardon should have left him nothing to fear from truthful testimony. Paul announced the committee would hold a vote the following week on a resolution addressing Fauci's refusal to answer, with the possibility of a contempt finding. HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. stated publicly ahead of the hearing that Fauci could still face perjury prosecution for any false statements made after the pardon's coverage period, separate from the pardon's protection of past conduct. As of this writing, the committee vote and any further legal consequences remain pending.

## RFK Jr.'s Synthesis: *The Real Anthony Fauci*

Kennedy's 2021 book remains the single most comprehensive critical synthesis of Fauci's career, treated in detail in the [[Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] profile and [[AZT (Zidovudine)]]. Its central structural argument is that Fauci's conduct during COVID-19 replicated a template Kennedy says Fauci first established during the HIV/AIDS epidemic: use of a frightening, poorly understood infectious threat to justify expanded institutional authority; promotion of a patentable pharmaceutical solution over cheaper alternatives; and suppression of dissenting scientific voices through funding, publication, and media pressure. Kennedy explicitly compares Fauci's decades-long tenure and institutional reach to [[J. Edgar Hoover]]'s at the FBI. The book's COVID-specific chapters extend this template to hydroxychloroquine and ivermectin (which Kennedy argues were suppressed in favor of Remdesivir and vaccines), NIAID's gain-of-function funding history, and the pandemic-era public health measures discussed above.

## Fauci's Own Responses and Institutional Defenses

For fairness, given the subject is a living public figure:

- Fauci has repeatedly stated under oath that he never lied to Congress regarding gain-of-function research funding, maintaining a technical distinction between the NIH's own funding definitions and the definition used by critics such as Ebright.
- On the Proximal Origin paper, Fauci has stated he did not direct its scientific conclusions and that the paper's authors reached their conclusions independently; Andersen has testified consistently with this account, attributing his public position shift to subsequent scientific analysis rather than pressure from Fauci or Collins.
- On school closures, Fauci has stated under oath that he did not close schools, noting that such decisions were made by state and local officials and school boards, not by NIAID or federal health agencies, which could recommend but not mandate closures.
- Regarding the pardon and the Fifth Amendment invocation, Fauci has stated his legal team advised the move specifically because the pardon's coverage ends on a fixed date (January 19, 2025) and does not protect statements made afterward, and because he believed the hearing's purpose was to elicit exactly such a statement.
- On royalty payments, Fauci has stated that existing federal statutes and regulations did not require the specific disclosures Senator Paul sought, and has generally directed such questions to NIH's institutional ethics and disclosure officers.
- Regarding Larry Kramer and the broader ACT UP-era relationship, Fauci has publicly described a evolution from adversarial confrontation to close personal friendship, crediting activist pressure with improving the ethical and practical structure of federal drug trials.

## Recurring Historical Patterns

- **The same regulatory-capture and revolving-door framework** Kennedy applies to CDC (thimerosal), FDA (Vioxx, PDUFA), and NIAID (Fauci) recurs here in the specific form of NIH's royalty and grant-concentration structure — see [[Regulatory Capture]].
- **Private doubt, public certainty**: the Proximal Origin/Slack-message pattern (private acknowledgment of lab-leak plausibility alongside public dismissal) is treated by critics as structurally similar to the VIGOR-era pattern in [[Vioxx (Rofecoxib)]] (internal data at variance with public presentation) and the Simpsonwood pattern in [[Thimerosal]] (internal scientific discussion at variance with public messaging).
- **Whistleblower and dissenting-scientist marginalization**: a throughline connecting Duesberg and Mullis (HIV era), the Great Barrington Declaration authors (COVID era), and — in a different register — David Graham at FDA (see [[Vioxx (Rofecoxib)]]), all treated by these sources as evidence of a general institutional pattern rather than isolated incidents.
- **Criticism from ideologically opposed directions**: as detailed above, Fauci is a rare case in this knowledge base of a figure attacked with comparable intensity from both a "moved too slowly" (Kramer/ACT UP) and a "moved too fast/too toxically" (Duesberg/Kennedy) position regarding the same underlying drug-approval history.
