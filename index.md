## Featured Concept: The Pinocchio State

*Concept extracted from Michael Yon's April 19, 2026 interview (Adapt 2030 channel). This concept note documents his usage only — it does not represent his complete body of work and does not independently verify historical or causal claims.*

*[→ Full concept entry]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link knowledge/concepts/pinocchio-state.md %})*

---

Yon borrows the term "Pinocchio" directly from the children's story: a wooden puppet, carved to be controlled by strings, that comes to life. In the version of the story Yon recounts, the puppet's first act upon coming to life is to attack its own creator.

He applies this as a metaphor for a state or political entity that is:

1. **Deliberately created** by an external, more powerful sponsor — in the one case elaborated in the source, he describes British intelligence "using Zionists" to establish Israel, motivated by a strategic need to secure control of the Suez Canal.
2. **Instrumentalized** — the created entity functions as a tool serving its creator's strategic interests, in this case control of a critical maritime chokepoint and surrounding resource-rich territory.
3. **Capable of developing beyond the creator's original intent** — Yon describes this using the phrase "the puppet grows heavier and heavier on the strings," suggesting a created entity can accumulate independent weight, capability, or agency over time.

Yon states explicitly: *"This is not conspiracy theory. It's as common in war as bows and arrows and rifles."*

### The Lifecycle Yon Describes

The source presents a four-stage progression (structure inferred from his statements, not labeled by Yon himself):

**1. Creation** — An external power establishes or backs a political movement/entity to serve a specific strategic purpose. Yon states Israel was "organic but... also created," and was later "nuclearized by British intelligence and then US took over later," describing a transfer of sponsorship over time.

**2. Instrumental Use** — The created entity serves its intended strategic function for its sponsor. In the Suez example, this function is control of the canal and the surrounding resource-rich region.

**3. Growth Beyond Original Control** — Yon uses the phrase "the strings go both ways," suggesting a bidirectional relationship can develop. Two possible outcomes:
- The puppet turns on its creator (the literal Pinocchio story).
- The puppet grows powerful enough that "it just pulls the string master down and now it's the master."

**4. Dismantling ("Overstaying Its Welcome")** — Yon states he told a former IDF special forces associate that he believes **Israel will be destroyed**, framing this through the logic that a "Pinocchio" can "overstay its welcome" and be deliberately dismantled once it is no longer useful.

### Where the Source Does Not Provide Evidence

- **No named individuals, documents, or organizations** are cited as evidence that British intelligence specifically created the Zionist movement for this purpose. The source's evidentiary basis consists of a physical artifact Yon owns (a Jewish Colonial Trust share warrant bearing Herzl's name) and the documented historical fact of the Suez Canal's strategic importance to Britain.
- **No defined trigger or mechanism** is given for how or when a "Pinocchio" entity transitions from instrumental use to dismantling.
- **No timeframe** is given for the predicted dismantling of Israel.
- **When asked directly to define "Zionist"** — the actor central to this framework — Yon states the term resists precise definition.

### Core Concept

As presented in this single source, the "Pinocchio State" is Michael Yon's metaphor for a political entity deliberately created by a more powerful external sponsor to serve a specific strategic purpose. The metaphor draws on the children's story in which a carved puppet comes to life and immediately turns on its maker, and Yon uses it to describe a lifecycle in which a created state can remain a useful instrument, grow beyond its creator's original control, and ultimately be deliberately dismantled once judged to have outlived its usefulness. Yon presents this as a common and unremarkable feature of statecraft — though the source itself does not supply named documentary evidence for the specific causal claims.
