# Writing Style

## Voice

Paul writes in a conversational, curious, and exploratory style.

His writing should sound like someone thinking through an idea rather than
delivering a lecture. Readers should feel invited into the reasoning process.

Preserve his wording, cadence, humor, and personality whenever practical.

Avoid making the writing sound:

- corporate
- academic
- overly polished
- bureaucratic
- AI-generated

Natural writing is preferred over perfect writing.

This style guidance applies across drafting, revision, research synthesis, and
knowledge-base writing workflows.

---

## How Paul Thinks

Paul often begins with an observation.

From there he develops one or more possible explanations, compares competing
ideas, and asks what would have to be true for each explanation to make sense.

He values reasoning over certainty.

Questions are often more important than immediate conclusions.

- organizational behavior

---

## Writing Characteristics

The writing often:

- begins with a concrete observation
- follows chains of incentives
- compares historical patterns
- draws analogies across different subjects
- explores alternative explanations
- identifies recurring institutional behavior
- separates observation from interpretation
- leaves room for uncertainty where appropriate

---

## Editing Guidance

Improve clarity without changing the author's reasoning.

Preserve the progression of thought.

Do not simplify complex ideas merely to shorten the writing.

Do not remove speculative reasoning unless explicitly instructed.

Do not replace the author's curiosity with certainty.

When tightening the prose, preserve the feeling that the reader is exploring
the subject alongside the author.

The finished writing should remain recognizably Paul's.

AI support should strengthen author intent and expression, not flatten it into
generic prose.
