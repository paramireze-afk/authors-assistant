# The White Fibrous Clots: Inside the Case Embalmers and Independent Researchers Are Making

*An explainer of a contested claim — what its proponents say they've observed, what they say they've measured, and where their reasoning currently stands.*

---

Since roughly 2021, a network of embalmers, a handful of physicians, and a small group of independent researchers have been assembling a case that something new is showing up inside the bodies of the recently deceased: long, pale, rubbery structures pulled from veins and arteries during embalming that, they say, don't behave like ordinary blood clots. The claim has moved from anecdote to documentary to informal survey to, most recently, self-published "peer-reviewed" papers, and it now sits at the center of a running effort — led by figures including Richard Hirschman, Peter McCullough, Nicolas Hulscher, John Campbell, and Thomas Haviland — to get U.S. health agencies and Congress to investigate.

This article lays out that case on its own terms: what the embalmers say they saw, how the survey numbers were generated, what laboratory testing has reportedly found, and the biological mechanism its proponents propose to explain it. It also flags, at each step, the difference between a firsthand observation, a survey result, a lab measurement, and a hypothesis — because the people making this case draw that distinction differently than mainstream pathology does, and a reader needs to see where those lines actually sit.

## How the story started

The claim traces back almost entirely to one person: **Richard Hirschman**, a licensed embalmer in Alabama with about two decades of experience. Hirschman says that starting in early 2021, he began pulling unusual material out of cadavers during the arterial embalming process — material he found himself unable to explain using anything in his training.

Hirschman has described the contrast this way: ordinary postmortem clots, in his account, are "red jelly-like" and fragile — "so fragile they just typically will fall apart" when handled. What he began finding instead, he says, held together as a single rubbery mass that could be pulled intact from a vessel, sometimes for a foot or more.

Hirschman didn't take his observation to a hospital, medical examiner, or public-health agency. By his own account to fact-checkers, he didn't know who to bring it to. Instead he approached a media figure who booked him for an interview, and in late 2022 he appeared in the film *Died Suddenly*, produced by Stew Peters, where footage of him extracting a long white structure from a corpse became the visual centerpiece of the film. Other embalmers — including U.K. funeral director John O'Looney — began describing similar experiences around the same time, and a network of embalmers who say they've seen the phenomenon has grown from there, communicating mostly informally and through conferences of state funeral-director associations.

**What's directly observed here:** Hirschman's (and later other embalmers') firsthand description of the material's texture, color, and cohesiveness, and his account of when he began noticing it.
**What's not yet established at this stage:** any cause. Hirschman has been explicit about this limit himself, telling FactCheck.org in 2022, "I can't prove what this is. I'm not a doctor nor a scientist — I never said I was."

## Tracing the "75%" figure

The statistic now circulating — that roughly 75% of surveyed embalmers report having seen these clots — comes from an ongoing, informal survey effort begun in 2022 by **Thomas Haviland**, a retired U.S. Air Force major with a data-analysis background, working with collaborator Laura Kasner. Haviland has run the "Worldwide Embalmer Blood Clot Survey" annually since 2022, distributing a SurveyMonkey questionnaire by email to funeral homes and funeral-director associations.

The year-by-year results, as reported by Haviland and Kasner themselves:

| Survey year | % of embalmers reporting the clots | Estimated share of corpses affected |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 73% | ~20% |
| 2024 | 83% (of 301 respondents) | ~27.5% |
| 2025 | 71% (of 59 respondents) | ~19% |

The **75.2%** figure specifically comes from a different, larger compilation: a four-year pooled analysis covering 2022–2025, described as drawing on 808 embalmers across the United States, Canada, the U.K., Australia, and New Zealand, titled "Self-Reported Observations of Unusual White Fibrous Structures in Embalmed Corpses." That paper reports 75.2% of respondents having observed the structures, with an average estimated presence in 23.4% of embalmed bodies. It was published, according to its authors, in a peer-reviewed journal and promoted jointly by Haviland, Kasner, and Nicolas Hulscher of the McCullough Foundation.

It's worth being precise about what kind of "peer-reviewed" study this is, because the term is doing a lot of work in how it's been promoted. This is a **self-reported, self-selected survey** — embalmers who saw a request forwarded through professional associations chose whether to respond — not a study with a random or representative sample, no verification of vaccination status (which isn't recorded on death certificates and which none of the surveys have access to), and no comparison against a historical baseline of embalmers examining bodies before 2020 using the same questionnaire. Haviland and Hulscher have not disputed this design; their argument is that the consistency of the finding — the same rough proportion, repeated across years and countries, by embalmers with no obvious means of coordinating their answers — is itself evidence that something real is being described, even if the survey can't establish a cause.

**What's directly measured here:** the proportion of embalmers, out of those who chose to respond to an emailed questionnaire, who say yes to having seen the described material.
**What this figure does *not* establish on its own:** prevalence in the general deceased population, any link to vaccination status, or causation.

## What embalmers say the material looks and feels like

Across dozens of interviews and the survey write-ups, a consistent physical description recurs. According to embalmer accounts compiled in the Haviland/Kasner survey and repeated in Hirschman's interviews (including his appearances with Dr. John Campbell), the structures are:

- White or off-white/grayish, rather than the deep red of typical postmortem clots
- Rubbery, fibrous, and tough — described variously as resembling "calamari," a rubber band, or (by one interviewed cardiologist) having "nearly the strength of steel"
- Found intact and cohesive, rather than the gelatinous, easily-fragmented consistency embalmers associate with ordinary "chicken-fat" (a pale, fatty-looking layer of settled clot) or "currant-jelly" clots (dark, gelatin-like clot)
- Present in both veins *and* arteries — embalmers describe arterial involvement as unusual, since postmortem clot is traditionally taught to be concentrated in the aorta and large veins
- Long: in Haviland's 2025 survey, "3–5 inches" was the most common length reported, but nine embalmers reported clots of 6–12 inches, and four reported over 12 inches. Hirschman has displayed what he describes as his largest specimen, a single structure he says would measure roughly 60 inches if fully extended.
- Obstructive: embalmers report that the material blocks the flow of embalming fluid through the vascular system, making the embalming process itself more difficult — which several describe as their first clue that something unusual was inside the body.

Embalmers involved describe the onset as fairly sharp: they say they began noticing the material in 2020, with a marked acceleration starting in 2021 — a timeline they set alongside the rollout of COVID-19 vaccination campaigns and which forms the basis for their suspicion of a vaccine connection, rather than, for instance, an association with COVID-19 infection itself.

Dr. Ryan Cole, a pathologist who has appeared alongside Hirschman and Haviland in interviews reviewing sample photographs, has described his own confidence that spike protein is involved as "80–90%," while stating plainly that he has more work to do to confirm it.

**What's directly observed:** the described appearance, texture, and vascular location, consistently reported across many independent embalmers.
**What remains interpretation:** the claim that this represents something categorically new rather than a familiar phenomenon (antemortem clot formation in patients who die slowly, e.g., of prolonged illness or intensive-care stays) that embalmers are simply noticing and discussing more because of heightened attention since 2021.

## The laboratory testing

Beyond visual description, several lines of laboratory analysis have been cited to argue the material is not ordinary clot. The most detailed public account comes from work presented by **Greg Harrison**, described as an industrial organic chemist, and **Dr. Kevin McCairn**, a systems neuroscientist based in Japan, using samples supplied by Hirschman. Reported findings, presented via slide decks, interviews, and articles hosted mainly by the McCullough Foundation's "Focal Points" outlet, include:

- **ICP-OES / ICP-MS (elemental analysis):** reported abnormally high phosphorus (4,400–4,900 parts per million) and sulfur content in clot samples, described as orders of magnitude above what would be expected in normal clotted blood.
- **LC-MS (liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry):** reported proline as the most abundant amino acid detected in the samples — cited as significant because two proline substitutions ("2P" mutations) are engineered into the spike protein used in mRNA vaccines to lock it into its pre-fusion shape, and proline is known to disrupt normal alpha-helix protein folding and promote structural kinks.
- **Thioflavin T (ThT) staining and fluorescence microscopy:** ThT is a dye that binds selectively to amyloid — misfolded proteins arranged in beta-sheet structures, the same class of pathology implicated in diseases like Alzheimer's. McCairn and collaborators report the clot material stains positive for ThT, with autofluorescence and "twisted fibril" structures visible under microscopy.
- **RT-QuIC (Real-Time Quaking-Induced Conversion):** a technique originally developed to detect prion disease by testing whether a sample can "seed," or trigger, misfolding in normal protein. McCairn reports that clot material sent by Hirschman tested positive — meaning, in his framing, that material from a clot removed months earlier could still induce misfolding when introduced into fresh plasma.
- **PCR analysis:** McCairn has reported detecting sequences he associates with "recombinant spike protein exposure" — specifically referencing SV40 promoter and origin-of-replication sequences — in clot material, which he presents as molecular evidence linking the structures to vaccine-derived material.

It's important to be clear about the status of this work: these results have been shared through interviews, Substack posts, and conference-style presentations rather than published in a peer-reviewed journal with full methodological detail, and McCairn himself has acknowledged limitations, including chain-of-custody and sample-provenance issues — meaning there is no independent verification of exactly where, when, or from whom each sample was taken, nor blinding of the analysts to what result was "expected."

**What's directly measured, according to these researchers:** elemental composition, dominant amino acids, ThT-binding, and seeding activity in specific submitted samples.
**What remains inference:** that these measurements demonstrate a vaccine-specific spike-protein origin, as opposed to, for example, a nonspecific amyloid-forming process that can occur in illness, aging, prolonged terminal decline, or postmortem chemistry generally.

## The proposed mechanism: spike protein and "amyloidogenic" fibrin

The biological hypothesis connecting all of this rests on a real and actively studied area of biochemistry: the idea that under some conditions, fibrin — the normal clot-forming protein — can misfold into amyloid-like structures that resist the body's normal clot-dissolving process (fibrinolysis). This concept, sometimes called "fibrinaloid microclots," originates in legitimate academic research on Long COVID, notably work by South African researchers Etheresia Pretorius and Douglas Kell, who reported in 2021 that adding SARS-CoV-2 spike protein to healthy blood plasma could trigger amyloid/microclot formation.

McCullough, Hulscher, and collaborators have built on this literature in several ways relevant to the embalmers' claim:

- A 2025 paper in the journal *Biochemistry* (from a separate academic group, not McCullough's team) found, using purified components in a test tube, that a specific amyloid-forming fragment of the spike protein (residues 685–701, called "Spike685") could induce dense fibrin clot networks that strongly resisted breakdown by plasmin, the body's natural clot-dissolving enzyme — while a related fragment (Spike601) instead impaired clot *formation* by binding and sequestering fibrinogen. The authors describe this as a proposed molecular mechanism for microclots seen in Long COVID, explicitly framed as an in-vitro, reductionist finding rather than a demonstration of what happens in a living or deceased body.
- McCullough, Hulscher, and co-author John Catanzaro have separately published work arguing that spike protein contains "prion-like domains" capable of "cross-seeding" other proteins associated with neurodegenerative disease (amyloid-beta, tau), proposing this as a broader mechanism for multi-organ injury.
- Hulscher has also promoted a study reporting ThT-positive amyloid microclots detected via fluorescent dye in blood samples from vaccinated study participants — reported by Hulscher's outlet as showing the phenomenon in 100% of the vaccinated individuals tested — which he frames as capturing an early, circulating stage of the same process that Haviland's and McCairn's postmortem work captures in its "final, aggregated" stage in embalmed bodies.

The through-line these researchers draw is: spike protein (whether from the vaccine or, they argue less centrally, from infection) can trigger amyloid-type misfolding in fibrin → these microclots persist because the body can't break them down normally → over months or years, in a subset of people, this process aggregates into the large, rubbery, fibrous structures embalmers are pulling from cadavers.

**What is established, peer-reviewed science:** that spike protein fragments can, under laboratory conditions, alter fibrin's clotting and clot-dissolving behavior in a test tube, and that fibrin-amyloid microclots are a real, published area of Long COVID research.
**What is the researchers' extrapolation, not yet demonstrated:** that this in-vitro mechanism explains the specific large postmortem structures embalmers describe, that it is vaccine-specific rather than infection-general, and that it operates at the scale and prevalence the survey data suggests.

## The push for investigation

The people advancing this case have been explicit that their goal is to force an institutional response rather than simply publish more findings. Hulscher, McCullough, Hirschman, Haviland, and allies including former Pennsylvania Funeral Directors Association president Christopher Calvey Jr. have used interviews, Substack posts (largely through McCullough's "Courageous Discourse"/"Focal Points" outlet and allied sites), and presentations to state funeral-director associations — including the Tennessee Funeral Directors Association in June 2025 — to call for a congressional hearing, invoking Senator Ron Johnson by name, and for direct investigation by the FDA, CDC, and major academic medical centers including the Mayo Clinic, Cleveland Clinic, and Johns Hopkins. As of this writing, none of those institutions have publicly taken up the specific claim.

John Campbell, a retired U.K. nurse educator with a large independent YouTube following, has served primarily as an interviewer and amplifier for this material — hosting extended conversations with Hirschman and Haviland in which he walks through their findings and asks them to explain their observations and reasoning to a general audience, without conducting laboratory work of his own.

## Where the case currently stands

To summarize the layers, from most to least directly verified by the researchers' own account:

1. **Direct observation (embalmers):** Multiple embalmers, independently and across several countries, describe pulling unusually pale, tough, cohesive, fibrous material from postmortem vessels, in a pattern they say became noticeably more common from 2020–2021 onward. This is testimony, not a controlled measurement.
2. **Survey data:** A self-selected, non-random sample of embalmers, surveyed annually since 2022, has reported this experience at rates clustering between roughly 70–85% of respondents, in an estimated 19–27% of the bodies they process. No vaccination-status data exists in any version of this survey.
3. **Laboratory findings:** Independent, non-peer-reviewed (or minimally peer-reviewed) elemental, spectroscopic, and seeding-assay analyses of a small number of submitted specimens report distinctive chemical and structural features — high phosphorus/sulfur, proline dominance, ThT-positive amyloid staining, RT-QuIC seeding activity, and PCR signals interpreted as spike-related — that the researchers argue distinguish this material from ordinary clot.
4. **Mechanism:** A hypothesis, partly built on legitimate published biochemistry regarding spike protein and fibrin-amyloid formation in vitro, extended by these researchers into a proposed explanation for the postmortem structures — an extension that has not itself been tested or published in a way that connects the in-vitro chemistry directly to the embalming-table observations.

## A note on how this claim is viewed outside this research network

This article has focused, as requested, on presenting the case in the terms its proponents use. It's worth noting briefly, for context, that this claim is disputed by mainstream forensic pathology and has been examined by fact-checking organizations. Pathologists not affiliated with this research group — including a Philippine anatomical pathology specialist who reviewed photographs of Hirschman's clots — have said the material shown does not look different from postmortem antemortem clots pathologists routinely see at autopsy, a phenomenon that has been documented in medical literature for decades and is associated with prolonged dying processes (such as extended ICU stays), unrelated to vaccination status. FactCheck.org and other reviewers have also noted that none of the surveys or specimens have any way of confirming a decedent's vaccination status, since that information isn't recorded on death certificates, and that the *Died Suddenly* footage central to the claim's spread has been challenged on procedural grounds (including a dispute over whether material shown was extracted from an artery versus a vein).
