# Day 33: A Newborn, a Fast, and a Warning About War

*An account of Michael Yon's October 3–4 livestream from Japan. Everything below is the speaker's own view and claims, written up as he presented them.*

---

Broadcasting late at night from Japan, just as October 3rd was rolling into the 4th, Michael Yon opened his livestream on a tired but upbeat note. He's 33 days into a water fast, six days into fatherhood, and short on sleep. Over the next hour he covered everything from diapers to what he believes is a coming war between the United States and Mexico.

## Why the Stream Kept Cutting Out

The night before, Yon had been about an hour and a half into a stream when the feed began freezing and skipping. He cut it off and decided not to upload the recording, worried the broken copy would only frustrate viewers. His wife, Masako, later found a cleaner copy, which he hopes to post.

He said connection problems follow him almost everywhere, at home, in hotels, and on the road, despite the antennas and satellite internet all around. He contrasted this with big-name influencers, whose feeds, he observed, never seem to drop. One viewer suggested that the connection tends to break when he tells the truth; Yon agreed, joking that if he started promoting drugs, the channel would run flawlessly. To fix the problem, Masako ordered a portable Wi-Fi unit due to arrive the next day.

He also read a note from a viewer who had worried about the family after the missing video, and reassured everyone: they're doing well.

## Life With "Baby Bear"

The couple's newborn son, nicknamed Baby Bear, is keeping them busy. Yon described a day of frequent diaper duty and said the baby is gaining weight well. Their midwife's scale and the one they bought both read the same weight, 2.84 kilos, which he found reassuring.

He's discovered what calms the baby: walks outdoors, the sound of small waterfalls and running water, birdsong, and classical music, which they played often during the pregnancy. In the mornings, they give the baby some time in the sun.

He said he's loving being a parent and that, in his view, parenthood didn't start at birth. The couple ate a strict diet long before conception and considered themselves parents from the moment they knew Masako was pregnant.

## The Fast: Day 33, No Salt, No Plan

Yon is now working on day 33 of a fast, and he insists he feels fine. The fatigue viewers noticed, including a few comments about his speech, is from lack of sleep, he said, not from fasting.

His approach is deliberately unplanned. His previous 40-day fast began on a whim at a dinner in Okinawa, watching F-15s take off, when he finished a last bite of steak and simply started the clock. This one began the same way: after a big dinner Masako cooked for a visiting friend, his final bite was matcha ice cream. He dismissed the usual advice about tapering in over a week or two beforehand.

The biggest departure from conventional wisdom, he said, is salt. Apart from a short stretch around days 14 to 17, he has taken none, and he said he feels better this way than he did on his first long fast. Salt, in his experience, made him feel worse.

As for breaking the fast, he wants to start with a coconut, maybe two, since that's how he ended his last long fast. A viewer's suggestion of avocado and eggs appealed to him enough that he made a note of it. He won't jump straight to steak, but he doesn't plan anything elaborate either. He'll stop, he said, when he knows he's done, and the moment anything feels off, he'll end it on the spot.

His advice to a viewer who was starting a fast of his own: don't set a goal, just go until you feel done. In his experience, you'll often go longer that way.

## A War Between the U.S. and Mexico

The heaviest topic of the night was a prediction Yon had already posted on X: that a war between the United States and Mexico is "baked in." He doesn't know when or what it will look like.

He stressed that ordinary people on both sides don't want it. He has spent a lot of time in Mexico, has Mexican friends, and spoke warmly of Mexican people as hardworking and deeply devoted to their families, which he called the base of the whole soup of a healthy society. The average Mexican, he pointed out, isn't in a cartel.

In his framing, the conflict wouldn't really be one nation against another. He sees it as oligarchic power structures fighting other oligarchic structures. He claimed that the leaders of Mexico, Argentina, the United States, and Ukraine are all Zionists who have been put in place, and that a war would provide cover for this network to eliminate wealthy and powerful people in both countries who sit outside it, and to seize their money, gold, and property. He compared what he expects to what he says has been done in Gaza, Lebanon, and Ukraine.

When a viewer commented that Mexico wouldn't stand a chance, Yon pushed back, saying the comment missed what's actually happening. The goal, as he sees it, isn't to take territory but to disrupt and transform, and any country can be badly disrupted regardless of its military strength. He made the same point about his prediction of a war between Brazil and Argentina.

## War Is Mostly Not About Guns

Yon argued that most of war has nothing to do with ships and rifles. He pointed to money printing, arguing that since the Federal Reserve was created in 1913, it has inflated away trillions in American wealth, and that whoever controls the printing press can finance wars through inflation instead of taxes. Because the dollar is the world's reserve currency, he said, that cost gets spread across the entire globe.

He recommended *War Is a Racket* by Marine Corps General Smedley Butler, a short book from the early 20th century, saying its description of war still applies today. A viewer noted it's available on archive.org, and Yon praised the site for old, out-of-copyright books that would otherwise cost thousands.

He described the president as a figurehead and claimed the military's top leadership is openly Zionist and Satanist. He agreed with viewers who said young people believe they're serving their country while being misled.

On Ukraine, he said men are being pulled off the streets and sent to the front to die, calling it depopulation. He warned that the same could happen in America, and that with digital money, families of young people who refuse a draft could be cut off financially.

He also recounted a cannabis show he and Masako attended in Buenos Aires, which he said was sponsored by several government ministries, including defense, health, and education. He pointed to the Argentine president's family name as another link in the network he describes.

## They Plan in Centuries

Agreeing with a viewer who said world events are scripted, Yon said the planning is often telegraphed decades in advance. The people behind it, he said, think in centuries; five-year plans are for children and university students. He noted many old documents survive that reveal this kind of long-term strategic thinking. A viewer who mentioned the Club of Rome's regional map got agreement that the world is clearly being divided into regions.

## Your Body Belongs to Your Grandchildren

One of the points Yon emphasized most was about preparing for parenthood long before it happens. He explained that the eggs that will form a woman's future children already exist inside her, and even existed while she was inside her own mother, spanning three generations. That means choices made in youth, such as drug use in college, can directly affect one's grandchildren.

His advice to young people: from the moment you know you want a family, even at 15, start treating your body as a parent would. Study up and be careful. Viewers agreed, and one said she already tells young people this.

## Raising a Bilingual Child

Responding to a viewer's sleep and reading tips, Yon said he and Masako plan to start reading to their son by six weeks at the latest. Masako will read in Japanese and he will read in English. To avoid confusing him early on, they'll let Japanese develop as the formal base, while Yon simply speaks and reads English to him.

Later he plans to go deep on grammar, earlier than most schools would, because he considers strong grammar the foundation for everything that follows. He wants his son copying books by hand alongside him, practicing cursive, since writing teaches things reading doesn't. He also said he'd never send his children to university.

## On Cannabis

Asked what might be added to cannabis to "weaponize" it, Yon said he didn't know, and that the contents could vary from place to place, but he was sure it's not for anyone's benefit. He recalled photographing a sign in a European cannabis district warning of fines for vomiting. Viewers added concerns about pesticides and greenhouse chemicals.

## Viewer Questions, Rapid Fire

- **Visiting Japan:** Japan has many climates. Okinawa is like perpetual Florida, while Honshu has mountains and real winters. He called it a wonderful country with something like ten times the history of the United States.
- **Georgia, Armenia, and Azerbaijan:** He and Masako spent about a month in Georgia and Armenia. Like Poland or Panama, they're small countries on major routes, caught "between elephants that are fighting." He said he doesn't know enough about their politics to say more.
- **The Dubai hijacking story:** He called it a near-certain false flag, putting it at 99 percent, and listed other events he regards the same way.
- **The UK:** He agreed talk of war is growing there, mentioning the Falklands and possible civil unrest, and pointed to France as well.
- **Trump and the vaccine:** He agreed with a viewer who asked how anyone can still support Trump while he promotes the vaccine, suggesting some supporters' thinking may have been affected.
- **Japan's economy:** Definitely inflationary, though he can't measure by how much.
- **Copper cups:** He'd gladly buy unlacquered copper drinkware from Japan, where he says trust is far higher than in most of the world.
- **A nephew deployed to Okinawa:** Marines tend to love it there, but Okinawa could become a war zone.
- **What's next in the war:** Famines, and more war everywhere.

## Signing Off

Yon described himself as a watchman on the wall, recalling that Bret Weinstein once called him "David's eyes" after a trip to the Darién Gap. His aim, he said, is to pass along what he calls "24-karat" information that people can use to protect their families, whether that means avoiding a draft or surviving a famine.

Urged by several viewers to get some rest, he agreed he'd better. He thanked his supporters on behalf of himself, Masako, and eventually Baby Bear, "when Baby Bear understands what that is," and said good night.
