Book Subtitle: Zionism's Journey from Identity Crisis to identity theft -Rabbi Yaakov Shapiro

Jan 29, 2026
Okay, I have to admit this is the first time I have ever seriously engaged with the topic of Torah or anything related to it. That said, I think about Zionism a lot, and I am hoping to learn more as I work my way through this monstrous book. I am just over a hundred pages in, and I have found myself curious about the concept of Erev Rav, which, broadly speaking, refers to figures within the Jewish tradition who are understood to be operating from within the community while undermining or distorting the Torah, some unwittingly and others wittingly.

When I bought this expensive 1,200 page book, I did so because I wanted to understand what Zionism actually is. I listened to the author speak on a few podcasts and some people have a way of getting through to me with their logic and reasoning. He was definitely one of them.

The author spends the first several hundred pages explaining Judaism and its relationship to the Torah. I find myself unexpectedly fascinated by much of what the author has said so far, particularly the idea of a decentralized people living in accordance with shared commandments rather than a conventional national identity. That, to me, raises an obvious tension. If someone is patriotic and believes strongly in national identity, how does that square with a Torah centered worldview, especially when Torah abiding people hold positions of power and feel an obligation to act in the interest of Klal Yisroel, meaning the collective people of Israel?

It is my hope that by the end of this book, I will be better able to discern the rhetoric I hear in the media and elsewhere, and to distinguish more clearly between Judaism, Zionism, and everything in between.
