# Michael Yon: Morocco, Ceuta, and Resource Geography Reference

*Primary source documentation, from a video transcript dated by Yon to August 1, 2026, recorded following a reported trip to Morocco with Chuck Holton. This entry documents what Yon states in this transcript; it is not a synthesis, comparison, or evaluation. Where his reasoning is not fully articulated in the source, that gap is noted rather than filled.*

---

## Funding and Support Model

Yon states his Substack content is free and not paywalled, noting that on a few occasions content was accidentally placed behind a paywall due to a platform setting and that this was not intentional. He describes accepting support via multiple channels (checks, PO box, wire transfer, Venmo, Cash App, Gifts and Go, mailed precious metals) and offers paid phone consultations priced at one ounce of gold or its cash equivalent. He states he needs "the money" and "the time" to continue operating, and describes a personal email-triage system: he does not answer general email in mornings (reserved for higher-level work), asks correspondents to write "consultation" in the subject line only if genuinely requesting a paid consultation, and states he blocks people who misuse that subject line or send lengthy unrelated messages, citing signal-to-noise concerns. He states he received roughly 25,000 emails during his time covering the Iraq War that he was unable to open.

## Ceuta and the Strait of Gibraltar

**Observation:** Yon reports recent (as of the transcript date) large-scale crossing attempts into Ceuta, a Spanish enclave in Morocco, and states he and Chuck Holton visited the Ceuta border and live-streamed from the area. He also references a second, more distant Spanish enclave, Melilla.

**Historical claims, as stated:**
- Portuguese forces attacked and took Ceuta in August 1415, which Yon frames as marking the beginning of what he calls "maritime globalism."
- Prince Henry the Navigator is named as a key leader of that action and later a central figure in the development of navigational technology.
- Yon states Ceuta remained under Portuguese control for approximately 225 years, followed by an intervening period, before becoming fully Spanish; he does not give exact transition dates in the transcript.

**Yon's interpretation:** Ceuta is strategically important primarily because of its position controlling access to the Strait of Gibraltar, which he describes as one of the major maritime chokepoints in the world. He states Spain "is clearly under attack and has been for a long time," without further elaboration in this transcript of what form that attack takes beyond the migration discussion below.

## The Benzion Netanyahu Reference

Yon references a book he states was written by Benzion Netanyahu (identified as Benjamin Netanyahu's father), which he says contains a short section discussing Ceuta ("Suta") and Malaga.

Yon states that the Netanyahu family's original surname was "Mileikowsky" (rendered phonetically in the transcript as "Meakovski"), changed to Netanyahu in the 1920s. He draws an explicit parallel to Argentine President Javier Milei, stating his surname is "the same" origin as the pre-change Netanyahu family name.

*Note on the source: Yon does not state what conclusion or significance he draws from this naming parallel within this transcript — he raises the comparison and moves on without further elaboration. This is presented here as stated, without inference beyond what he says.*

## Morocco: Political and Demographic Context

Yon states Morocco was the first country to formally recognize the United States, and describes a long history of positive US-Morocco relations dating to the founding of the United States. He states Moroccans he has spoken with are frequently surprised Americans are unaware of this history.

He describes Morocco as having a small historical Catholic community, a smaller Protestant community, and a Jewish population present for a long time, noting this reflected in Morocco's flag, alongside a present-day Muslim majority. He frames this religious history as something that "comes and goes" over time.

---

## Language Policy as "Language Warfare"

**Observation:** Yon states that during his Morocco trip, he and Chuck Holton noticed road signs using a Berber-language script alongside Arabic (Arabic being, per Yon, the predominant language), and that neither he nor Holton — despite extensive travel — recognized the script, which Holton compared to a "Klingon alphabet." Yon states this script was introduced around 2015.

**Yon's interpretation:** He identifies this immediately as an instance of what he calls "information war" or "language war," arguing that language standardization or introduction of new scripts has historically (he says "since at least the 1800s") been used as a tool to divide populations.

**Analogy given:** Yon compares this to his own observations among Maoist groups in Nepal, whom he states pursued a deliberate strategy of promoting distinct, separate languages village-by-village as a divide-and-rule tactic.

## Morocco and Panama as Comparative Cases

Yon argues Morocco would be strategically significant even without its position on the Strait of Gibraltar, because of its resource base — and separately argues the same logic applies to Panama, which he states would be a "very valuable" country due to large copper, gold, and lumber reserves even setting aside its canal/maritime position.

## Phosphate as a Strategic Resource

This is one of the most developed themes in the transcript.

**Core claim:** Yon states Morocco holds approximately 70% of global phosphate reserves. He connects phosphate directly to NPK fertilizer production (nitrogen, phosphorus/phosphate, potassium), arguing phosphate is "absolutely vital" to agriculture and should be understood as a strategic resource on par with oil.

**Personal/biographical framing:** Yon states he is from Polk County, Florida, which he describes as the largest phosphate-producing county in the United States, as part of a four-county phosphate region (naming Hardee, Hillsborough, Manatee, and Polk counties). He describes large-scale dragline phosphate mining operations there from personal observation/childhood experience (finding fossils and sharks' teeth in mining spoil).

**Global comparison, as stated:** Yon states China is also a major phosphate producer but has stopped exporting its reserves, which he interprets as a deliberate strategic move anticipating phosphate's future importance. He states the United States has enough phosphate reserves for domestic use for "another couple generations" if it does not export, while explicitly noting his own uncertainty about the reliability of published reserve figures ("I can't trust any of the reports").

**Uneven reporting claim:** Yon states that standard published lists of top global phosphate reserve-holders (which he says typically list Morocco, Tunisia, Algeria, and China) usually omit Iraq, despite his own view (below) that Iraq holds substantial reserves.

## Iraq as a Phosphate Case Study

Yon draws on his own experience serving in Iraq to argue that Iraq possesses very large phosphate reserves/resources — by his account, larger than the United States' — and distinguishes between "reserves" and "resources" as a technical distinction without fully defining it in this transcript. He suggests more precise figures may exist but be held on classified ("secret") maps.

**Historical claim:** Yon states Iraq was previously a major phosphate and fertilizer exporter, and that this export capacity was progressively eliminated by the 1991 Gulf War, the 2003 war, and subsequent conflict and economic measures, to the point that (per Yon) Iraq now exports essentially none.

**Yon's interpretation, connecting this to the broader framework:** he states that where he understands proposed "Greater Israel" border claims to extend inside Iraq, this territory would overlap with Iraq's phosphate deposits. He states directly that both "the Zionist" and China are competing over Iraq's resources, and states — presented as a personal realization rather than a sourced claim — that he now believes US military presence in Iraq served "greater Israel" interests, connecting this to broader operations against Iran within what he calls "a larger global context, a global war."

## "Routes and Resources": Yon's Framing Principle

Yon uses the phrase "routes and resources" repeatedly as a summary label for how he believes conflicts should be understood — not as disputes over particular places for their own sake, but as competition over maritime routes, transportation corridors, and strategic resources (fertilizer, energy, minerals, agricultural capacity).

He extends this framing to a list of countries he argues are being contested on this basis: Morocco, Panama, Argentina, Brazil, Thailand, Iraq, Turkey, Egypt, and the United States, stating this is "not any place in particular" but a "global" pattern.

---

## Named Competing Actors: "The Zionist" and China

Yon repeatedly frames global resource/route competition as occurring primarily between two actors he names directly: "the Zionist" and China, stating both are competing for Morocco "100%," and applying the same framing to Argentina, Panama, Thailand, and the United States.

He uses the phrase "Zionist trolls in the basement over in Tel Aviv" (alongside a parallel reference to "Chinese trolls") when explaining why he restricts commenting privileges on his Substack, framing this as a way of limiting hostile commentary from these actors.

*Note on the source: the transcript does not further specify the composition, structure, or leadership of "the Zionist" as an actor beyond these references.*

## Migration as a Strategic Instrument

Yon argues that the migration pressure on Ceuta reported in the news at the time of this transcript is not a new development, stating he observed similar activity ("migrants... already being used") at the Ceuta border five years prior to this recording. He argues migrants are being "used" strategically rather than acting independently of any coordinating influence, though the transcript does not specify by whom in this passage.

He draws a direct comparison to the US southern border, describing sustained, large-scale crossings there over multiple years, and states he disputes the accuracy of claims (which he attributes to Donald Trump) that the border is no longer being crossed in significant numbers — citing a secondhand account from Chuck Holton's recent observations in San Diego.

## Predictions and Yon's Stated Confidence Levels

This transcript is notable for including explicit statements from Yon about his own evidentiary basis for different predictions — he does not present all predictions as equally supported, and distinguishes between them directly.

**Predictions Yon connects to prior specific evidence:**
- He references a past prediction regarding New World screwworm reaching the United States via Panama, which he states had significant advance value ("could have been worth billions of dollars" to those who acted on it), though he does not specify the evidentiary basis for that particular prediction in this transcript.
- He references a past Nord Stream pipeline prediction, for which he states there was "pretty serious evidence" in the form of prior US government statements about Nord Stream — explicitly distinguishing this from his lower-confidence predictions below.

**Predictions Yon explicitly labels as low-evidence or intuitive:**
- A predicted future conflict between Argentina and Brazil, including a claim that Argentina will retake the Falkland Islands ("Falllands") before that conflict. Yon states directly: "I have no proof of it," "I really don't have much evidence," and that the evidence he does have "could be written on one sheet of paper... with a few sentences." He describes this as an intuitive feeling ("I'm starting to feel it on the spiderweb") rather than an evidence-based forecast, and explicitly compares its evidentiary weakness to the (in his account, similarly non-evidence-based) screwworm and Nord Stream predictions at the time he first made them.

**Additional predictions, presented without a stated evidentiary basis in this transcript:**
- Possible future damage to or attacks on: the Mosul Dam, the Aswan Dam (Egypt), the Three Gorges Dam (China), and US water infrastructure generally.
- Continued or expanded conflict at additional maritime chokepoints: the Strait of Malacca (which he states will "almost certainly" involve "the Zionist"), the Sunda and Lombok Straits, and the Danish straits (which he suggests Russia "may" target).
- Continued closure of the Strait of Hormuz, which he states he expects to remain closed ("in my view").
- Continued or renewed closure of the Suez Canal, in combination with Hormuz, affecting fertilizer shipments to Asia (see below).

**Biographical basis offered for the dam-related predictions specifically:** Yon states he received sabotage training in the US Army Special Forces at Fort Bragg around age 19–20, including training addressing dams, and states that destroying one dam can cause cascading ("domino") failures in dams downstream. He presents this training background as informing (though not directly evidencing) his dam-related predictions.

## Depopulation and Global Food Supply as the Organizing Framework

Yon connects the preceding material into a single overarching claim: that global fertilizer/food production has been deliberately and gradually constrained over a long period as part of an intentional effort toward global depopulation.

He states this process "takes a long time to get it all cranked up" and that "it's taking a long time to turn out the lights" for those he says are pursuing global famine, framing the Iraq phosphate export elimination (see above) as one component of this longer-term pattern.

He extends this to Japan specifically (his location at the time of recording), stating Japan depends on phosphate imports, and expresses concern that closures at Suez and Hormuz, combined with a reduced ability to route shipments around Africa (which he suggests may also eventually be disrupted), would constrain fertilizer availability across Asia.

**Direct statement of the framework's priority:** Yon states plainly, "this is about depopulation. This is about global depopulation," and explicitly rejects alternative framings — he describes discussion of "tit for tat" escalation, "memorandums of understanding," or peace talks as "nonsense," stating he does not treat such coverage as informative except to monitor "what they're saying" as part of what he calls "the information game."

**Stated overarching goal beyond depopulation itself:** Yon frames the endpoint of this process as "a fight over who controls the remnant" — i.e., control over the surviving population after depopulation occurs — consistent with language used in prior Yon material documented separately in this knowledge base.

**List of historical/ongoing chokepoint conflicts, as stated:** Yon frames the Strait of Gibraltar, the Suez Canal (since 1869), the Strait of Hormuz, and the Bab-el-Mandeb Strait as long-standing sites of conflict, extending this designation to the Strait of Malacca, the Sunda and Lombok Straits, and the Danish straits as current or future sites of the same pattern.

## Core Analytical Framework

As presented in this transcript, Yon's interpretation of geopolitical events adds specificity to the framework documented in prior entries:

1. **Strategic geography and critical resources are treated as the primary units of analysis**, rather than nations, ideologies, or individual political disputes. Conflicts are read through the lens of chokepoint control (straits, canals) and resource control (phosphate, copper, energy), summarized in his own phrase "routes and resources."
2. **A consistent two-actor competitive structure** — "the Zionist" and China — is applied across multiple, otherwise unconnected regions (Morocco, Iraq, Argentina, Panama, Thailand, the United States), treating disparate local events as instances of the same global contest.
3. **Yon distinguishes explicitly between his own high- and low-confidence claims.** This transcript is notable for Yon directly stating that some predictions (Nord Stream) were grounded in specific external evidence (government statements), while others (Argentina-Brazil conflict) are explicitly acknowledged by him as intuition without objective evidentiary support. This self-graded distinction is presented by Yon himself, not inferred.
4. **Personal biography and firsthand experience are used as interpretive grounding** — his phosphate-region upbringing, his Iraq deployment, and his Special Forces sabotage training are each offered as the basis for specific claims (Iraq's phosphate significance, dam vulnerability), distinguishing these from claims he does not connect to personal experience.
5. **Peace processes, diplomatic statements, and "tit for tat" framings are treated as noise or misdirection** within an underlying condition of total, continuous war, consistent with material documented elsewhere.
6. **Depopulation remains the top-level explanatory goal**, with this transcript specifically developing the food-supply/fertilizer mechanism (alongside the energy mechanism documented previously) as a means toward that end, and introducing "controlling the remnant" as the stated subsequent objective once depopulation occurs.

One additional note on this transcript specifically, offered as description rather than external comparison: this source is more explicit than the previously documented transcript in directly naming "the Zionist" as a hostile actor in personal/dismissive terms (e.g., "Zionist trolls in the basement over in Tel Aviv") and in raising an unelaborated insinuation about a public figure's family name change (the Netanyahu/Mileikowsky reference, paralleled to Milei) without stating what conclusion he draws from it. Both are documented above as Yon's own stated words, consistent with the rest of this entry's sourcing standard.
