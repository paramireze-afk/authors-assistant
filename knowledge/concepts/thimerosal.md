# Thimerosal (Thiomersal / Merthiolate)

> **Method note:** This document reconstructs the case against thimerosal as built by [[Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] and a defined set of researchers, physicians, journalists, and whistleblowers who have made that case in their own primary work — papers, books, congressional testimony, FOIA-obtained transcripts, and court filings. Claims are attributed to the person or document making them. A short editorial note on current scientific status appears at the end, consistent with this knowledge base's practice elsewhere (see [[Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] → Scientific Controversies and [[AZT (Zidovudine)]] → Editorial Note).

## Overview

Thimerosal is an ethylmercury-based preservative (approximately 49.6% mercury by weight) used since the 1930s to prevent bacterial and fungal contamination in multi-dose vaccine vials. It was developed and patented by [[Eli Lilly]] and marketed under the trade name Merthiolate. Between 1999 and 2001, thimerosal was removed or reduced to trace amounts in nearly all vaccines on the routine U.S. childhood schedule as a precautionary measure; it remains in some multi-dose influenza vaccines and in vaccines distributed through the WHO's Expanded Programme on Immunization in parts of the developing world. The removal decision itself — and the scientific and institutional record surrounding it — is the central factual battleground this document maps, reconstructed from the people who have argued most forcefully that thimerosal caused a measurable rise in neurodevelopmental disorders and that this connection was mismanaged, minimized, or concealed by federal health agencies.

---

## The Central Thesis (Shared Across Sources)

Stripped to its core, the argument common to every source in this document runs:

1. The U.S. childhood immunization schedule expanded substantially during the 1990s (notably with the addition of the Hepatitis B and Hib vaccines), without anyone totaling the cumulative mercury dose a fully vaccinated infant would receive from thimerosal across all those shots.
2. When that cumulative dose was calculated in 1999 (prompted by the FDA Modernization Act's requirement that FDA audit mercury content across all regulated products), it was found to exceed at least one federal mercury-exposure guideline (the EPA's reference dose) for some infants in the first six months of life — a fact publicly acknowledged in the July 1999 joint statement by the [[American Academy of Pediatrics]] and the U.S. Public Health Service that first called for thimerosal's removal.
3. Ethylmercury, like other organic mercury compounds, is a known neurotoxicant; critics argue regulators wrongly assumed its safety based on data for a different compound, methylmercury, with different — and, they argue, understudied — pharmacokinetics in infants.
4. Internal CDC epidemiological analysis conducted in 1999–2000 (the Verstraeten Vaccine Safety Datalink study) initially showed statistically elevated relative risks for neurodevelopmental disorders, including autism, at higher cumulative thimerosal exposure — a finding discussed at a closed-door 2000 meeting at the Simpsonwood Conference Center before later re-analyses reduced or eliminated the signal.
5. A subset of children, these sources argue, are genetically or metabolically less able to excrete mercury (via impaired glutathione/methylation pathways), making population-wide epidemiology poorly suited to detect a real effect concentrated in a vulnerable minority.
6. The removal of thimerosal from most vaccines by 2001 is treated by these sources as a tacit admission of risk, even though public health agencies characterized it as precautionary rather than an acknowledgment of harm.

## Foundational Primary Documents

### The Simpsonwood Transcript (June 7–8, 2000)
A two-day, closed-door meeting — officially titled "Scientific Review of Vaccine Safety Datalink Information" — convened by the CDC at the Simpsonwood Retreat and Conference Center in Norcross, Georgia, chaired by CDC National Immunization Program director [[Walter Orenstein]]. Approximately 50–52 participants attended, drawn from the CDC, FDA, NIH, WHO, vaccine manufacturers, HMOs, and independent academic experts. The meeting reviewed CDC epidemiologist [[Thomas Verstraeten]]'s Phase I Vaccine Safety Datalink (VSD) analysis, which showed elevated relative risks for several neurodevelopmental outcomes — including a relative risk of 11.35 for autism in the highest-exposure quartile, based on a small number of cases (roughly 40). The transcript was stamped "Do Not Copy or Release" and "Confidential." [[Lyn Redwood]] (a nurse practitioner and later co-founder of [[SafeMinds]]) has described filing the FOIA request that eventually surfaced the transcript publicly, and has recounted reading it through the night and being alarmed by the tenor of the discussion, which she and others characterize as more focused on liability, public confidence, and communications strategy than on the underlying data. Kennedy, drawing on this same transcript in "Deadly Immunity" (2005) and later in *[[The Real Anthony Fauci]]*, alleges that in the months and years following Simpsonwood, the CDC replaced Verstraeten with a team that reworked the analysis using what Kennedy calls "dodgy" statistical adjustments until the signal disappeared from the final published study.

### The Verstraeten Studies: Phase I to Publication (1999–2003)
Verstraeten's VSD analysis went through multiple iterations before its eventual publication in *Pediatrics* in 2003, by which point Verstraeten had left the CDC for a position at [[GlaxoSmithKline]] — a fact Kennedy and other critics highlight as a conflict-of-interest concern, while Verstraeten himself has stated in *Pediatrics* correspondence that the change in results reflected the addition of further HMO data and refined statistical methods, not manipulation. Successive re-analyses, incorporating data from additional health maintenance organizations, progressively weakened and ultimately eliminated the statistically significant associations seen in the earliest phase. Critics (Kennedy, SafeMinds, [[Boyd Haley]], and the Geiers among them) argue the added HMO data diluted a real signal; CDC and Verstraeten's own published account attribute the change to correcting confounding and small-sample instability in the original analysis.

### The 1999 AAP/PHS Joint Statement
On July 7, 1999, the American Academy of Pediatrics and the U.S. Public Health Service issued a joint statement recommending the removal or reduction of thimerosal from childhood vaccines "as soon as possible" as a precautionary measure, explicitly citing the cumulative-dose calculation described above. This document is treated by every source in this corpus as the trigger event of the controversy — the moment official public health authorities themselves acknowledged the exposure problem, even while maintaining no causal link to any specific disorder had been established.

### Institute of Medicine Reports (2001 and 2004)
- The IOM's 2001 report, *Immunization Safety Review: Thimerosal–Containing Vaccines and Neurodevelopmental Disorders*, concluded the hypothesis was biologically plausible but that evidence was insufficient to accept or reject a causal relationship, and called for further research.
- The IOM's 2004 report, *Immunization Safety Review: Vaccines and Autism*, reviewed additional epidemiological studies (including Danish, UK, and Swedish population data) and concluded the evidence favored rejecting a causal relationship between thimerosal-containing vaccines and autism, further recommending that federal research resources not prioritize further study of the vaccine-autism hypothesis. Kennedy, SafeMinds, and Mark Blaxill have criticized this 2004 conclusion as a premature and policy-driven closure of the question rather than a scientifically compelled one, arguing the underlying studies (particularly the Danish registry data — see below) had significant methodological limitations that the IOM committee did not adequately weigh.

### SafeMinds' Founding Paper (2001)
Sallie Bernard, Albert Enayati, Lyn Redwood, Heidi Roger, and Teresa Binstock published "Autism: A Novel Form of Mercury Poisoning" in *Medical Hypotheses* (2001), the foundational document of the modern thimerosal-autism hypothesis. The paper reviews the clinical, biochemical, and neuroanatomical overlap between known mercury-poisoning syndromes (including acrodynia, or "Pink disease," a 20th-century mercury-exposure illness in children) and autism spectrum symptoms, and argues that a review of medical literature and government exposure data suggests a subset of autism cases may represent an unrecognized form of mercury toxicity. Bernard, Redwood, and colleagues subsequently co-founded [[SafeMinds]] as an advocacy and research organization built around this hypothesis.

## Key Figures and Their Primary Works

### Robert F. Kennedy Jr.
See full profile: [[Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]]. Thimerosal-specific works: "Deadly Immunity" (2005, *Rolling Stone*/*Salon*, later retracted by Salon in 2011); *[[Thimerosal: Let the Science Speak]]* (2014, editor); relevant chapters of *[[The Real Anthony Fauci]]* (2021); founding chairmanship of the [[World Mercury Project]] (2016), later renamed [[Children's Health Defense]].

### SafeMinds (Sallie Bernard, Lyn Redwood, Mark Blaxill, and others)
Advocacy and research organization founded in the early 2000s by parents and professionals following the 2001 *Medical Hypotheses* paper. Bernard is a co-author of that founding paper. Redwood, a nurse practitioner and mother of a child she attributes to thimerosal exposure, has given repeated congressional testimony and led the FOIA effort that surfaced the Simpsonwood transcript. Blaxill, an engineer by training, authored SafeMinds' methodological critiques of subsequent epidemiological studies — including "What's Going On? The Question of Time Trends in Autism" (*Public Health Reports*, 2004) and "Study Fails to Establish Diagnostic Substitution as a Factor in Increased Rate of Autism" (*Pharmacotherapy*, 2004) — and, with journalist Dan Olmsted, later co-authored *The Age of Autism: Mercury, Medicine, and a Man-Made Epidemic* (2010).

### Mark Geier and David Geier
Father-son team who published a series of papers from the mid-2000s onward (using VAERS and VSD data) arguing for a statistical association between thimerosal exposure and autism and other neurodevelopmental outcomes, cited extensively in vaccine-injury litigation (see *Blackwell v. Wyeth*, 408 Md. 575 (2009), which lists their publications). Mark Geier additionally developed and patented a treatment protocol combining chelation therapy with Lupron (a hormone-suppressing drug), based on his hypothesis that testosterone potentiates mercury's neurotoxicity and that lowering testosterone would make chelation more effective in autistic children. Following a multi-year investigation (complaint filed 2006), the Maryland Board of Physicians summarily suspended Mark Geier's medical license in April 2011, finding — after eleven days of hearings and a 126-page proposed decision — that he had misdiagnosed precocious puberty in autistic children to justify Lupron treatment, failed to obtain adequate informed consent, and practiced outside the standard of care; the Board formally revoked his license in August 2012, a decision upheld on judicial review in 2014. His license was subsequently suspended or revoked in every other state in which he was licensed. David Geier, who does not hold a medical degree, was separately charged in Maryland with practicing medicine without a license in connection with the same clinic operations. Mark Geier died in early 2025. **In March 2025, HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. appointed David Geier as a senior data analyst at HHS to review CDC Vaccine Safety Datalink data as part of a federally directed study of vaccines and autism** — see [[#From Historical Dispute to Federal Policy (2025–2026)]] below.

### Boyd Haley
Emeritus professor and former chair of chemistry, University of Kentucky. Haley's laboratory research (with colleague Murray Vimy) documented that mercury vapor exposure in animal models causes depolymerization of brain tubulin — a mechanism he argued was relevant to both Alzheimer's disease and, later, autism, given thimerosal's status as an organic mercury compound. He gave congressional and IOM testimony (including testimony before a House committee in 2001 on synergistic toxicity between dental-amalgam mercury vapor and thimerosal-derived ethylmercury) and served as a designated expert witness for petitioners in the Omnibus Autism Proceeding (see below). He later founded CTI Science and developed a chelating compound, OSR#1, marketed as a dietary supplement; the FDA issued a warning letter in June 2010 stating it was an unapproved drug with inadequately disclosed safety data, and the product was withdrawn from the U.S. market that July.

### David Kirby
Journalist; author of *Evidence of Harm: Mercury in Vaccines and the Autism Epidemic — A Medical Controversy* (2005), a book-length journalistic account following several families and the SafeMinds-affiliated researchers through the early 2000s controversy. Kirby was also among the first journalists to obtain and publish the government's concession document in the Hannah Poling case (see below), reporting it at *The Huffington Post* in 2008.

### Sharyl Attkisson
Investigative journalist, then with CBS News, who reported extensively on vaccine-safety and VICP proceedings during the 2000s, including early coverage of the Hannah Poling case. She has described in her own later public statements and writing that she encountered internal resistance at CBS to continued coverage of vaccine-safety stories, which she cites as a factor in her eventual departure from the network.

### William Thompson
CDC epidemiologist who, in 2014, stated through his attorney and in recorded conversations with biochemical engineer [[Brian Hooker]] that he and CDC co-authors had omitted statistically significant findings from a 2004 published study (primarily concerning the MMR vaccine and timing of autism diagnosis in African American boys, a related but distinct strand of vaccine-safety controversy from thimerosal specifically). Thompson's broader body of CDC work included co-authorship of thimerosal-focused studies, including a 2007 *New England Journal of Medicine* paper on early thimerosal exposure and neuropsychological outcomes. Kennedy cites Thompson's statements in *The Real Anthony Fauci* as evidence of a broader pattern of selective data reporting within CDC's Immunization Safety Office.

### Neal Halsey
Pediatrician and vaccine researcher, then director of the Johns Hopkins Institute for Vaccine Safety, who — while not endorsing an autism causal link — was among the most prominent physician-scientists to sound an early alarm about cumulative thimerosal exposure once the FDA's 1999 mercury audit results became known, and pressed publicly and within professional pediatric circles for the precautionary removal that followed. His inclusion here reflects his role as a primary, non-fringe voice raising concern about thimerosal exposure levels, distinct from the causal claims made by other sources in this document.

### S. Jill James and Richard Deth
Biochemical researchers whose published work (James on oxidative stress and glutathione metabolism; Deth on methylation pathways and dopamine signaling) is cited by Kennedy, SafeMinds, and the Geiers as supplying a plausible mechanism by which a genetically or metabolically vulnerable subset of children could be harmed by thimerosal exposure at doses tolerated by the general population — addressing a standard critique that population-level epidemiology would fail to detect an effect concentrated in a minority.

## Mechanism Arguments

- **Tubulin/cytoskeletal disruption** (Haley): mercury's high affinity for sulfhydryl (thiol) groups on structural brain proteins, demonstrated in animal models for elemental mercury vapor and argued by extension to apply to ethylmercury from thimerosal.
- **Oxidative stress and impaired methylation/transsulfuration** (James, Deth): a proposed biochemical vulnerability, more pronounced in some children, that would impair the body's ability to bind and excrete mercury via glutathione conjugation, and that critics argue is measurable via metabolic biomarkers rather than population-average epidemiology.
- **Testosterone potentiation / estrogen protection** (Mark Geier): the hypothesis — later the basis of his Lupron protocol and the proximate cause of his license revocation — that androgens increase mercury's neurotoxic effect and that suppressing testosterone could mitigate it.
- **Ethylmercury vs. methylmercury pharmacokinetics**: critics argue regulatory bodies initially applied methylmercury safety thresholds (derived largely from dietary fish-exposure data) to ethylmercury without adequate independent pharmacokinetic study, and dispute how much weight should be given to later primate studies (e.g., Burbacher et al., 2005) showing faster blood clearance of ethylmercury, on the grounds that faster blood clearance does not resolve questions about conversion to inorganic mercury and retention in brain tissue.

## Court Records

### The Omnibus Autism Proceeding (2002–2010)
A consolidated set of proceedings before the U.S. Court of Federal Claims (the "vaccine court," operating under the [[National Childhood Vaccine Injury Act]] of 1986) to resolve roughly 5,000 pending claims alleging vaccines — thimerosal-containing vaccines and/or the MMR vaccine — caused autism. Special masters heard extensive expert testimony (including from Boyd Haley and the Geiers as petitioners' experts) across three test theories in 2007–2009 and ultimately ruled against causation in all test cases decided within the omnibus framework, finding petitioners' expert evidence unpersuasive relative to the government's experts and the broader epidemiological record.

### The Hannah Poling Case (2007–2008)
Outside the omnibus test-case framework, the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services conceded, in a November 2007 filing (made public in early 2008 and first reported by David Kirby), that vaccines Hannah Poling received at 19 months "significantly aggravated an underlying mitochondrial disorder, which predisposed her to deficits in cellular energy metabolism, and manifested as a regressive encephalopathy with features of autism spectrum disorder." The family was later awarded compensation estimated at $1.5–20 million over her lifetime. Kennedy and other critics cite this as the government's own concession, in a specific documented case, that vaccination can precipitate autism symptoms in a susceptible child — while HHS and CDC officials at the time, including then-CDC director [[Julie Gerberding]], stated publicly that the concession applied narrowly to Hannah Poling's specific mitochondrial condition and should not be read as a general finding on vaccines and autism.

### Geier Medical Board Proceedings
See Mark Geier entry above; the Maryland Board of Physicians' findings (2011–2012) and the subsequent appellate record (*Geier v. Maryland State Board of Physicians*, 2015; *Maryland Board of Physicians v. Geier*, 2017) are treated here as primary government/court documents relevant to evaluating the Geiers' body of published thimerosal-autism research and its real-world clinical application.

## Congressional Testimony and Hearings

Representative [[Dan Burton]] (R-IN), then chairman of the House Committee on Government Reform, held a series of hearings under the title "Mercury in Medicine" between 2000 and 2003, in which Boyd Haley, SafeMinds-affiliated researchers, and CDC and FDA officials testified. Burton, whose grandson was diagnosed with autism following vaccination according to his own public statements, used the hearings to press CDC and FDA officials on the Verstraeten data, the FDA's 1999 mercury audit, and the pace of thimerosal removal. These hearings are cited by Kennedy and SafeMinds as an early instance of elected oversight pressing the same concerns later raised via FOIA and litigation.

## The Danish Registry Dispute

A recurring point of direct methodological conflict: Denmark removed thimerosal from its vaccines earlier than the U.S. (largely by 1992), and subsequent Danish national registry studies (notably Madsen et al., 2003, in *Pediatrics*) found autism diagnosis rates continued rising after thimerosal's removal — a finding widely cited by health agencies as strong evidence against the thimerosal-autism hypothesis. Sallie Bernard, in a SafeMinds analysis ("Analysis of the Danish Autism Registry Database in Response to the Hviid et al. Paper on Thimerosal in JAMA," 2003), disputed this reading, arguing that changes in Danish autism-diagnosis practices during the study period — specifically, a shift from counting only inpatient psychiatric diagnoses to also counting outpatient clinic diagnoses starting in 1995 — artificially inflated the apparent post-thimerosal autism trend and masked any real decline. This dispute over Danish registry methodology remains a central point of disagreement between the sources in this document and the epidemiological studies most often cited against them.

## From Historical Dispute to Federal Policy (2025–2026)

This section documents the direct institutional continuity between the historical thimerosal-autism dispute and current U.S. federal health policy, based on primary government and public records:

- **March 2025:** HHS, under Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., hired David Geier as a senior data analyst, reportedly to review CDC Vaccine Safety Datalink data as part of a directed federal study into vaccines and autism.
- **September 4, 2025:** At a Senate Finance Committee hearing, Kennedy stated that Geier's study protocols were public; as of an April 2026 follow-up letter from Senator Ben Ray Luján, those protocols had not been produced to Congress or identified in the public domain.
- **September 8, 2025:** President Trump posted a video to social media featuring Mark and David Geier discussing thimerosal and vaccine-autism claims dating from earlier in their careers; a White House official characterized the video as focused on thimerosal safety.
- **2025–2026, ongoing:** HHS's broader restructuring of [[ACIP]] and revision of the childhood vaccine schedule (documented in the [[Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] profile's HHS Secretary Tenure section) proceeds in parallel with the Geier-led data review, representing the first instance of the thimerosal-autism hypothesis being investigated with the institutional authority and data access of the federal government itself, rather than by outside petitioners seeking access via FOIA or court discovery as in the 2000s.

This section is included because it is a documented, primary-record continuation of the exact dispute chronicled above — the same VSD dataset fought over at Simpsonwood in 2000 and via FOIA in the mid-2000s is, as of this writing, being reviewed again by one of the original thimerosal-autism hypothesis's most prominent (and most sanctioned) proponents, under direct federal authority.

## Recurring Historical Patterns

- **Regulatory capture and revolving-door concerns**: Verstraeten's move to GlaxoSmithKline following his CDC analysis is treated by critics as emblematic of the broader [[Regulatory Capture]] dynamic RFK Jr. develops across his body of work.
- **Closed-door scientific review followed by FOIA disclosure**: the Simpsonwood pattern (confidential meeting → later public disclosure via FOIA → allegations of a gap between private and public framing) recurs in Kennedy's account of NIH and NIAID conduct in *The Real Anthony Fauci* and *The Wuhan Cover-Up*.
- **Professional and regulatory consequences for dissenting researchers**: Haley's FDA warning letter, the Geiers' license revocations, and Wakefield-era GMC proceedings (outside this document's direct scope but frequently cross-referenced by these sources) are treated by Kennedy and SafeMinds as a chilling pattern; medical boards and regulators frame the same actions as necessary enforcement against unsafe or fraudulent practice.
- **The precautionary-removal-as-admission argument**: this pattern — treating a regulatory agency's voluntary removal of a substance as evidence of concealed harm rather than genuine precaution — recurs across Kennedy's environmental and pharmaceutical writing (e.g., his treatment of DDT and PCB removals in his earlier environmental-law career).

## Editorial Note on Scientific Status

*(Included per this knowledge base's general practice — see the parent [[Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] document and [[AZT (Zidovudine)]] for the same approach.)*

This document faithfully reconstructs what these specific individuals and organizations argued, in their own words, papers, and cited government records. It should be read as documentation of a historical and ongoing dispute, not as a summary of current scientific consensus. Within epidemiology and toxicology, the causal thimerosal-autism hypothesis is considered extensively studied and not supported: autism prevalence has continued to rise in multiple countries (including the U.S. and Denmark) after thimerosal's removal from routine schedules, a trend regarded as strong evidence against a causal role, though critics represented in this document (particularly Bernard's Danish-registry analysis) dispute the comparability of pre- and post-removal diagnostic counting in at least one of the key studies cited for this conclusion. Separately, several individuals central to this document's source record — most notably Mark Geier — faced documented, adjudicated regulatory and legal consequences (medical license revocation, findings of inadequate informed consent and misdiagnosis) connected directly to clinical treatments built on the thimerosal-mercury hypothesis; these are matters of public board and court record, not press characterization, and are included for factual completeness given their direct relevance to evaluating the associated body of research. As of this writing, aspects of the historical dispute are being reopened at the federal regulatory level under the current HHS Secretary, making this an active rather than purely historical topic.
