# Charlie Kirk's Necklace: Research Reference

## What This Document Is

This is a research reference on the necklace Charlie Kirk wore on the day of his assassination, September 10, 2025. The document explains what investigators have observed about the necklace's behavior, documents the mutually contradictory official accounts of how it was removed, and presents the competing theories about its role in the incident. Claims are sourced where possible and labeled by evidentiary status where contested.

## Introduction: Why the Necklace Matters

Charlie Kirk wore a necklace on the day he was killed. That fact would be unremarkable except for what happened to it in the first fraction of a second of the incident, and what happened to the official story about it in the days and weeks that followed.

The necklace has become one of the most contested pieces of physical evidence in the entire investigation. Investigators examining high-resolution video footage argue that the necklace's behavior at the moment of impact is physically inconsistent with a rifle shot to the neck. Multiple conflicting official accounts have been given about how and where the necklace was removed. And the necklace itself, which investigators argue should have been tested for explosive residue as a matter of basic forensic procedure, appears never to have been subjected to independent analysis.

## Physical Description and Ownership

### The Necklace

Charlie Kirk wore a rope-style chain necklace, described by investigators as thick rather than thin, approximately 26 to 30 inches in length. The necklace carried multiple pendants, including:

- A cross
- A St. Michael the Archangel medal
- A third charm that has not been definitively identified in publicly available footage

The necklace was a regular part of Kirk's personal presentation. Multiple accounts describe him as rarely or never removing it.

### Chain of Custody (Pre-Incident)

Erika Kirk, Charlie's wife, reportedly had possession of the necklace the night before the assassination. This detail is noted by investigators primarily because it establishes a window during which the necklace could theoretically have been accessed or modified, though no evidence of substitution has been publicly presented. [12]

## What the Video Footage Shows

This is the core of the investigative dispute. Multiple researchers have independently analyzed high-resolution video of the moment Kirk was struck, and their findings are broadly consistent with each other even where their conclusions differ.

### The Necklace Moves Before Any Visible Wound

Researcher Blake Bednarz, analyzing high-quality footage, argues that the necklace begins moving before any visible wound appears, which he interprets as inconsistent with a bullet impact to the neck being the initiating event. [1]

### The Necklace Travels Over Kirk's Head

Multiple researchers describe the necklace snapping and traveling upward and over Kirk's head with what they characterize as extreme force. [6] Baron Coleman notes that the footage shows the chain swinging wildly overhead with a long section of chain airborne, suggesting the break point was not at the clasp but somewhere along the chain itself. [6]

### The Break Point Is Not at the Neck

This is a key technical claim. Coleman's analysis, based on measuring the visible chain length from the back of Kirk's neck, places the break point in the middle of the chest rather than at the neck where the wound appeared. [4] If accurate, this is significant: a bullet impact to the neck would not be expected to snap a chain at mid-chest.

### The Necklace Is the Fastest-Moving Object in the Footage

Jon Bray, using modified MIT Labs optical flow software to track pixel displacement frame by frame, identifies the necklace as the fastest-moving object in all analyzed footage, traveling more than two feet in a single frame at 60 frames per second. [8] He argues this speed is physically impossible to explain as a secondary effect of a neck gunshot, and requires a direct energy input to the necklace itself.

### The Energy Epicenter Is the Sternum, Not the Neck

Bray's pixel flow analysis maps the epicenter of movement to Kirk's upper sternum area, not to the neck where the wound appeared. [13] He identifies three simultaneous energetic events:

- The shirt pulling upward toward Kirk's face
- A magnetic microphone clasp moving across the chest
- The necklace flying overhead

The primary energy source, per this analysis, originates from the middle of the chest. [20]

### The Wound Characteristics

Investigators examining the wound note that it shows flesh slowly stretching outward with a visible bulge rather than the cavitation pattern expected from a high-velocity rifle round. Ian Carroll describes this as consistent with medium-velocity impact from a blunt object rather than a .30-06 rifle bullet. [12]

### The High-Resolution Footage Timeline

Multiple investigators note that the highest-quality version of the footage was publicly accessible for only three to four days before being restricted online. [16] Analysis conducted during that window forms the basis of most of the technical claims above. Approximately three to four days after the assassination, the highest-resolution footage was restricted. [16]

## The Official Accounts: Contradictions and Inconsistencies

This is where the evidentiary picture becomes particularly complicated. Multiple conflicting official accounts have been given about how the necklace was removed from Kirk's body, and these accounts contradict each other and, investigators argue, contradict the video evidence.

### Account 1: Medics Ripped It Off (September 11, 2025)

**Source and Attribution:**

A New York Times article, citing Erika Kirk, stated that medics ripped the necklace off Kirk's body during life-saving efforts. A Fox News interview with TPUSA communications director Andrew Kolvet also reported the same account. [2]

**Timeline Context:**

This is the earliest version of the official account, emerging within two days of the assassination.

**Investigator Critique:**

Investigators reject this account on two grounds:

1. **Video evidence contradiction:** Video evidence shows the necklace snapping off within the first milliseconds of the incident, before any medic could have touched Kirk. [2]
2. **Scene protocol contradiction:** Accounts from the scene indicate that no medic treated Kirk at the event venue itself; medical treatment occurred at the hospital.

### Account 2: The Necklace Was Found in the SUV (September 9, 2025)

**Source and Attribution:**

Frank Turek, who was in the vehicle with Kirk during the emergency transport, described finding the necklace draped over his bag in the backseat and handing it to a person named Mikey to give to Erika. [17]

**Direct Contradiction:**

This account does not mention any medic removing the necklace, directly contradicting Account 1. If the necklace was found intact in the SUV, it could not simultaneously have been ripped off by medics during emergency treatment.

### Account 3: Kirk Removed It the Night Before (Post-November 2025)

**Source and Attribution:**

In a later Jesse Watters interview, Erika Kirk introduced a new detail: that Charlie had actually removed the necklace himself the night before the assassination and left it in the bathroom, meaning he was not wearing it at all when he was shot. [2]

**Multiple Contradictions:**

This account directly contradicts both earlier accounts:

1. It contradicts the medic narrative, which presupposes he was wearing the necklace
2. It raises the question of how the necklace appears in the video footage of the incident
3. It is inconsistent with Erika Kirk's own earlier statement about the medics removing it

### Evidentiary Status of the Three Accounts

**Documented fact:** At least two of the three accounts cannot be true simultaneously. This constitutes a documented pattern of false or contradictory official statements.

**Investigator interpretation:** Baron Coleman characterizes the shifting narratives as evidence either of deliberate obfuscation or of an evolving cover story. [2] The pattern of initially attributing necklace removal to medics, then revealing it was found in the vehicle, then suggesting it was not being worn at all, is read by investigators as evidence-management rather than honest confusion. [2]

## The Federal Agents and the Necklace

### Search at the Hospital

Candace Owens reported that federal agents searched for the necklace in Kirk's car at the hospital. [3] The significance of this detail is disputed:

- **Official interpretation:** Routine evidence collection as part of scene investigation
- **Investigator interpretation:** The urgency of the search for a necklace (as opposed to searching for weapons, shells, or other ballistic evidence) suggests the necklace itself was forensically significant to the federal investigation

### Why This Matters

If the necklace were ordinary personal jewelry with no evidentiary significance, the targeted search would be unusual. If the necklace were potentially involved in the mechanism of death, a priority search makes sense.

### Erika Kirk's Focus on the Necklace

Multiple investigators note that Erika Kirk reportedly asked about the necklace almost immediately upon arriving at the hospital, despite not asking about Charlie's wedding ring, whose whereabouts remain unknown. [17]

Jon Bray characterizes the necklace as having been consciously crafted into an emotional centerpiece of the public narrative. [5] He notes that the image of a grieving widow holding a bloody necklace is a powerful emotional appeal that may be intentionally obscuring rather than clarifying the forensic questions. [5]

## The Forensic Gap

### Missing Residue Analysis

Jon Bray states plainly that the necklace should have been tested for explosive residue as a matter of basic forensic due diligence, given that it was at the center of the energetic event. [5] No independent forensic analysis of the necklace has been publicly released. No official statement has confirmed or denied whether residue testing was conducted.

### Chain of Custody Questions

Bray further argues that the necklace presents a chain of custody problem:

- The necklace snapped and broke apart during the incident
- Someone had to recover the broken pieces
- Someone had to reunite the pendant and chain
- The necklace was then presented publicly, apparently intact

Bray notes that authorities have not publicly acknowledged that the necklace was broken or that the pendant and chain were separated. [5] He raises the possibility that either:

1. The necklace no longer exists in its original form
2. The necklace is secured by authorities and unavailable for independent analysis
3. A replacement necklace was publicly presented, substituted for the original

No official statement has addressed these questions directly.

## Competing Explanations

### Explanation 1: The Official Narrative (Rifle Shot)

**The Theory:**

A .30-06 rifle bullet struck Kirk in the neck. The force of impact, or the rapid expansion of tissue in the neck, caused the necklace chain to snap. The necklace was subsequently found in the SUV. Medics or others handled it during the emergency response. The various accounts reflect the confusion of a traumatic event rather than deliberate inconsistency.

**Evidentiary Status:**

This is the prosecution's implied position. No official forensic analysis of the necklace has been released to support it. Investigators argue the physics of this explanation are not reproducible: a neck wound would not be expected to snap a chain at mid-chest, and the speed and trajectory of the necklace in the footage are not consistent with this mechanism.

**Supporting Arguments:**

- Simplest explanation (Occam's Razor argument)
- No forensic analysis yet published disputes it

**Weaknesses:**

- Inconsistent with the break point in the middle of the chest
- Inconsistent with the speed of necklace movement documented by pixel analysis
- Does not account for the contradictory official accounts of removal

### Explanation 2: The Exploding Microphone Theory

**The Theorist:**

Jon Bray developed this theory through pixel flow optical analysis starting in December 2025.

**The Theory:**

Kirk was killed by a shaped explosive charge concealed within his wireless microphone, specifically within the battery compartment of the transmitter unit worn against his chest. [13]

**Mechanism Details:**

1. The microphone's battery was partially replaced with explosive material, following a method similar to the Mossad pager attacks of 2024, in which devices remained functional for years while carrying concealed explosives [14]
2. The explosion originated at the sternum, consistent with the pixel flow epicenter
3. The blast forced the cross pendant downward until the necklace reached full extension, then the pendant swung upward like a pendulum, pulling the chain across the peak pressure point and snapping it [15]
4. The broken necklace's momentum carried it over Kirk's head as a single strand rather than a connected loop [15]
5. The neck wound was an unintended consequence: the device was designed to create a chest wound, but the necklace or microphone components deflected energy upward [1]

**Evidentiary Status:**

Supported by pixel flow analysis, the Mossad pager precedent, and the wound characteristics. The theory has been engaged with seriously by multiple independent researchers including Ian Carroll, who described himself as "coming around" on it. [16] It has not been confirmed or refuted by any official forensic analysis.

**Supporting Evidence:**

- Pixel flow analysis identifying the sternum as the energy epicenter
- The Mossad pager precedent establishes technical feasibility
- The microphone model (Rode GO) has a battery compartment compatible with the theory
- Ian Carroll's engagement with the theory based on wound timing analysis
- The speed of necklace movement is more consistent with direct blast than secondary effect

**Challenges to the Theory:**

- No forensic evidence of microphone involvement has been recovered or released
- Questions about how a microphone could be modified while remaining functional
- The absence of microphone documentation in chain of custody

### Explanation 3: The Rigged Necklace Charm Theory

**The Theorist:**

Baron Coleman developed this alternative theory, presented in Episode 91 (April 2026).

**The Theory:**

The assassination mechanism was not the microphone but something concealed within one of the necklace's charms, potentially one of the cross prongs or pendant pieces made of PETN or a similar explosive material designed to resemble metal. [9]

**Mechanism Details:**

Under this theory, the necklace itself was the weapon, triggered remotely or by proximity. This would explain:

1. Why the necklace was the fastest-moving object in the footage (it received energy directly rather than as a secondary effect)
2. Why federal agents searched urgently for the necklace
3. Why Erika Kirk asked about the necklace before anything else at the hospital
4. The contradictory official accounts (the necklace itself, not a rifle or microphone, is the object that must be recovered and contained)

**Historical Precedent:**

Coleman notes that lapel pins and jewelry have historically been used as assassination mechanisms, establishing the concept as not unprecedented. [12]

**Evidentiary Status:**

Speculative but internally consistent with the video evidence. The theory is complicated by the fact that no part of the necklace visible in footage matches the rectangular shape seen in wound analysis, [12] though investigators disagree about what that rectangular object is.

**Supporting Evidence:**

- The necklace was the fastest-moving object (direct energy receipt)
- Federal agents' urgent search for the necklace
- Erika Kirk's prioritization of the necklace at the hospital
- Historical precedent for jewelry-based explosives
- Explains the pattern of contradictory official accounts

**Challenges to the Theory:**

- The charm size appears too small to contain significant explosive material
- No forensic evidence of charm tampering
- The wound characteristics and angle seem inconsistent with a thoracic charm detonation

### Explanation 4: The Necklace as Secondary Evidence of Cover-Up

**The Framing:**

Some investigators treat the necklace primarily as evidence of cover-up rather than as the mechanism of death itself. Under this view, the necklace's behavior is consistent with an explosive device of some kind being involved, but the specific mechanism (microphone vs. charm vs. other) remains uncertain.

**What Is Significant:**

What matters is the pattern of:

1. Conflicting official accounts about how the necklace was removed
2. The apparent urgency of federal agents to locate the necklace
3. The failure to conduct or release independent forensic analysis
4. The restriction of high-resolution footage within days

**Evidentiary Status:**

This framing is supported by the documented inconsistencies in official accounts and the forensic gap. It does not require commitment to a specific death mechanism, only to the observation that the necklace was treated as forensically important despite being presented as ordinary jewelry.

## Disagreements Among Investigators

The main disagreement among independent investigators is not whether the necklace behavior is anomalous (there is broad agreement that it is) but what caused it.

### Bray vs. Coleman: The Primary Debate

**Jon Bray's position:** The directional force pattern, specifically the asymmetric shirt movement and the pendant's pendulum trajectory, is more consistent with the microphone's position and angle than with a charm explosion. [7]

**Baron Coleman's position:** The necklace receiving energy directly, as the fastest-moving object, points to the charm cluster as the source rather than the microphone as a secondary transmitter of force. [9]

Both researchers agree on the basic video evidence (the break point, the speed, the energy epicenter). They disagree on the most likely source of that energy.

### Ian Carroll's Evolution

Ian Carroll has publicly engaged with both theories and described himself as moving toward the exploding microphone explanation based on primer charge and wound timing analysis. [19] In December 2025, Carroll published "Maybe I'm Coming Around On The Exploding Mic Theory," indicating the microphone theory's growing credibility with independent analysts. [16]

### Blake Bednarz's Methodological Contribution

Blake Bednarz has contributed specific video forensic work establishing that shirt movement precedes neck wound appearance, which both theories use but interpret differently. His work is cited by both the microphone and charm camps. [1]

## Investigative Significance

The necklace matters for several distinct reasons, and it is worth separating them:

### 1. Physical Evidence of Death Mechanism

If the necklace's behavior is inconsistent with a rifle shot, that is evidence that the official cause of death is wrong. The pixel flow analysis and break-point analysis are the most technically developed arguments in the entire investigation for an alternative mechanism of death.

### 2. Evidence of Cover-Up

Even setting aside the mechanism question, the three mutually contradictory official accounts of how the necklace was removed constitute a documented pattern of false statements. At least two of the three accounts cannot be true simultaneously.

### 3. Chain of Custody Failure

The necklace has not been subjected to independent forensic analysis, despite being the object most directly involved in the energetic event. This is either a significant investigative failure or a deliberate suppression of evidence.

### 4. Behavioral Anomaly

The reported urgency of federal agents to locate the necklace, and Erika Kirk's prioritization of the necklace over the wedding ring at the hospital, are behavioral data points that investigators argue are inconsistent with the necklace being ordinary personal jewelry.

### 5. Precedent: The Mossad Pager Attacks

The Mossad pager attacks of September 2024 established that explosive devices can be concealed in everyday electronic items, remain functional for years, pass through security screening, and be remotely triggered. This precedent is directly relevant to both the exploding microphone and rigged charm theories.

## What Is Well Supported

The following claims are supported by multiple independent investigators working from the same publicly available footage:

- The necklace snapped at the moment of the incident, not during subsequent medical treatment
- The necklace traveled upward and over Kirk's head with significant force
- The break point appears to be in the mid-chest area rather than at the neck wound site
- The necklace is the fastest-moving object in the analyzed footage
- Pixel flow analysis places the primary energy epicenter at the sternum, not the neck
- At least two of the three official accounts of how the necklace was removed are mutually contradictory
- No independent forensic analysis of the necklace has been publicly released
- The high-resolution footage was restricted online within days of the assassination

## What Remains Uncertain

- Whether the necklace was tested for explosive residue, and if so, what those results showed
- The exact break point of the chain (mid-chest is the investigator consensus, but precise measurement from available footage is limited)
- Whether the third charm on the necklace has been identified
- Whether the necklace presented publicly after the assassination is the same physical object that was on Kirk's body at the time of the incident
- The identity of the rectangular object visible in wound analysis footage, and whether it is related to the necklace
- Whether Erika Kirk's account of Charlie removing the necklace the night before is accurate, and if so, how the necklace appears in the incident footage
- The full chain of custody of the necklace from the SUV backseat to its public presentation
- Whether federal agents' search for the necklace at the hospital was routine evidence collection or something more targeted
- The current location and physical condition of the original necklace
