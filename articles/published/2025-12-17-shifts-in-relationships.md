Shifts in relationship strategies
A reflection of lessons learned since 2016
Paul Ramirez
Dec 17, 2025
I’ve noticed myself acting in ways I never used to. Before COVID, I believed that if I showed up, did my job, and invested time in friendships, I would eventually earn a stable place in my social circles, so long as I kept my opinions to myself far as politics or religion.

However, over time, that list of things to keep to myself kept growing and I sort of started experiencing a form of social paralysis where I just couldn’t talk anymore or say what I was thinking.

I experience something similar on the right as well, though to a lesser extent. In both cases, maintaining relationships now comes with significant overhead. I haven’t encountered many people who are comfortable hearing my personal experience with mRNA injections, including being hospitalized from them. That experience is deeply traumatic to me, and I see people getting sick all the time today because they are still taking the damn things. And it makes honest conversation hard because it’s something I think about a lot.

Anyways, I see Nick Fuentes’ behavior less of a provocation and more as a refusal to play the traditional long-term strategy. That strategy isn’t sustainable unless people can tolerate criticism and discourse. It feels like geniune discourse is a lost art that people are rediscovering, at least at a societal scale. Also, I personally don’t like Nick, I think he is not acting in good faith at all and exploits conservative discourse. I do understand the cultural phenomenon that produces movements like the Groypers, however.
