# Panama's M7.7 Earthquake, the Krakatau Antipode, and the Echo of 1882–1883

## Overview

On October 9, 2026, a magnitude 7.7 earthquake struck Panama. In a video recorded the following day, geophysicist **Afon Burns** argued that the event may be part of a larger pattern connecting Panama with **Krakatau (Anak Krakatau)** in Indonesia, a volcano that lies almost exactly on the opposite side of the planet. His case rests on three threads:

1. **Antipodal geometry**: Panama and Krakatau sit nearly antipodal to each other, and recent large earthquakes in each region have struck near the other's antipode.
2. **Lunar timing**: two of the largest earthquakes in the recent sequence occurred during new-moon windows, exactly one lunar cycle apart.
3. **A historical precedent**: in 1882, Panama experienced its largest known earthquake, and less than a year later, in 1883, Krakatoa produced one of the most violent eruptions in recorded history.

From this, Burns suggests the next one to two years may be critical for watching Krakatau, and that continued movement around the Caribbean plate could raise risk farther north, including in California.

---

## The Panama Earthquake of October 9, 2026

Burns describes the M7.7 as one of the largest earthquakes in Panama's history and the **largest instrumentally observed earthquake** in the country. Stronger shocks are known from the 1800s, but those predate seismometers.

**Aftershocks** were substantial. Sorted by magnitude, the largest included:

| Aftershock | Magnitude |
|---|---|
| Largest | 6.6 |
| Second | 6.0 |
| Third | 5.8 |

Measuring the three-day aftershock cloud, Burns estimates the rupture zone at roughly **116 miles (180–190 km)** across. He interprets the event as a large **strike-slip** rupture in which one part of the Panama microplate sheared relative to another. Noting subduction off Colombia, he reasons that one side moved right and the other left, which would make it a **left-lateral transform fault**, representing "huge movement" along that boundary.

---

## The Antipode: Panama and Krakatau

An **antipode** is the point directly opposite a location on Earth's surface. Burns plotted the antipode of the Panama epicenter and found it lands near the **Java Trench**, roughly **600–700 km (about 430 miles)** from Krakatau.

He then turned the comparison around. Between September 2026 and the Panama quake, only **three earthquakes worldwide exceeded M6.5** (excluding the Panama mainshock and its M6.6 aftershock). One of them was an **M6.5 in Indonesia near Krakatau on September 11, 2026**, about a week after Krakatau's major eruption. When Burns plotted that quake's antipode, it fell **very close to the Panama epicenter**. Conversely, Panama sits very close to the Indonesian quake's antipode. A line drawn between the two traces directly through Krakatau.

Burns emphasizes how unlikely he considers this. Large earthquakes can occur along many plate boundaries worldwide, yet these two landed with only a "very, very minor deviation" from each other's exact antipodes.

---

## Lunar Phasing: Two New Moons, Two Large Quakes

Burns noted that the Indonesian M6.5 occurred **29 days before** the Panama quake, almost exactly one lunar cycle. Both fell within a **new-moon window**, which he describes as a phase of roughly two to three days. For the September 11 event he showed the Sun and Moon positioned close together in the sky at the time of the quake.

His conclusion: the previous new moon produced one of the largest earthquakes of the sequence, and the current new moon produced the Panama M7.7. He treats this as evidence of **lunar phasing and triggering** working alongside the antipodal geometry.

---

## Krakatau's September 2026 Eruption

On **September 4–5, 2026**, Anak Krakatau produced what Burns calls a huge eruption:

- **Lava fountains** reported up to about **5,000 feet**
- An **ash plume** reaching roughly **15–20 km**, into the stratosphere
- Some observers called it the biggest since 1883; Burns considers it at least among the **top three since 1883**

Burns adds a personal observation. Flying from Hawaii to California around **September 10**, he saw what he describes as pitch-black clouds high in the atmosphere at cruising altitude (about 35,000 ft), interspersed with ordinary clouds. He believes these were ash from the Krakatau eruption.

## The Historical Echo: 1882 Panama and 1883 Krakatoa

### The 1883 eruption of Krakatoa

Krakatoa was highly active from **May to October 1883**. The climactic eruption on **August 27, 1883**:

- Rated **VEI 6** on the Volcanic Explosivity Index
- Destroyed **more than 70%** of the island and its surrounding archipelago, which collapsed into a caldera
- Ranks among the deadliest and most destructive volcanic events in recorded history
- Produced a blast that ruptured eardrums hundreds of miles away and was heard about **3,100 km away in Australia**
- Generated an acoustic pressure wave that **circled the globe more than three times**

(Burns sourced these figures from the Wikipedia article on the 1883 eruption.)

### The 1882 Panama earthquake

In **September 1882**, Panama was struck by one of the largest earthquakes in its history, with estimates of **M7.9 to M8.2–8.3** (Burns averages this to about **M8.1**, a "proper mega quake"). Because it predates instruments, its exact size is unknown. It occurred **north** of the 2026 epicenter.

When Burns plotted the antipode of a point in the 1882 rupture area, it fell in essentially the same region as the 2026 antipode, near Krakatau, and if anything slightly closer.

### The pattern Burns sees

| | 1882–1883 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Panama | M~8.1 earthquake (Sept 1882) | M7.7 earthquake (Oct 9, 2026), largest instrumentally recorded |
| Krakatau | VEI 6 eruption (climax Aug 1883), activity starting ~8–9 months after the quake | Major eruption (Sept 4–5, 2026), roughly a month *before* the quake |
| Antipodal alignment | 1882 rupture antipode near Krakatau | 2026 rupture antipode near Krakatau; Krakatau-area M6.5 antipode near Panama |

Taken alone, Burns calls the 1882–1883 sequence "kind of an interesting coincidence." Combined with the 2026 events, he says it becomes "very, very interesting."

## Outlook and Concerns

Burns raises the question directly: was the September 2026 eruption a **"warm-up"** for something larger? He considers it **possible**, reasoning that if these dynamics are real and general, ones we may only now be recognizing, then concern is warranted. In his words, the combination of the M6.5 a week after the eruption, the antipodal sequence, and the lunar phasing is "too much to write off."

### Potential consequences of a large Krakatau eruption

Burns warns that a major eruption could:

- Trigger a **volcanic winter**
- Affect **food supplies and agriculture** globally
- Kill many people and destroy homes and buildings

He suggests the **next one to two years** will be critical for monitoring Krakatau.

### The Caribbean plate and California

Burns places the Panama quake within a broader sequence of movement around the **Caribbean plate**:

- Activity involving **Venezuela, Colombia, and Panama**
- An **M7.6 near the Cayman Islands** (February 2025)
- An **M7.3 in Mexico** (July 2026)

With this movement "accomplished," and possibly more to come, he argues attention should also turn **north to California**, where he believes the risk "just keeps increasing."
