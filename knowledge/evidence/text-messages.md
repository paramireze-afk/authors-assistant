# Text Messages: Robinson-Twiggs Exchange

## Overview

The text message exchange between Tyler Robinson and his roommate and romantic
partner Lance Twiggs is the single most consequential piece of evidence in the
government's case. It functions as a near-complete confession: Robinson admits to
the shooting, describes hiding the rifle, references the bullet engravings, and
ultimately announces his intention to surrender. The prosecution relies on it
heavily precisely because Robinson has not confessed in any independently
verifiable way.

The messages have never been independently forensically verified. They were
accessed by police from Twiggs's device, not Robinson's, and were photographed
off Twiggs's screen rather than extracted through a forensic imaging process. No
timestamps appear in any court filing. The original handwritten note referenced
in the exchange has been destroyed.

## Source and Chain of Custody

**Official claim (PCA [Source 19], Criminal Information [Source 20]):** Police
interviewed Lance Twiggs, who described the messages and allowed investigators
to photograph his screen as he scrolled through them. The PCA describes the
messages as showing "a need to retrieve a rifle from a drop point," references
to bullet engravings, a scope, and a change of outfits.

**What this means in practice:** The evidentiary chain runs:
Robinson's alleged device > Twiggs's device > photograph of Twiggs's screen >
prosecution summary. No independent forensic extraction. No hash verification.
No chain-of-custody documentation in the public record.

**Discord platform statement:** Discord told investigators they found "no
evidence that the suspect planned this incident or promoted violence" on their
service. This does not directly address the Twiggs messages (which appear to
have been sent via a separate messaging channel), but it contradicts the broader
narrative of Robinson as a radicalized planner.

## The Note Under the Keyboard

Robinson allegedly sent Twiggs a text reading: "drop what you are doing, look
under my keyboard."

Twiggs found a handwritten note stating: "I had the opportunity to take out
Charlie Kirk and I'm going to take it."

**Official claim:** Police found a photograph of this note. The note itself has
been destroyed.

**Key anomaly (tense construction):** The note uses past tense ("I had the
opportunity") combined with future intent ("I am going to take it"). At the time
the note would have been written, Robinson had not yet acted. The phrasing
implies the note was written before the shooting as a declaration of intent, yet
Robinson simultaneously claims he "hoped to keep this secret till I died of old
age." These two positions are difficult to reconcile: a person planning lifelong
secrecy does not pre-plant a written confession.

**Evidentiary gap:** No handwriting analysis has been released publicly. No
photograph of the note has been released. The only evidence the note existed is
the prosecution's characterization of Twiggs's account.

## Timestamp Problem

No timestamps appear anywhere in the Criminal Information filing [Source 20] or the
PCA [Source 19]. The documents state only that the messages occurred "on September
10, 2025."

**Why this matters for the timeline:** Two details in the messages can only have
been known after specific public events:

1. "They grabbed some crazy old dude" refers to George Zinn, arrested at
	approximately 12:25 PM and released by approximately 1:53 PM.
2. "Interrogated someone in similar clothing" refers to Zachariah Qureshi, whose
	arrest and release were not publicly confirmed until FBI Director Kash Patel
	tweeted at approximately 6:00 PM.

If Robinson's reference to Qureshi reflects public knowledge, the earliest
plausible send time for that portion of the exchange is after 6:00 PM, roughly
six hours after the shooting. This is consistent with the Fox News original
report [Source 17] placing Robinson's encounter with a police officer near the rifle
drop point at approximately 6:30 PM.

Candace Owens raised the timestamp absence as an immediate red flag upon reading
the indictment, noting that without timestamps it is impossible to place the
messages in the established timeline [Source 4].

## Linguistic Anomalies

Multiple independent analysts and commentators have flagged the register of
Robinson's messages as inconsistent with his documented communication style
elsewhere.

### Law Enforcement Vocabulary

The messages use terminology that analysts describe as consistent with
professional law enforcement rather than a 22-year-old gamer from southern Utah:

- "retrieve" (not "get" or "grab")
- "vehicle" (not "car" or "truck"; Robinson's own bodycam footage from 2022
  shows him using "my car")
- "drop point" (tactical jargon)
- "swept that spot" (law enforcement search terminology)
- "squad car" (not "cop car" or "police car")
- "lingering" (formal, literary register)

Candace Owens flagged "retrieve" and "vehicle" in her September 16 episode
[Source 2]. The Brandi and Billie Show noted the overall formal register as
"borderline poetic" and inconsistent with Gen Z communication patterns [Source
1].

### The OWO/UWU Discrepancy

The PCA [Source 19] records the engraving on the fired casing as: "NoTices Bulge
OWO what's this?"

Robinson's text references the same engraving as: "notices bulge uwu"

"OWO" and "uwu" are distinct internet meme expressions with different meanings
and different visual appearances. A person who painstakingly engraved "OWO" onto
a brass casing would be unlikely to misremember it as "uwu" moments later in a
text message. The discrepancy raises the question of whether the author of the
texts had direct knowledge of the physical evidence.

### Roommate's Emotional Register

Twiggs's side of the exchange has also drawn scrutiny. After an initial burst of
punctuation ("What?????????????? You're joking, right????"), his responses
compress to single-word questions: "Why?" "Yeah." The emotional arc moves from
apparent shock to calm interrogation. Analysts have noted this reads more like a
structured interview than an intimate partner's reaction to learning their
companion has just committed a high-profile assassination [Source 7].

### The MAGA Non-Sequitur

At the point in the exchange where Robinson's father has apparently identified
the murder weapon from FBI photos and is calling Robinson repeatedly, Robinson's
response is a casual political observation: "since trump got into office [my dad]
has been pretty diehard maga." Independent analysts have flagged this as
tonally inconsistent with the crisis described in the surrounding messages, and
structurally consistent with a talking point being inserted into a fabricated
document.

## The Keyboard Note Paradox

Robinson states in the exchange: "I had hoped to keep this secret till I died of
old age."

This statement is structurally irreconcilable with the pre-planted note under
the keyboard. A person planning lifelong secrecy does not write a confession and
leave it where their partner will find it. The two elements of the prosecution's
own evidence contradict each other directly.

One resolution: the note was written as a contingency ("if something happens to
me"). But the text does not frame it that way, and Robinson does not explain the
note's existence to Twiggs in those terms.

Another resolution: the texts are fabricated and the note is fabricated, and the
fabricators did not notice the internal contradiction.

A third resolution: Robinson wrote the note for reasons not captured in the
exchange, and the "hoped to keep this secret" line is genuine but refers to
something other than the note's existence.

The original note cannot be examined. It has been destroyed.

## The Wordle Anomaly

Robinson allegedly played Wordle on the New York Times website 13 minutes before
arriving on campus, and again the following morning while a fugitive. This
behavioral detail, sourced from the prosecution, is cited by independent
investigators as incompatible with the psychological state of someone executing
a premeditated assassination and then evading a statewide manhunt.

## Twiggs's Cooperation: Disputed

The government stated publicly that Twiggs cooperated with investigators. This
claim has been directly contradicted by Twiggs's own family.

A close family member told Baron Coleman [Source 9] and the Brandi and Billie
Show [Source 10] that:

- The FBI surrounded the condo and detained Twiggs for hours.
- Twiggs was not cooperating as reported.
- Twiggs currently refuses to speak to any family member about the incident,
  including his parents, despite Robinson facing the death penalty.

The family member also stated that the woman who appeared in media interviews
claiming to be a Robinson family member was in fact a distant relative who had
met Twiggs approximately three times and had no meaningful knowledge of either
individual [Source 11, Source 12].

Twiggs's family expressed the belief that both Tyler and Lance were involved in
some capacity but "didn't know what they were getting into," suggesting they may
have been directed to perform specific actions [Source 9].

## The Fox News Silent Edit

The original Fox News article published September 25 [Source 17] stated Robinson
"made contact" with a police officer near the rifle drop point "around 6:30 p.m."
on September 10.

Within approximately 48 hours, this was silently changed to "much later in the
evening" [Source 18]. No correction notice was issued.

The significance: 6:30 PM is consistent with the text message timeline (Qureshi
released at approximately 6:00 PM, Robinson references both arrests as past
events). "Much later in the evening" is not consistent with Robinson also having
eaten at Dairy Queen in Orem and then driven three hours to Panguitch to eat a
steak at Cowboy's Smokehouse before the restaurant's 9:00 PM closing time. The
geography and timing cannot simultaneously accommodate a late-evening rifle
retrieval attempt, a Dairy Queen visit, and a Panguitch dinner.

## The Discord Confession

Separate from the Twiggs exchange, Robinson posted to his Discord friend group
on September 11 at 8:57 PM [Source 21]:

> "Hey guys, I have bad news for you all. it was me at UVU yesterday. im sorry
> for all of this. im surrendering through a sheriff friend in a few moments.
> thanks for all the good times and laughs, you've all been so amazing. thank
> you all for everything"

This message is notable for several reasons:

- The register is markedly different from the Twiggs exchange: informal,
  emotional, consistent with Robinson's documented Discord voice elsewhere in
  the same archive.
- Robinson states he is "surrendering through a sheriff friend," consistent with
  the Criminal Information's reference to "one of my neighbors here is a deputy
  for the sheriff."
- Friends in the server initially assumed it was a joke. The New York Times
  reported Robinson joking in the same server that the shooter was his
  "doppelganger trying to get him in trouble," a report that directly
  contradicts the Washington Post's claim that his only Discord message was a
  confession.

For Robinson's full Discord profile and communication patterns, see
`knowledge/persons/tyler-robinson.md`.

## Google Trends: Pre-Assassination Searches for Twiggs

Google Trends data shows searches for "Lance Twiggs" originating from:

- Washington D.C.: October 2020, April 2021, and approximately one and a half
  months before the assassination [Source 23]
- Israel: June 2025, approximately three months before the assassination, and a
  separate hit from 2021 when Twiggs would have been approximately 17 to 18
  years old [Source 22]

The name "Lance Twiggs" is sufficiently unusual that independent investigators
consider coincidental searches unlikely. Baron Coleman noted that most people
who first encountered the name assumed it was a pseudonym or a furry cosplay
name [Source 16].

## Open Questions

1. Why were the messages accessed from Twiggs's device rather than Robinson's?
2. Why do no timestamps appear in any court filing for messages sent on a
	platform that auto-timestamps every message?
3. Who destroyed the keyboard note, when, and on whose authority?
4. How did Robinson know the second arrestee was wearing "similar clothing" when
	no public source confirmed this detail at the time?
5. What explains the OWO/UWU discrepancy between the physical evidence and the
	text messages?
6. Is Twiggs cooperating with the government, as officially claimed, or not, as
	his family states?
7. What is the significance of searches for "Lance Twiggs" originating from
	Washington D.C. and Israel years before the assassination?
